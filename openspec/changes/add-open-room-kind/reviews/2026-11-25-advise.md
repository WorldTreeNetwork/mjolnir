## Findings and required amendments

1. **High — decide what one room means.** ADR 0019:7–14 and design.md:25–45 change the owner's decision twice. They choose a different address for open rooms and allow two kinds under the same owner/name. Segment isolation is a sound reason to consider this option. The response exposes the split to the mint caller, but neither participant necessarily sees the other's response. An empty roster looks like an empty call. A room bearer cannot mint or alter kind. An owner app forwarding untrusted participant input can exercise its own mint authority on the participant's behalf. Require an owner-controlled choice, bind invitations and renewals to the expected identity and refuse mismatches before media starts. Show the effective kind and a specific join error in the client handoff. Ask for an explicit decision on mixed mints.
2. **High — the gateway is the enforcement point.** Design.md:57–60 overstates the second wall. With the open leg's token the relay confines that leg to open. With a protected leg's valid publish token the relay admits an in-grant MoQ publish into protected, even from the gateway. No wide credential is needed. An authenticating reader discards plaintext, while the host or a plain subscriber can read it. S7 actually observed both outcomes (add-webrtc-edge/spikes/finding.md:166–171). Document this residual exposure and assign the actual WHIP/WHEP gate to .13. Check the verified original token root, URL room and operation grant before opening any relay session or answering SDP. Use only that leg's token. Distinguish receipt of a bearer for verification from possession of a standing relay credential. If independent relay refusal is required, scope a distinguishable authenticated gateway connection and a relay admission policy.
3. **High — protect joins as well as keys.** Design.md:80 and ADR 0017:12. Equal secret, kid, owner, name and label give equal credential context and opaque name across kinds. A relay can re-present authentic ciphertext and manifest to a matching credential holder in the other room, subject to instance and local replay checks. The relay gains no decryption key. A different exposure arises if an invitation expecting protection follows an open token and sends clear media. A missing checkbox cannot enforce either property. Assign invite/token identity checks and refuse protected credential import/use or plaintext fallback on an open join across supported clients, including renewals. If encrypted open rooms are supported, assign a versioned full-room identity in the KDF and invite to call-e2ee/ADR 0017, with incompatible-version refusal and cross-kind test vectors. Do this before enabling the affected client path.
4. **Medium — qualify isolation and make the parser unambiguous.** Delta specs/relay-room-auth/spec.md:118 both rejects non-room roots and calls every unmarked root protected. Specify exactly three results. Split the original verified string on literal slash with exact arity, canonical 32-byte base58 owner and exact room alphabet. No URL decoding, trimming, path normalization or pattern interpretation in classification. Use one Rust implementation in the gateway and one conformance corpus for other languages. Invalid includes owner-only, empty/extra/dot segments, escapes, percent encodings and marker lookalikes. All non-open results deny WebRTC. The relay itself continues using its broader admission grammar.

   For ordinary room-mint tokens, reads, announcements and writes are rooted by whole segments (relay lib.rs:215–244, 404–437). The mint permits only 1–64 ASCII letters, digits, underscore and hyphen (mint.ex:6,56). Names like `open` and `_open` are valid and distinct. Neither a literal tilde nor a percent sign passes. Decoding an encoded tilde before mint validation still leaves a forbidden tilde. moq-pattern at 4a6f522 splits on unescaped separators, treats tilde literally, matches a segment wildcard or partial wildcard within one segment, and lets globstar span zero or more segments under the root (pattern.rs:82,465). Relative subscribe `**` cannot cross a literal sibling. WebTransport request-path handling strips query and boundary slashes and checks equality or a slash-delimited prefix. It does not percent-decode in that check. An upstream URL spelling may be normalized or refused but cannot rewrite the signed root. Test actual wire spellings on both routes.

   Tenant-wide grants are real supported shapes: root_allowed accepts the owner itself (lib.rs:50), and webtransport.rs:57–72 and iroh_expiry.rs:45 exercise owner-rooted credentials. Effective reach depends on the patterns. A tenant-rooted subscribe globstar spans both kinds, and a matching publish grant permits writes to both. Operator socket minting is refused and legacy hex owner conversion still produces a room root. A holder of a trusted signing key can issue broader rights. Source review establishes neither issuance nor absence of outstanding wide credentials on production. Limit the isolation claim to room tokens and reject wide credentials at the gateway even on an open request URL.
5. **Medium — rollback and downstream identities.** Design.md:52,89–95. The old mint ignores the new field and omits kind in its response (mint.ex:13–39,62–68). After rollback an open renewal can receive a valid protected token. Existing open grants survive to individual exp, bounded by the configured ceiling, which can exceed 3600. Test old-controller/new-client joins and renewals: an explicit open request must fail visibly on a missing/mismatched kind or root, without reconnecting or publishing. Stop open issuance and communicate the drain before rollback. For split addresses, key roster membership and visibility by full identity before exposing the roster, or a sibling room can see presence and labels. Metering otherwise blends usage, quotas and charges. TURN's proposed expiry/class/owner/room/participant username can collide across kinds at equal expiry, merging attribution and any username-based limits. TURN allocation authority by itself grants no MoQ room rights. Use an unambiguous versioned encoding compatible with the TURN segment rules (add-turn-service/specs/turn-service/spec.md:42), or explicitly accept aggregate accounting until changed.
6. **Medium — make the evidence executable and fold only shipped behavior.** Tasks.md:16–22,35–41 have boxes and expected outcomes, but H1 names a WHIP/WHEP page using RTCPeerConnection (spikes/pages/rtc.js:65). Supply a real Chromium WebTransport/MoQ harness with a trusted local certificate and observed data transfer. Before act, sign equivalent short-lived test tokens from a fixture generator, then repeat with real mint output after implementation. The spike's current exact-three-segment guard (edge.rs:63) must be replaced for the positive open control. Exercise native WT and Iroh on both same-name roots with distinguishable broadcasts, own positive controls, opposite reads, writes and observed announcement sets. Include literal and encoded marker spellings at HTTP and WT, including double encoding, with bounded negative observations. Unit grant inspection is supplementary.

   H2 production binary provenance is already attested in the bead against ca61ceb. Record it once and keep the behavioral test on the matching source with valid and invalid controls. Both old and open fixtures need a deterministic clock through *signature verification and time checks*: key.rs:615–617 checks wall time before relay profile runs. Supplying now only to profile is insufficient. Move delta lines 122,143–152 concerning the future production edge to .13; retain the strict parser and its unit scenarios here. A patched spike run is supporting evidence only. Run H1/H2 before act; repeat advise on the amended contract.
7. **Low — preserve the mint authority boundary.** Delta lines 5–17 fit router.ex:239–265: required scope then user, own verified sub, body_params only. Preserve segment/ttl-type/kind/env/ttl-range/owner/key ordering. Test conflicting body versus header/query as well as omission, null and invalid values. Return no-store and canonical root/kind. Logging the validated two-value enum discloses the owner's declared media policy alongside existing room metadata. It adds neither a bearer nor a content credential. Keep raw request values out of logs.

## Owner decisions in answer order

1. **Claim or root. Recommend the dedicated signed claim with a reader-first rollout for the recorded requirement.** Preserve the existing address and one kind per owner/name. Extend the pinned verifier narrowly for an optional exact enum, preserve refusal of other unknown fields, default missing to protected, upgrade every verifier before issuing open, and maintain a durable atomic room-kind assignment shared by issuers. Unknown-claim refusal is explicit in moq-auth wire.rs:21,47 and tested in claims.rs:594. A bare claim cannot enforce consistency. Define an immutable room lifetime and conflict response, handle legacy protected tokens through expiry, and fence legacy issuers including rollback before enabling open at an existing address. Cost includes verifier maintenance, relay rollout/drain, durable state and migration. The signed-root alternative avoids that rollout and store, keeps legacy protected grants intact and physically separates mixed mints. Its cost is a lasting second identity for the same name and updates to clients, E2EE, roster and accounting. It needs affirmative approval of the reversal.
2. **Mixed mints. Recommend one owner/name, one kind** with atomic conflict refusal, including concurrent requests, restarts and renewal. This costs persistent assignment and a reuse policy. If two rooms are intended, accept the split explicitly and require the invitation/join mismatch signal described above. That costs client and operator work.
3. **Marker, if roots win. Recommend literal ~open** subject to corrected H1. Another reserved marker costs a fresh transport run and review. A marker from the room alphabet collides with today's protected namespace.
4. **Mint authority. Recommend retaining relay:join** for an owner choosing their own room policy. A distinct open-room scope buys independent delegation and cost control but requires scope provisioning and client changes.
5. **Open-room encryption. Recommend protected credentials work only in protected joins.** Enforce this in all supported clients; open WebRTC stays interoperable. Supporting encrypted open MoQ costs a versioned invite/KDF migration and still cannot make that media playable by an uncredentialed WebRTC bridge.
6. **Enforcement. Recommend gateway admission for .13**, with explicit acceptance of a buggy gateway's residual access under a protected bearer. Independent relay enforcement costs authenticated bridge provenance, a relay policy change and rollout.
7. **Deployment. Recommend relay/readers first for the claim choice**, with open issuance last and rollback fenced. For the root choice, controller-only deployment is reasonable after H1/H2 and explicit-open client checks. Rollback preserves outstanding bearer access and can interrupt renewals; relay redeploy alone does not repair incompatible clients.

The five ASK boxes at tasks.md:8–12 are unanswered. Add the identity and enforcement decisions, record the owner's answers and revise the ADR before activation. This review used local source and the orchestrator's provenance attestation. It did not run transport hypotheses or access production.
