## Independent constraints

Why, living relay-room-auth/edge-relay, actual mint and relay admission were read before design/tasks and prior verdicts. The principal constraints were recorded in commentary before that comparison; expanded here for the durable record:

1. Derive kind only from authenticated token authority, never the URL's appearance.
2. Preserve ordinary legacy protected tokens and their exact namespace.
3. A reserved segment can separate room roots without changing JWT verification.
4. Keep classification narrower than the existing relay admission grammar.
5. Wide grants are an isolation exception; ordinary room-mint grants are not wide.
6. Bind invites, renewal, credentials and downstream identities to the complete root.
7. An explicit open request must fail closed against the old mint.
8. Perform full admission before edge negotiation or connection side effects.
9. The relay cannot recognize an edge presenting an ordinary protected bearer.
10. The tradeoff is less verifier/state machinery for a lasting second same-name identity.

## Prioritized findings

### 1. High — finish the full-admission and expected-identity handoff

Design D4:37–43 says “existing relay verifier,” then checks class, room name and operation. The actual boundary is more than `KeySet::verify`: `native/mjolnir_relay/src/lib.rs:215–238` also runs `profile` (required iat/exp, TTL, future-iat allowance, root), path containment and `grant_from` validation. The independent offline run itself shows a future-iat token that verifies but fails profile. Prior review 2 finding 6 asked for this distinction; switching back to roots does not remove it. Name the full admission operation/sequence for .13, from one validated trust snapshot, returning the original authenticated root and validated grant. Require it before relay connection or SDP, and require leg teardown at grant expiry. Keep the pure classifier separate; no new cryptographic verifier is requested.

D5:51 requires protected *class*, but does not explicitly compare a protected invitation's expected owner/room with the authenticated token root or bind response metadata to the actual token used. A valid protected token for another room passes that stated condition. For both kinds, require exact expected full-root equality on initial join, credential replacement and renewal; compare any response root/kind with that authenticated identity. Obtain expected owner/room from the invitation/join context, not from the token being checked. Refuse wrong owner, wrong room, sibling kind and mismatched response/token before credential use or media; show effective kind and a specific failure. For a route containing an owner, compare it too; if .13 has only a room name in its URL, derive owner from the admitted root and carry that full identity everywhere. These are downstream contract obligations, not a demand to build .13/.15 during this review.

## What the revision resolves

- The user-authorized path trial explicitly permits two identities for one owner/name. It no longer quietly promises one room or a conflicting-mint 409.
- D1 specifies exact arity, canonical 32-byte base58 owner, literal marker and bounded ASCII room. No decoding/normalization; owner-only, escapes and extra segments are invalid. Names `open` and `_open` remain protected. Keep one conformance corpus and use whole-string matching in implementation.
- Keeping the pinned verifier and claim-free tokens removes the claim decoder/projection differential and verifier-maintenance work. The relay's key loader and moq-auth still enforce key/algorithm/scope policy; this review does not require the abandoned custom-verifier parity suite.
- Removing the mutable kind record and using disjoint roots removes the old unrecorded-token collision, durable writer, loss/restore, retention and legacy-drain requirements. Ordinary protected root/pattern/claim shapes are unchanged. Existing time, expiry and key-rotation limits remain.
- Body-only kind, enum validation/order, no-store, validated logging and owner `sub` authority are clear. An owner application forwarding an untrusted kind is still exercising its own authority.
- Wide credentials can span both roots subject to their patterns, and .13 must refuse them even on open-looking URLs. Source/tests do not establish whether production has outstanding wide grants.
- The buggy-edge residual is now accurate: a protected publish bearer authorizes protected plaintext publication even from an edge. An authenticating reader may reject it while the host/plain readers can see it. This review accepts that description as the trial boundary, not as a new independent owner approval of deployment.
- Rollback to the old mint stops new open issuance but preserves already-issued open tokens until individual expiry. D6 correctly requires explicit-open client checks with or before issuance. Keep that prerequisite in the concrete release gate, including automated rollback/in-flight mints; do not describe rollback as immediate revocation or a relay restart. No verifier rollout/drain is needed merely for the marker.
