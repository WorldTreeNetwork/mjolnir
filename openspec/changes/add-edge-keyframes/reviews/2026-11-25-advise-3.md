## Findings

**High — local playback changes who can authorize and terminate a subscriber.** Design:91–98, spec:103 and tasks:82. The existing spike establishes a relay connection with each leg's token before accepting the offer, and terminates the WebRTC task on relay connection closure (add-webrtc-edge/spikes/rust/src/bin/edge.rs:115,157–162). A publisher's still-valid relay connection establishes its right to publish. It cannot establish a particular viewer's current right to subscribe. Local playback needs no new signing key, but the local origin handle lets the gateway deliver media after its own decision alone.

- **Admission and scope.** Checking the full verified room identity, kind and requested operation is necessary. The relay currently scopes all reads/announcements for the session's lifetime (edge-relay/spec.md:225). The proposed sibling-target check guards PLI forwarding, not media reads. A local consumer must constrain every selected or referenced media/catalog path to the viewer's grant, including rendition switches. The per-WHIP origin reduces the reachable set but is not a subscriber grant. Room kind is a planned gateway rule, not a relay-enforced WebRTC-origin rule. Both local and fallback paths would need .11/.13's complete verified-open admission before SDP or media, including on renewed sessions.
- **Expiry and revocation.** A mandatory, tested local timer can enforce exp for an honest gateway. The spike timer is optional and currently has the independent relay closure as a backstop (edge.rs:150–162,257). A bypass removes the viewer's backstop. At setup/fallback completion, recheck that the viewer still has time left before answering; expiry during a wait must not start a new play. The relay loads a replacement key set or fails closed for **new** admissions within five seconds. Routine rotation leaves existing admissions intact until expiry. Emergency revocation additionally restarts/drains the relay and ends existing sessions host-wide (edge-relay/spec.md:89,258; relay-room-auth/spec.md:175). The spike reads its key file once at startup (edge.rs:245). It does not demonstrate equivalent key freshness, invalid-set refusal, drain or emergency termination for local WHEP. Publisher teardown may end the feed, but cannot replace enforcement of the individual viewer's authority. Specify closure of active local plays, lingering/buffered delivery and requester retirement if this route is chosen.
- **Limits and metering.** A local viewer consumes a WebRTC connection and gateway egress; no corresponding WHEP subscriber connection or delivered bytes appear at the relay. Any enforced relay admission cap or drain is bypassed for that leg. Per-owner connection/byte limits and accounting are owed by .4; do not describe those as shipped. Counting local plays later is insufficient for a hard limit shared with relay legs. Define common admission/reservation and release, byte attribution and failure behavior across paths before enabling a bypass under those limits. Count actual gateway viewer egress separately from the publisher's relay traffic and avoid double counting on fallback.

**Medium — the revised measurement changes two variables.** Tasks:30–31,60–71; design:240–254. Before uses relayed WHEP; after can use an in-process source. An improvement in setup, first frame or recovery can come from the shorter media path and different cached groups, even if a viewer-cause PLI is observed. D3e proves forwarding on a local viewer leg while the *publisher* also has a lite-06 connection. It does not demonstrate feedback crossing a relay leg. Keep the same subscriber media route for the principal comparison. If the owner chooses local playback, add same-local-route feedback-off/on controls with identical stimulus, alongside the relayed control. Record actual route, source generation and fallback duration; pinning can spend a catalog timeout before a second relay attempt. At design:242 verify the measurement probe produces no join-cause PLI, including on rendition changes; subscription at announcement alone does not establish when its video cursor becomes active.

## Decision and exact amendments

1. **Recommended owner decision: defer WHEP PLI/FIR forwarding and preserve relay admission on every viewer leg.** This narrows .12's original viewer-feedback objective. Record Duke's decision explicitly. Keep the periodic 2 s request, ratio-4 join, uplink discontinuity request and single 1 s floor. On a lite-06 hop a later downstream join cannot itself send refresh upstream. A cached decodable group may still start a viewer immediately. When a new keyframe is needed, a responsive encoder and negligible loss give a phase wait of roughly 0–2 s to the next periodic request (about 1 s on average for uniform phase), then transport, encode and decode time. Direct feedback can request immediately when the gate is free, or wait up to the remaining 1 s cooldown. That floor limits request spacing; it does not guarantee a 1 s response. Viewer-side loss alone no longer prompts WHIP, so D1 can exceed the 1.2 s target. NACK may recover sooner; an ignored/lost PLI can take longer. Publisher-side loss still triggers ingest recovery. No measured latency delta is available yet.
2. On that choice, remove the carried source/requester API, pairing table and bypass SHALL from the proposal, design, spec, tasks and .13 handoff. Keep the pinned egress behavior. For D1, record viewer feedback dropped and measure recovery against the ordinary cadence; retain the existing threshold as an observation if desired, without a viewer-cause pass requirement. For E, combine eligible ingest causes to test the shared floor, and use viewer storms as a negative control for forwarding. For F, generate saturated requests through a controlled eligible ingest cause or gate harness and identify it as such in the PR evidence. Keep comparisons on one media path. Update U1 and the drop condition to cover the ingest patch; track viewer-driven feedback separately. Resume it when an authorized relayed playback can bind the actual served instance or transport refresh end to end. Moving to an upstream API alone does not carry refresh across lite-06.
3. **If Duke wants immediate viewer-driven recovery, explicitly approve a separate local-delivery design.** Its cost is another subscriber enforcement point and operational integration with .4/.11/.13. Require equivalent key-set reload/invalid-state handling, emergency drain and active-play closure, open-kind and effective grant checks on every media path, expiry during setup/play/fallback, and shared limits/accounting. Exercise negative cases where the relay would refuse a new subscriber, plus already-active viewers during routine rotation and emergency revoke. Keep the generation-pinning tests and isolate the latency variables above. A checked ASK about trying a cadence or preparing an upstream PR does not grant this architecture change.

After the owner choice and amendment, **K1a is the first build after K0** and K1b is its companion feasibility gate. No code, live service or benchmark was run in this contract review.
