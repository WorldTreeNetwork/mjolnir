## Findings and required changes

1. **High — make the sender enforce the stated bound.** The delta at specs/webrtc-edge/spec.md:66 and P1 at tasks.md:24 exempt first-keyframe and deferred-step requests. Those are real writer calls (pinned session.rs:298–313), so two independent cooldowns do not enforce the advertised minimum spacing. With edge policy enabled, pass every actual PLI through one monotonic last-send time per negotiated WHIP video line, including startup and clock steps. Preserve pinned behavior with the policy disabled. Keep one pending bit and deadline per line. A keyframe cancels pending work; if a PLI gets no answer, define the next retry deadline without spinning on a past period deadline. Only target a current receiving stream with negotiated PLI. str0m writer.rs:302–321 can return NoReceiverSource before a MID/RID has a receiver. A timer or stale handle must not close a healthy session. Test actual writer requests across overlapping old/new causes, ignored PLIs, no RTP yet, and no negotiated PLI.

   One authorized WHEP viewer can sustain one request per second for the played line. Many viewers share that budget; independent lines and publishers have independent budgets. Encoders that comply can spend materially more bytes on keyframes, reduce delta quality or exceed a cap. One request per second is a bound on feedback, not a bound on encoder output or a guarantee of admission under rate.rs:18–50. Keep the meter intact. Coalesce before enqueue so the sender's queue also stays bounded. Confirm the residual sustained influence with the owner after measurement.

2. **High — bind feedback to the live, authorized source.** Design.md:79,89–91 specifies a session-wide request with no track argument, triggered on any egress MID/RID. The spike verifies the token and matches the operation's path grant (add-webrtc-edge/spikes/rust/src/bin/edge.rs:58,98). Keep both checks ahead of pairing. Key by the full verified root and the authorized canonical relative broadcast. Associate the egress line's selected video track with the corresponding ingest line, or explicitly restrict the supported pairing to one unambiguous video line and ignore ambiguous feedback. Session-local MIDs are not portable between peers. Pin feedback to the lifetime of the authorized play and the particular WHIP publisher instance. Use a fresh receiver per accepted WHIP session and remove registry entries only if they still belong to the finishing generation. A weak sender to that receiver is sufficient for teardown; a weak pointer to a reusable table slot is insufficient. Expiry, DELETE, error and replacement must retire feedback even during content linger. P6/D3 should prove an old viewer cannot signal a replacement at the same address, and late cleanup cannot delete the replacement. Use active same-path broadcasts under different roots with positive controls and causal dispatch counters. A viewer failing to establish the second session proves little. Both whip.rs:20 and whep.rs:21 re-export the same Response type; specify the no-requester result for an egress response.

3. **Medium — define the join trigger and its reach.** Delta line 23 asks on zero-to-one demand while line 34 forbids a request for an under-ratio join, including the first. Recommend asking on an observed demand for a group beyond the latest written group and letting the startup gate handle an empty track. If the first actual upstream subscription intentionally bypasses ratio, state and test that exception. Test first/repeated joins above and below ratio, including demand observer initialization. H1's P4 covers in-process demand; add a distinct trace for the first relay subscription and a subsequent relay-side join with the upstream subscription still active. At the pin, lite/version.rs:382–390 disables refresh through lite-06 and lite/track.rs:183,225 omits it on this wire; the spike negotiated lite-06 (finding.md:5). An ordinary downstream join cannot forward a refresh across an already-subscribed hop. First/last-reader churn can change actual upstream subscription demand, depending on the chosen rule. Neither case escapes the source's gate. Periodic requests benefit remote readers even without remote refresh. They cannot guarantee a two-second GOP when a sender ignores feedback.

## Owner decisions in answer order

1. **Upstream submission.** Duke opens the prepared PR from the Foundation fork. Cost is his submission and review time; the builder supplies the reproducible local port, CI result and draft.
2. **Outbound licence.** Approve MIT OR Apache-2.0 for the Foundation-authorized patch. This permits upstream and other permissive reuse. Without approval, defer publication and resolve licensing before distributing the carry on that basis.
3. **Carry mechanism.** Vendor just moq-webrtc with the consumer-local patch and keep the existing pin. Cost is ownership of a materialized manifest, locks, provenance checks and maintenance until the drop condition. The scratch resolution supports this choice.
4. **Edge defaults and residual cost.** Trial 2 s, ratio 4, discontinuity requests on, and a single 1 s floor per eligible source line. Adopt after F and the latency/storm runs. A viewer can sustain that floor; under cap, quality may fall, and across lines the cost adds. A longer floor reduces encoder pressure at the price of slower recovery.
5. **Deltas after loss.** Continue forwarding usable arriving deltas while awaiting the requested keyframe. This retains current behavior and may briefly display corruption. Holding deltas trades that risk for a freeze.
