## Blind take

Written from proposal Why, the living web-pty-edge spec, and the cited application, strategy, token, and escrow code before opening design, tasks, or the delta.

1. Pin successful application startup independently of issuer reachability, including an empty or unusable cache.
2. Pin offline verification to previously trusted issuer keys; never derive trust from a token's own key hints.
3. Preserve signature, issuer, expiry, and existing consumer authorization checks for every cached-key verification.
4. Bind persisted keys to the configured issuer and validate the complete document before admitting it to trust.
5. Refuse unknown kids and malformed, ambiguous, or unsupported keys without a permissive fallback.
6. Pin replacement and retirement semantics so refresh neither accidentally discards overlap nor retains withdrawn keys forever.
7. Protect the cache location, ownership, permissions, and atomic writes outside the VM snapshot tree.
8. Specify memory-versus-disk behavior on refresh failure, persistence failure, restart, and operator withdrawal.
9. Require bounded background fetches and tests proving outage boot, recovery, rotation, and rejection through the real token hook.
10. Accept an explicit availability-versus-revocation tradeoff for stale keys during an outage, with a workable local withdrawal procedure.

## Comparison and steelman

Decisions 1, 3, and 4 answer the boot, binding, cache placement, validation, and atomic-publication concerns. Decision 5 identifies a viable integration seam: the installed `JokenJwks.before_verify/2` calls `strategy.match_signer_for_kid/2` (`deps/joken_jwks/lib/joken_jwks.ex:51`), so an owned signer table can serve the existing Token hook. Decision 6 covers bootstrap with the same validation and persistence contract. The delta preserves signature, algorithm, issuer, and expiry checks. These are sufficient directions for implementation; this review does not require a different cache architecture.

**R6 is substantively resolved.** Proposal What and Decisions 2, 5, and 7 consistently choose replacement. The issuer supplies overlap by publishing `{A,B}`; a later `{B}` removes A. The delta's overlap sequence explicitly covers successful persistence and restart. No numeric TTL or token-expiry-derived key retention is required.

**R7 is resolved.** Decision 7 supplies stop → delete → start with the issuer unreachable, explains why a pre-stop fetch cannot survive, rejects live-file deletion as an invalidation promise, and makes an operator seed effective on restart. It also makes the empty-JWKS exception explicit: removing the final issuer key does not withdraw local trust. The delta now exercises the chosen withdrawal and seeding procedures.

**R8's policy is resolved; its old scenario wording is not fully reconciled.** Decision 7 and the main SHALL clause use the active set. New failure scenarios explicitly cover additions and retirements before and after restart. Logging degraded durability and recovering by stop/write-or-delete/start are specified. The remaining issue is the two older scenarios identified below, not a missing decision about persistence failure.

The strongest case against a stricter version of my blind take is that issuer-managed overlap plus the last validated snapshot gives useful offline service without inventing key lifetimes. Serving a validated set while persistence fails also immediately applies a retirement in the running process; refusing that publication would retain the withdrawn key there. Restoring older trust after restart is an explicit, consequential availability tradeoff with a stated local recovery procedure. I do not require a new durable revocation ledger, a TTL, or live invalidation as the price of acceptance.

## Required amendment

### R9 — P1: Align the two legacy scenarios with active trust and successful persistence

**Evidence:** `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:28–40` versus its active-set requirement at lines 10–16 and failed-persist scenarios at lines 132–147; `design.md:99–108`.

The remaining contradictions can be demonstrated using only the states now expressly allowed by the design:

| Allowed state | Older scenario's outcome | Revised contract's outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disk `{A}`, active `{A,Z}` after validated fetch and failed persist; otherwise-valid token signed by Z | “Unknown kid is rejected” requires failure because the persisted JWKS lacks Z | Active-set requirement and “Failed persist of an added key” require success |
| Disk `{A,B}`, validated fetch `{B}`, persist fails, restart offline; otherwise-valid token signed by A | “Issuer-set retirement across restart” requires A to be rejected after a validated fetch and restart | Decision 7 and “Failed persist of a retirement” require A to verify again |

**Owed:** change the unknown-kid scenario's GIVEN to absence from the **active** issuer-bound set, or explicitly establish a disk-only boot with no intervening successful fetch. Qualify the retirement-across-restart scenario with **successful persistence of `{B}` before restart**, and issuer unavailability during the restart check. Keep the new failed-persist scenarios as the complementary cases. These are precondition corrections to the author's chosen policy, not a request for another design decision.

The charitable reading is that both older scenarios intend ordinary successful disk-backed operation. That intent is plausible, but their written GIVEN/WHEN clauses also admit the explicit failure states above. Since these scenarios are the executable acceptance contract for sensitive authentication, the missing qualifications should be written rather than supplied differently by implementers and test authors. No additional architecture expansion is owed by this finding.

## Non-blocking notes and verification boundary

- Implement the specified background isolation through the real Token hook, including a blocked refresh, empty offline start followed by recovery, and failed-persist restart cases. Local dependency inspection confirms the hook seam; no implementation or runtime correctness is certified by this review.
- The Why overstates ordinary returned fetch errors: the installed template invokes synchronous fetch but does not propagate its returned error from initialization (`deps/joken_jwks/lib/joken_jwks/default_strategy_template.ex:195–205`). Startup still waits for that fetch, and raised failures can still matter. This does not undermine disk-first boot.
