## Independent take — recorded before opening design, tasks, or delta

1. Pin host-owned AIE execution behind an overlay RPC; refuse guest device passthrough or a new Cloud Hypervisor PCI contract.
2. Pin an optional vm.json locator with explicit absent/unavailable behavior; discovery must never imply permission to execute.
3. Pin the distinction between VM execution principal, IdentiKey keyspace owner, and proven token holder; require an authenticated binding between them.
4. Reuse the durable host Biscuit authority and algorithm-committing holder fingerprint; refuse raw-key keyspace fingerprints as holder identifiers.
5. Require nonce-based possession proof bound to the token, audience, operation, resource, and submitted work before injecting holder facts outside the evaluator.
6. Keep single-key grants usable without guild lookup; accept guild constraints only through trusted verifier-injected membership, never caller assertions.
7. Treat offers as bounded claims on capacity with explicit expiry, revocation, ownership, and execution limits; refuse speculative assets or bearer discovery records.
8. Require a typed, signed offer-record contract and enforcement owner; SecretStore's existing envelope dispatcher does not accept arbitrary signed JSON.
9. Pin occupancy, concurrent admission, cancellation, and fail-closed crash behavior before claiming safe sharing; the vector-add spike proves none of these.
10. Tradeoff: host-side mediation preserves the existing driver and guest simplicity but concentrates trust and scheduling on the host; bound supported kernels rather than imply arbitrary guest-code safety.

## Comparison and steelman

The revision substantially answers the previous review. Decisions 1–3 preserve host KMD ownership, overlay INPUT, optional locator discovery, and the explicit lack of confidentiality against the host. Decision 4 now requires an execute-specific possession proof over the token, destination, offer, and job before holder injection. Its domain fixes the operation as execute. Decision 5 separates keyspace and holder fingerprints, scopes rights by owner, ties the offering VM to the signing being, and pins the existing durable host mint root. These are adequate architectural answers to R1 and R2; HTTP framing and the typed SecretStore envelope remain child-node work.

Decision 7 and the delta now extend exclusion through worker/device quiescence, address cancellation and restart, limit the guarantee to cooperating IRON clients, and explicitly assign the unproved integration to `add-aie-host-sidecar`. That answers R4 at this node's rigor. The existing RPC spike is correctly not offered as lock evidence.

The strongest case for acceptance is that this node produces an ADR, not an executable sidecar. It should not have to choose an HTTP route, record serialization, membership provider, exact flock path, or scheduler implementation. I narrow my blind take accordingly: fixed job limits, grant lifetime defaults, and supported kernel details can be settled in the owning implementation contracts. The existing boot/resume identity injection also provides a suitable locator seam; continuous locator updates are not required here.

R3 is improved but not settled. Current-offer policy, same-key membership, and the admission boundary are now explicit. Two remaining gaps cross the offer, mint, membership, and execution nodes: how a grant identifies the offer incarnation it authorizes, and when membership removal becomes authoritative. These require a shared contract, not product implementation in this review.

## Required amendments

### R3a — P1: Bind replacement revocation to an identifiable offer incarnation

**Evidence:** `design.md:66` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:64` grant `right("<owner_fp>/<offer_id>", "aie-execute")`. `design.md:96` says a replacement leaf supersedes the old one and old tokens fail. The delta at line 127 requires replacement to invalidate prior grants, but neither document defines an incarnation binding, an authenticated grant issuance boundary, or a rule that a replacement must use a permanently new offer ID. Reading the current signed record supplies current policy; it does not reveal which version the issuer authorized when minting this token.

**Failure:** Mint T1 for personal offer `A/npu`; replace that offer with another personal offer under the same key; then have the same valid holder submit T1 with a fresh nonce and proof. Resource, root, holder, and current policy all match. The declared checks cannot distinguish T1 from a newly authorized grant for the replacement. A personal-to-guild replacement has the same problem when the old holder is also a current guild member: membership alone does not implement the unconditional invalidation promise. Tombstone followed by recreation can resurrect the old grant as well.

**Required contract:** Choose and pin how a grant is tied to the authorized incarnation and compared with the verified current offer at admission. An issuer-authenticated generation or record commitment is one option; permanently retiring an old resource ID and publishing a new one is another. State the tombstone/recreation and restart behavior so an old grant cannot become current again. A caller-supplied version in the request proof alone is insufficient: a holder of T1 can sign a new request. Assign the binding consistently across `add-aie-keyspace-claims`, mint/guest-offer, and the sidecar. Exact field encoding remains with those children.

**Acceptance scenarios:** Replacement under the chosen naming rules rejects T1 even when the policy and holder remain unchanged; a newly minted T2 succeeds; personal-to-guild replacement rejects T1 even for a holder who is a member; tombstone/recreation and verifier restart do not revive T1. All rejection happens before load.

### R3b — P2: Reconcile TTL freshness with the removal-at-admission promise

**Evidence:** `design.md:108` delegates freshness to the trusted lookup's stated TTL, while line 116 says a removed member fails at admission. The delta at lines 147–153 promises refusal whenever removal precedes admission, without qualifying that promise by a freshness interval. Its requirement rejects expired evidence but permits evidence that is still valid under that TTL.

**Failure:** The verifier receives membership evidence valid for a positive TTL. The member is removed after issuance, and a queued job reaches admission before that TTL expires. Reusing the evidence satisfies the stated freshness rule while violating the removal scenario. Re-querying a provider whose response can itself be cached does not resolve this unless the provider's revocation semantics are specified.

**Required contract:** Choose either authoritative membership revalidation at admission with the corresponding removal visibility guarantee, or explicitly bounded delayed revocation. For the latter, define who sets/enforces the maximum evidence age, what event/time it is measured from, and when removal must cause refusal; align the scenario with that bound. The numeric configuration and lookup implementation may remain with `add-guild-compute-holder`, but that node and the sidecar must inherit one consistent rule. Preserve same-key binding and fail-closed handling of unavailable or expired evidence.

**Acceptance scenarios:** Exercise removal after evidence issuance but before TTL expiry, removal beyond the allowed bound, and lookup failure at admission. Each must have one specified outcome, including for a job queued before removal.

## Implementation handoff and validation

The execute proof contract remains outside the Biscuit evaluator, as required by the living runtime and demonstrated by `lib/mjolnir/secrets/redeem.ex:53`. `Mjolnir.Secrets.Nonces` is a process-local, 60-second, consume-once store; child implementation must distinguish internal revalidation of an authenticated queued request from accepting a replay or consuming its nonce twice. This observation does not require a second queue design in this architecture node. The new offer type also remains explicit work: `SecretStore` verifies recognized signed records and rejects unknown envelope types despite its stale module header.
