## Independent take — written before opening design, tasks, or delta

1. Pin host-owned AIE execution behind overlay RPC; refuse guest PCI or accel-device exposure as an inferred extension of the successful spike.
2. Pin a discoverable vm.json locator through configure_identity, with explicit unavailable behavior; an address is discovery, never authorization.
3. Pin the distinction between host issuer, offering VM principal, keyspace owner, and requesting holder; refuse implicit authority from network reachability or a caller-supplied VM id.
4. Pin one durable host Biscuit root and algorithm-committing holder fingerprints; refuse a new mint root or interchange with raw-key Sites fingerprints.
5. Pin signature proof over the token, verifier nonce, audience, and exact requested operation/input before injecting holder facts; refuse bearer-only compute access or replay.
6. Pin explicit resource, operation, expiry, and offer-lifecycle checks so stopped, replaced, or withdrawn offers cannot retain usable execution grants.
7. Pin a typed signed offer record and its verification/keyspace binding; refuse treating SecretStore's existing envelope dispatcher as arbitrary record support.
8. Pin single-key use without guild lookup, and a separate fail-closed membership-injection extension; refuse token-asserted membership or silently mandatory guilds.
9. Pin bounded job/input/output behavior, exclusive occupancy, and terminal failure ownership; the vector-add spike proves neither scheduling nor safe arbitrary kernel execution.
10. Accept RPC serialization and a narrow kernel surface in exchange for simple sharing and centralized admission; capacity claims must not become speculative assets or unproven allocation guarantees.

The host remains trusted. The locator is not an execution grant, and the spike is not evidence for authentication or occupancy safety.

HTTP and proof verification remain outside the Biscuit evaluator, consistent with `lib/mjolnir/secrets/redeem.ex:53` and the living runtime. Personal offers remain usable without a guild lookup.

Decision 7 retains R4's exclusion boundary through worker/device quiescence, including timeout, cancellation, and restart. It expressly limits flock coordination to cooperating clients and assigns the remaining evidence to the sidecar node.

The strongest challenge to my independent take is scope: this node specifies the architecture and an ADR, while children own the HTTP surface, typed envelope, membership integration, and execution machinery. Requiring grant lifetime defaults, job size limits, supported kernel choices, exact record encoding, or a scheduler here would expand the review beyond the cross-node decisions. I therefore do not turn take lines 6 and 9 into new blockers. Likewise, a stopped VM that remains dormant-restorable is intentionally still a being under the delta; disappearance is the refusal boundary, rather than every temporary stop. These are defensible choices for a host-executed offer.

## Prior blocking findings are resolved

**R3a — offer incarnation.** `design.md:66–109` and the delta's Execute and Capacity requirements bind the issuer-authenticated right to `<owner_fp>/<offer_id>/<incarnation>`. Mint obtains the incarnation from the verified leaf; admission compares it to the current verified leaf before load. The caller cannot repair an old grant by signing a new request with a different incarnation. The delta at lines 236–258 covers unchanged-policy replacement, successful newly minted grants, a personal-to-guild replacement whose old holder is still a member, and tombstone/recreation across verifier restart. This supplies the missing shared binding across keyspace publication, mint, and sidecar admission.

The non-resurrection requirement is substantive: hashing an identical old signed leaf would reproduce its old incarnation. The accepted contract explicitly requires new bytes/new incarnation on replacement and recreation and prohibits revival of prior grants (`design.md:101–109`, delta lines 203–208). Enforcing that requirement belongs to the assigned typed-record lifecycle work, including preventing retired bytes from becoming the current leaf again. This is already required by the contract, not an additional amendment or an assumption that hashing by itself prevents rollback. Existing `SecretStore.handle_call/3` simply writes recognized records and currently removes the file on delete (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:255–273`); that code is not implementation evidence for the new lifecycle guarantee.

**R3b — membership freshness and removal.** Decision 6 explicitly chooses authoritative admission revalidation, with no delayed-revocation allowance. The delta at lines 120–140 requires a lookup for that admission attempt, bound to the proof key and current offer keyspace; prior enqueue evidence cannot authorize admission. The provider max-age is measured from provider `issued-at` to admission, and configuration ownership is assigned to `add-guild-compute-holder`. A negative or unavailable lookup cannot fall back to still-unexpired older evidence. Lines 171–191 make removal before the older evidence expires, expired evidence, and lookup failure testable refusals. A provider that returns stale positive membership after the specified removal would violate the authoritative lookup/removal scenario; the TTL does not permit it. That resolves the former ambiguity without dictating the provider implementation.

Decision 6 also separates authentication of the queued request from replay acceptance: enqueue consumes the client nonce once, while admission revalidates the authenticated request and current authorization without consuming a second nonce. Work already loaded may finish; queued work must satisfy the current incarnation and membership at the specified admission boundary.
