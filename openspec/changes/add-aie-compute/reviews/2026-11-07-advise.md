## Independent take — written before opening design, tasks, or delta

1. Pin host-owned AIE execution behind overlay RPC; the spike proves this path without adding PCI devices or a guest KMD.
2. Pin a locator as discovery only, never a grant, and specify refresh/removal across boot and resume so old guests do not retain misleading availability.
3. Pin the identity that owns and offers capacity separately from the authenticated principal consuming it; VM identity alone does not prove either authority.
4. Pin holder proof, verifier audience, freshness, and operation/resource binding before injecting any holder fact; possession of token bytes must not authorize compute.
5. Pin algorithm-committing holder fingerprints separately from SecretStore keyspace identifiers, with an explicit binding between their namespaces.
6. Pin guild membership as authenticated, verifier-injected evidence with fail-closed freshness and revocation semantics; preserve the existing single-key mint without guild lookup.
7. Refuse a second mint authority or HTTP inside the Biscuit runtime, and require issuer-signed scope plus attenuation that cannot widen a compute grant.
8. Pin admission, cancellation, and occupancy semantics against host IRON users; the spike did not prove mutual exclusion, bounded work, or recovery after failure.
9. Refuse speculative assets in spawn and implicit trust in stored offer bytes; claims need authenticated ownership, withdrawal semantics, and no promise beyond available capacity.
10. Accept the tradeoff of host mediation and bounded contention instead of direct guest hardware access, provided dependent nodes inherit testable trust and lifecycle contracts.

## Comparison and steelman

The host-sidecar choice is supported. Decisions 1–3 answer the device, principal, locator, and deployment concerns without adding a PCI path. Decision 4 explicitly preserves personal-key minting without guild lookup and uses the correct holder-fingerprint namespace. Decision 5 keeps claims out of spawn. Decision 6 chooses serialization rather than promising concurrent array isolation. The explicit statement that the host is trusted is appropriate.

The strongest case for accepting is that this node lands an ADR and hands implementation to named children. It need not specify HTTP routes, envelope serialization, a membership provider's wire format, or a scheduler. I also withdraw any implication in my initial take that a locator needs continuous push updates: boot/resume reconciliation plus explicit request-time failure can be sufficient. Existing `lib/mjolnir/vm.ex:3000` deliberately reinjects identity on resume.

That scope argument does not settle the authorization relationships the children must share. A holder check is not itself caller authentication; a signed leaf is not itself permission to offer this host's capacity; and a flock held by one process is not automatically the lifetime of its XRT worker. The following are missing contracts, not requests for a competing architecture or product implementation in this node.

## Required amendments

### R1 — P1: Authenticate the execute request before injecting holder facts

**Evidence:** `design.md:55` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:64` require a holder check and injection for “the caller's key,” but do not define how that key is authenticated. `lib/mjolnir/biscuit.ex:201` accepts an already supplied fingerprint and an injection boolean; it performs no transport proof. The cited runtime explicitly has no HTTP. The archived capability requirement's signed tuple applies to secret redemption and explicitly does not automatically apply to other profiles. Post-blind inspection of `lib/mjolnir/secrets/redeem.ex:60` confirms that signature verification and nonce consumption live outside the Biscuit evaluator.

**Failure:** A presenter supplies stolen token bytes and the public key named by the token. Computing that public key's fingerprint and injecting it satisfies the described holder check without proving possession of its private key. Even an added signature over only the token leaves the submitted job or destination unbound.

**Required contract:** Name the execute authentication profile and the component responsible for it. Require proof of possession bound to this token, the intended verifier, offer/operation, and submitted job, with defined replay handling, before injecting `holder`. Specify that token-supplied facts cannot satisfy verifier-only holder evidence and that holderless tokens are rejected. The exact route and framing implementation remain with the child nodes; an explicit reference to an applicable existing profile is also acceptable. Preserve HTTP outside the runtime crate.

**Acceptance scenarios:** Stolen token plus the correct public key but no proof; replayed proof; changed job or offer after signing; and token-authored holder facts all refuse before NPU load. A valid proof still succeeds for a personal offer without a guild lookup.

### R2 — P1: Bind the grant to an authoritative, unambiguous offer

**Evidence:** `design.md:21` makes the VM being the principal, `design.md:53` places a path-safe offer id in an owner's keyspace, and `design.md:55` grants only `right(<offer_id>, "aie-execute")`. The delta at lines 44 and 108 does not establish the verified relationship between the signing owner, the named VM, host capacity, and the Biscuit issuer. It does not say whether `offer_id` is globally unique or keyspace-local. The living runtime already has a durable host mint root; being the offer principal and being the cryptographic mint issuer are distinct roles.

**Failure:** Two keyspaces publish the same local id and a token for one is evaluated against the other. Alternatively, a valid signer names someone else's extant VM in its own signed record. Signature validity and VM existence alone do not establish authority to share that VM's host capacity.

**Required contract:** Define the canonical resource identity and its binding to the owner keyspace, being/VM, and executing host. Specify the authenticated ownership/delegation check required to publish and grant capacity, who may mint execute rights, and which trust root the sidecar accepts. State how keyspace fingerprints, holder fingerprints, and any being identifier relate without equating their namespaces. A host-root mint on the being's authorized instruction can fit the current design; the author must pin the chosen relationship.

**Acceptance scenarios:** Equal local ids in different keyspaces cannot share rights; an owner-signed record naming an unrelated VM is refused; an untrusted issuer cannot authorize execution. Keep actual offer serialization in `add-aie-keyspace-claims`. Its implementation must account for `lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:711`, which currently recognizes specific record types and rejects an unknown envelope type; the module header's “STUBBED” description is stale and is not evidence that arbitrary signed offers already work.

### R3 — P1: Make current offer and guild policy authoritative at admission

**Evidence:** `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:85` requires injected membership and a token check, but defines no source/freshness contract or handling of an older token lacking that check. Lines 46 and 57 refuse a missing being but do not define offer withdrawal, changed guild policy, or the validity of a job that waited for occupancy. Decision 6 permits waiting, so admission and actual execution need a stated boundary.

**Failure:** An offer changes from personal to guild-scoped, or a member is removed, while an old token or queued job remains valid under previously observed policy. Different children can each implement their local text yet disagree about whether the job may start. Membership for one key must also not authorize a holder proof from another key.

**Required contract:** Pin which verified offer state governs execution, how withdrawal/replacement invalidates prior grants, and when a queued request is revalidated. Bind `member(ks, pk)` to the same authenticated key that produced the holder fingerprint and to the keyspace named by the verified offer. Require trusted membership evidence with a stated freshness/revocation bound and fail-closed handling of unavailable or expired evidence; token-authored membership must not supply that evidence. Define whether already-running work may finish after withdrawal. These are policy boundaries; the provider and lookup implementation remain `add-guild-compute-holder`.

**Acceptance scenarios:** A removed member, withdrawn offer, personal-to-guild change with an old token, member/holder key mismatch, and withdrawal while queued produce the specified refusal at the chosen admission boundary. Personal offers continue to need no membership service.

### R4 — P2: Bound the occupancy guarantee to the real worker lifetime

**Evidence:** `design.md:76` asserts IRON Lab flock protection and the delta at line 128 says to hold “the IRON occupancy lock around a run.” Neither identifies the shared lock protocol nor defines completion on timeout, cancellation, or sidecar failure. The spike finding explicitly says occupancy/locking was not tested; `design.md:93` nevertheless lists no open hypotheses.

**Failure:** A request times out or the sidecar exits, releasing its lock while its XRT child still runs. A replacement process takes the lock and loads another design. Separately, a sidecar-private lock serializes two guests but does not coordinate with a host IRON invocation using another lock.

**Required contract:** Pin the shared lock identity/protocol used by supported host IRON and sidecar entry points, and keep exclusion from before load until the worker/device is quiescent. State fail-closed recovery when quiescence cannot be established and the limit of an advisory lock against non-cooperating direct clients. Do not broaden this into a scheduler. Record the remaining lock integration uncertainty with a focused verification obligation owned by `add-aie-host-sidecar`; the RPC spike is not evidence for it.

**Acceptance scenarios:** A host IRON run contends with a guest run; timeout/cancellation cannot permit an overlapping load; killing and restarting the sidecar with a live worker either preserves exclusion or refuses new execution until safe recovery.
