## What I would pin

1. **Everything the relay chooses must be in the key or the AAD.** The
   relay owns the path. Owner, room, label, publish instance, track
   name, group and frame sequence each need to be bound, or the relay
   can move valid ciphertext between them without a key.
2. **One key must never meet one nonce twice across reconnects.**
   `relay-room-auth` closes every session at `exp` and the relay does
   not bridge publishers, so a publisher restarts at least hourly. If
   sequence numbers restart under the same key, AES-GCM breaks. A
   per-publish instance value in the derivation is required, not
   optional.
3. **The reader's choice among instances is made from data the relay
   controls.** Any high-water mark must move only after a frame of
   that instance decrypts. A mark set from an announced path alone
   lets a relay with no key pin every reader with one fake
   announcement.
4. **Fail closed on plaintext.** A client that holds a credential for
   a room must neither publish nor render unencrypted media there.
   Otherwise the page or relay downgrades the call silently.
5. **The catalog or track list is content.** It is encrypted, bound to
   the same label and instance, and ordered, so a relay cannot serve
   an old list or another member's list.
6. **Acceptance capture uses a relay built for the test.** No key-log
   switch reaches the production unit or its environment file.

## What I would refuse

7. Any requirement that says a member cannot impersonate another
   member. The secret is shared. Per-label keys derived from it stop
   the relay, not a member. Member against member rests on the relay
   enforcing `<participant>/**`, so relay plus one member defeats it.
   The spec must say so.
8. Any removal requirement for link guests that has no delivery path
   built in this change. The mint needs a user principal
   (`mint.ex:48`), so a guest cannot fetch its own token or key.
9. "Hidden from the host" for room, label, who is present and when.
   The token root and publish pattern carry room and label
   (`mint.ex:23-24`), and the mint logs them.
10. A "proven" that cites a summary. Each one names a log line.

## One tradeoff

11. Per-label keys from one room secret are cheap and work with the
    upstream profile, but give no sender authentication between
    members and no forward secrecy. Per-sender signatures would give
    both the relay and members no room, at a per-frame cost and off
    the upstream path. I would take the first and print the limit.

## Questions I expect the design to have answered

- Where does a guest's second room token come from once the first
  expires, and does the link still work after an hour?
- Is a room's secret one per call or standing? A standing link leaves
  the key in every guest's history for the life of the room.
- Is the high-water mark kept across a page reload? If not, rollback
  protection lasts one page life.
- A publisher whose clock runs ahead and is then corrected mints a
  lower UUIDv7. Does the reader refuse its own honest publisher?
- What does the reader do when upstream removes ordering from the
  instance value?
