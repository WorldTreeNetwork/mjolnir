## Verdict in one screen

The architecture is right and should be kept: two separate credentials, a content secret Mjolnir never holds, no relay change, removal by a new credential, and acceptance by a capture taken with the relay's own session keys. The contract is also unusually honest about what was not run.

It goes back for six reasons. The first two are the ones that matter.

1. **A security claim in D2 is false at this pin.** The design says a relay that re-mounts alice's broadcast under `bob/` is caught. It is not. The keys do not include the label. A session with no content credential re-served alice's ciphertext under bob's label, and a credential holder applying exactly D2's checks decrypted it as bob's (`2026-11-23-relabel-probe.out`). The fix is small and needs no new cryptography: put the label in the credential's `context`. The same probe then gets `Authentication`.
2. **The contract does not say that upstream calls this format "not ready yet".** The pinned tree itself holds the maintainer's audit of 2026-11-16. It documents the weakness in point 1, and it has already decided what replaces `moq-e2ee-01-wip`: different key derivation, no UUIDv7 epoch, and native support in the publish and watch components in place of bridges. That does not stop this work, because nothing is stored and a profile change is a client upgrade. It does have to be written down, and "which profile ships on by default" becomes a decision for Duke.
3. Four requirements in the spec delta promise more than this change can deliver (B3).
4. The threat model lists what the relay can see and not what an active relay can do (B4).
5. The browser bridge and the native bridge do not agree on what goes on the wire (B5).
6. The acceptance relay is not named, and the production relay has no keylog switch (B6).

None of the unrun proofs (Hang components, real media, cross-language through the relay, fragment leaks, relay-key capture) blocks accepting a corrected contract. They block shipping. One of them, the Hang component bridge, should run first because it is the only one whose failure changes the architecture.

## Independent take, written before the design

1. Pin the key boundary: the content credential never transits or rests on any Mjolnir process. Browser: fragment only, scrubbed at once, no referrer, no third-party script.
2. Pin (key, nonce) uniqueness as a stated invariant. What makes a publisher session's key fresh across reconnects and rejoins?
3. Pin that hazard for Mjolnir specifically: a guest restored from a snapshot resumes with the same key state and counters.
4. Pin what the ciphertext is bound to. The relay may drop and delay. It must not replay across tracks or splice one participant's frames into another's. Say that members can forge each other.
5. Pin metadata honestly: owner id, room and label are clear in the token and in the mint log line.
6. Pin removal: no forward secrecy; a removed member keeps the old secret and a working token; define who decides and how the new credential travels.
7. Refuse plaintext fallback.
8. Refuse our own cryptography and silent divergence: one pin on both sides, vectors both ways, a profile version that fails closed.
9. Refuse overclaiming: with an operator-served page this protects against the relay, its logs and a seized box, not against an operator who serves different code. Non-extractable keys stop export, not use.
10. Tradeoff: a shared link secret on the upstream draft now, with the credential as the seam where a real group key agreement could plug in later. The relay should need no code. Content-layer encryption is also what would let untrusted peers carry fan-out later.

How the contract answered them: 1, 5, 7, 8 and 9 are answered well. 2 and 3 are answered by upstream's epoch rule and D8. 4 is where it fails (B1, B4). 6 is half answered (B3d). 10 is not discussed (B4).

## Findings: contract defects

### B1. The label is not in the keys, so a relay can present one participant as another

The claim: `design.md:32` says binding `<opaque>` to the label means a relay that re-mounts alice's broadcast under `bob/` is caught, "proven in the epoch-in-path test". The spec repeats it as a scenario (`specs/call-e2ee/spec.md:27-30`), and ADR 0017 leans on it (`docs/decisions/0017-call-e2ee.md:12`).

What the pinned code does (moq `4a6f52248`):

- Payload keys derive from `context ‖ epoch ‖ kid ‖ track name ‖ domain` (`drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-e2ee.md:193-199` and `:216-224`; `rs/moq-e2ee/src/generation.rs:146-153` and `:170-176`).
- The name passed to `path()` enters only the path derivation (`rs/moq-e2ee/src/credential.rs:108-115`).
- `consume(epoch)` takes no broadcast name (`rs/moq-e2ee/src/credential.rs:104`).

So `path("alice")` and `path("bob")` differ, but nothing a subscriber derives after that depends on the label. A relay sees `path(bob)` as soon as bob publishes. It can serve alice's bytes at `bob/<path(bob)>/<alice's epoch>`. The subscriber's check `opaque == path("bob")` passes, and `consume(alice's epoch)` opens alice's frames.

The spec scenario does not catch this because it offers `bob/<path(alice)>/<epoch>`, the relay that forgot to swap the second segment.

Upstream found the same thing. The pinned tree's `quest/m3/e2ee-format.md:54-63` reads: a room that shares one credential and publishes two broadcasts has two paths and two epochs, and a relay that answers a request for the first with the second's instance and tracks is accepted. It adds that the draft's sentence "A relay that presents a wrong epoch causes authentication failure" is false when the wrong epoch is another broadcast's real one. `rs/moq-e2ee/Cargo.toml:13` points at that file.

Shown here, on the real relay harness with the Mjolnir decider and tokens shaped as `Mjolnir.Relay.Mint` signs them:

```
probe relabel: a session with no content credential re-serves alice's ciphertext at bob/VgnZo2FYE3-Vkx2r5LXGXw/01a20c5a-d74c-…
probe relabel: dave sees 2 canonical epoch(s) under bob/VgnZo2FYE3-Vkx2r5LXGXw/
probe relabel: dave, reading label `bob` with every D2 check passed, decrypted "ALICE-FRAME-36"
```

Source: `2026-11-23-relabel-probe.rs`. In the probe a second token for label `bob` stands in for the relay, which needs no token because it owns the namespace.

Why it matters: the relay is the adversary this change exists for. `relay-room-auth` left "attributing a track by the first segment as received" to the call client (`openspec/changes/archive/2026-11-23-add-relay-room-auth/proposal.md:36`), so the label is what the page will use to say who is speaking.

Resolution, in order of preference:

1. **Put the label in the credential context.** Each publisher's context becomes `mjolnir-call/v1/<owner>/<room>/<label>`. The opaque segment is `path("call")` under that credential. A subscriber builds the credential for the label it was announced. `context` is the draft's own field for "the broadcast's end-to-end identity" (`drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-e2ee.md:103-106`), so this is ordinary use of the profile. The probe's second test does exactly this, lets the relay serve alice's ciphertext even under the physical name the victim asks for, and gets `refused: Authentication`.
2. Keep the layout and withdraw the claim: say the relay can re-label, and require the page to attribute streams only by identity carried inside the generation. That is weaker, since any member can assert any display name.
3. Wait for `moq-e2ee-01`, which binds the broadcast name natively (B2).

After option 1 the honest statement is: a member cannot publish under another label because the relay's grant stops it, and the relay cannot move a stream to another label because the keys stop it. Only the two together can impersonate.

Owed edits: D1, D2, the spec's first two requirements, and the re-label scenario rewritten for a relay that swaps both segments. Move the probe into `spikes/rust/tests/` as the regression.

### B2. Upstream's own status at this pin is missing from the record

ADR 0017 says the profile "remains experimental" (`docs/decisions/0017-call-e2ee.md:7`). The pinned tree says more than that, and it bears on four decisions here.

`quest/m5/e2ee/README.md:11`: "Decided 2026-11-16 … E2EE is 'not ready yet'", and the finished format ships as `moq-e2ee-01` "before any quest in this epic builds on it". `quest/m3/e2ee-format.md:25-49` lists what was decided:

| Upstream decision for `moq-e2ee-01` | What it does to this contract |
|---|---|
| Keys bind the semantic broadcast name; `produce(semantic)`, `consume(semantic, instance)` (`:27-32`, `:109-115`) | Fixes B1 natively. The adapter's calls change. |
| One key per group; `Exhausted` and the budget counters are deleted (`:33-34`, `:64-70`) | Every key changes. Until then a track stops at 2^36 bytes or 2^24 operations per key (19.1 hours at 8 Mb/s by upstream's figure), and failed opens count, so a relay can spend a subscriber's budget. The contract mentions neither. |
| `e2ee::Epoch` becomes `moq_net::Instance`; UUIDv7 and its ordering are deleted because "Nothing outside tests compares epochs" (`:37-38`, `:77-82`) | D2's rule "read the greatest epoch" (`design.md:43`, `spec.md:19`) would be the only reader of that ordering, and it has no successor. |
| The per-track wrap is public, the wrappers implement the moq-net traits, and upstream's own mux bridge is "closed and superseded" (`quest/m5/e2ee/README.md:38-39`); publish and watch take an encrypted broadcast through the `@moq/net` interfaces (`quest/m5/e2ee/browser.md:11`) | The bridges in D4 and C2 are stopgaps. |

State of upstream on 2026-11-24 05:34Z, from the GitHub API: `main` is `db025c8c5`. The draft and the vector file have not changed since the pin. No open pull request has e2ee in its title. So `-01` is decided and not landed.

This is not a reason to stop. No ciphertext is stored and recording is out of scope, so moving profiles is a coordinated client upgrade, which D7 already says. It is a reason to:

- record the above in ADR 0017 and D7, with the file references;
- keep the Mjolnir-specific surface thin (an adapter, the path layout, the invite), and say in the spec that the layout belongs to the profile named in the link;
- ask Duke which profile ships "on by default" (new ASK in `tasks.md`).

### B3. Four requirements promise more than this change delivers

**a. The room token is written into the encryption spec.** `spec.md:51` says a browser guest SHALL receive the content credential and the room token in the fragment. How a token arrives is not an encryption property, and D1 says the two stay separate. Combined with facts already on the books, that SHALL freezes three limits:

- A token lives at most 3600 seconds by default (the ceiling is an operator setting) and only the room's owner can mint one, because the token root is the caller's own id (`openspec/specs/relay-room-auth/spec.md:66-68`; `lib/mjolnir/relay/mint.ex:70-74`). The archived room-auth proposal says it plainly: "a guest call lasts up to 3600 seconds" and "a delegated invite … is a later change" (`openspec/changes/archive/2026-11-23-add-relay-room-auth/proposal.md:26`). No bead exists for that later change (`bd search invite` returns nothing).
- So an invite link dies an hour after it is made, and a guest is cut off at `exp` with no way to renew. Make-before-break assumes a client that can mint.
- One link carries one label. A forwarded link puts two people on one label, and D2's "read the greatest epoch" then shows only one of them.

Resolution: the spec constrains the content credential only. Say that a token delivered in a link goes to the relay and nowhere else. State the three limits in the proposal and name who owns them (`mjolnir-rurh.5`, or a delegated-invite change that does not exist yet).

**b. The guest thaw rule is a requirement with no task and no signal on some guests.** `spec.md:58-64` adds a SHALL and a scenario for a publisher in a Mjolnir guest. `tasks.md:33` says no task implements it. Folding it would put unbuilt behaviour into a spec that records what is built. The rule also cannot hold everywhere today: a memory snapshot of a guest whose kernel lacks `CONFIG_VMGENID` thaws with no generation-id change (`openspec/specs/vm-freeze/spec.md:175`), and the production fleet kernel is still that kernel (`docs/LEARNINGS.md:13`).

Resolution: keep "a publisher mints a new generation for each process instance", which is true by construction and testable. Move the guest rule into ADR 0017 as a constraint on the change that ships a guest publisher, with three additions: it is supported on VMGENID captures only; the re-mint happens only after the guest observes the generation change, so that the new epoch is drawn from randomness reseeded after the thaw and never from state copied in the snapshot; and on a legacy capture a guest publisher does not run. Add one sentence that any credential holder inside a guest, publisher or reader, is inside the host's trust boundary, because a memory snapshot writes guest memory to the host's disk.

**c. "History" claims more than `replaceState` gives.** `spec.md:51` says the page removes the fragment "from the address bar and history". `design.md:48` says "session history", which is the accurate half. `history.replaceState` modifies the current session history entry ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/History/replaceState)). Whether the browser's persistent history keeps the original visit is up to the browser. There are reports that it does: Mozilla bug [753264](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=753264) reports that a `replaceState` adds the new state to global history without removing the original entry, with the comment that no specification covers global history, and Auth0 treated the URL with and without the hash as two history entries ([auth0/lock#967](https://github.com/auth0/lock/pull/967)). I read these through search summaries and did not test Chromium 158 or Firefox 159.

Resolution: write "session history" in the spec. Add to C3: after a join, inspect the browser's history store and record what it holds. If the secret stays there, say so in the page's security note. That is the ordinary cost of a secret in a link, and it should be stated instead of denied.

**d. Removal "without reopening its session" has no path for a browser guest.** `spec.md:41` and `proposal.md:37` say remaining pages switch on the open session. Nothing says how an open page receives a new credential. Opening a re-shared link in a new tab is a new session. Each guest's link also carries that guest's own token, so "re-share the link" means one hand-delivered link per remaining guest. The overlap is unbounded too: `design.md:63-68` says each publisher announces the new broadcast "beside the old" and "then" finishes the old one, with no rule for when. While any publisher has not yet received the new link, it keeps feeding the removed member.

Resolution, three pins:

- How an open page takes a new credential. Either a same-document fragment navigation that the page handles on `hashchange`, or pasting the link into the page. Or narrow the requirement to clients that receive the credential in-app, and say that for link guests removal is a rejoin.
- A publisher stops encrypting under the old credential as soon as its new broadcast is announced. It does not wait for subscribers. The spike measured 44–48 ms for a subscriber that already holds the new credential.
- A reader MAY keep the previous credential, read-only, until the broadcasts under it end. That gives continuity during a slow hand-out without sending anything new to the removed member.

### B4. The threat model stops at what the relay can see

`design.md:92-101` is a good table of what is visible. There is no list of what an active relay can do, and an architecture record for encryption needs one. From the draft and the pinned source:

| An active relay can | Source |
|---|---|
| Drop, delay and reorder | `drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-e2ee.md:329` |
| Replay earlier ciphertext of the same publisher instance to a new or late subscriber | `:329` |
| End a group after any frame without detection | `:372-373` |
| Shift timestamps | `:370` |
| Withhold a newer instance, or the broadcasts of a newer credential | `:148` |
| Spend a subscriber's per-key budget with frames that fail to open | `quest/m3/e2ee-format.md:68-69` |
| Present one member's stream under another member's label | B1, until fixed |

It cannot read, forge, or move ciphertext across track, group, frame, epoch, key id or context.

The record should also carry three things that shape later work:

- **A permanent product consequence.** With content encryption the platform can never record, transcode, transcribe or compose a call (`quest/m5/e2ee/README.md:46`). Only an endpoint that holds the credential can. A recorder that runs in a Mjolnir guest puts the credential on the operator's host.
- **What was not chosen.** The ADR compares three places for the epoch and no alternatives for the scheme. Two sentences are enough: SFrame with MLS gives forward secrecy, recovery after compromise and real removal, and needs a delivery service and member identity keys with no implementation at this pin. The credential is the seam: an MLS exporter could later supply `{kid, secret}` per MLS epoch without changing frame encryption.
- **The accepted boundary in the capability sentence.** `proposal.md:15` and the first requirement say content is readable "only by holders of the room's content credential". Add: against the relay and host processes; the origin that serves the page's code is trusted (Duke, 2026-11-23, this iteration).

### B5. The browser bridge and the native bridge do not agree on a wire surface

The browser scaffolding adds a protected track named `bridge.tracks`, a manifest of semantic names and track info, and its decrypt side waits for it (`spikes/js/bridge.ts:22`, `:56`, `:115`). The Rust bridge has no such track and hardcodes `catalog.json` and `video` (`spikes/rust/tests/e2ee_iroh_live.rs:16-29`, `:77-78`). C2 and N1 are separate tasks. Built as they stand, a browser reading a native publisher finds no manifest and never learns the tracks.

Resolution: pin one surface in the spec. I recommend the draft's own model, where the decrypted catalog names the tracks and the subscriber derives each physical name (`drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-e2ee.md:281-285`), with no extra track. If a manifest is kept, it is part of the call's wire contract and both bridges implement it.

### B6. The acceptance relay is not named

`spec.md:36` says "the relay running with its QUIC keylog", and D9 says the relay runs with `quic.keylog`. `mjolnir-relay` builds its `moq_relay::Config` in code (`native/mjolnir_relay/src/main.rs:156-161`) and has no keylog setting. The setting is a moq-tokio flag and environment variable (`rs/moq-tokio/src/quic.rs:316-322`). Left open, the shortest way to satisfy A1 is a keylog variable in the shipped unit, which would be a switch that dumps every tenant's session keys.

Resolution: one line in D9 and A1. The capture runs on a harness relay built from the same crate, as the spikes already do, or on a test-only build. It never runs on the production unit, and the shipped binary gets no runtime keylog switch.

## Browser keys

Duke's steering: the browser is necessarily less secure for now, non-extractable keys can mitigate somewhat, "but the cipher is different". The first part is accepted and recorded. The rest, checked against the pinned code:

**The pinned browser package already uses non-extractable keys.**

- The secret is imported as an HKDF key with `extractable = false`, and the copy of the bytes is zero-filled (`js/e2ee/src/secret.ts:21-26`).
- The constructor also accepts a non-extractable HKDF `CryptoKey` in place of bytes and refuses an extractable one (`secret.ts:27-36`; `credential.ts:15-23`). So the page can import the fragment itself, first thing, and hand the library only a handle. The page then owns the 32-byte length check.
- Every derived key is imported with `extractable = false`: per-track AES-GCM keys (`key.ts:94-106`), the name sealing key and the name MAC key (`name.ts:73-84`).

So `design.md:49` ("hold the secret in a `CryptoKey`-like closure") can be made exact: import to a non-extractable HKDF key before any other script runs, pass the handle, keep no other copy.

**What that prevents.** Reading the 32 bytes back out. A script that gets in later cannot leave with the room secret, so it cannot decrypt publisher instances that start after it loses access, and the secret cannot be logged or serialized by accident.

**What it does not prevent.**

- *Use of the key.* The root is an HKDF key with `deriveBits`. Any same-origin script that reaches the handle can derive the raw bytes of every track key for every epoch it knows, and epochs are public. WebCrypto lets the caller choose extractability per derived key, so a non-extractable root never makes derived keys non-extractable. This holds whether the library uses `deriveBits` plus `importKey`, as it does, or `deriveKey`.
- *Plaintext.* Decoded frames, media streams and the decrypted catalog are ordinary page data.
- *The secret before import.* It is in `location.hash` and in whatever string the parser makes of it. JavaScript strings cannot be wiped.
- *Code served by the operator.* That is the accepted boundary.

So "mitigates somewhat" is accurate. It limits a passing script compromise to the time it lasts. It does nothing against the origin.

**One asymmetry.** A guest's page can hold only a handle. The owner's page cannot, if it is to make invite links later, because a link contains the raw secret. Either the owner's page keeps the secret or the link text, or it makes every invite at the start.

**Possible hardening, same cipher, later.** A worker that owns the key and exposes only per-track seal and open, so the main document never holds the handle. A stricter policy than D3 lists: `script-src 'self'` with no inline or eval, Trusted Types, `frame-ancestors 'none'`.

**"The cipher is different."** I cannot tell what this refers to, so here is each reading against the pin.

| If it means | Finding | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| The browser and native ends use different ciphers | Not at this pin. Both use AES-128-GCM with a 96-bit nonce and a 16-byte tag under HKDF-SHA-256 (`rs/moq-e2ee/src/protect.rs:7-15`, `:33`; `js/e2ee/src/key.ts:50-68`). WebCrypto's HKDF with the profile salt computes the draft's extract-then-expand exactly (`secret.ts:47-51`). The shared vectors pass on both. | Nothing to change. C5 keeps it true across pin bumps. |
| The call cipher differs from the one the rest of Mjolnir uses | True. Sites, blob-store wraps and Recrypt use XChaCha20-Poly1305 (`lib/mjolnir/sites/crypto.ex`; `docs/encryption-and-security.md:253`; `openspec/specs/blob-store/spec.md:511`). Upstream chose AES-128-GCM because it runs on aws-lc-rs, graviola and WebCrypto alike (`quest/m5/e2ee/README.md:51`). XChaCha20-Poly1305 is not in the [Web Cryptography standard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WebCrypto/), so a browser cannot hold a non-extractable key for it. Plain ChaCha20-Poly1305 is in a newer proposal that Chromium is implementing ([blink-dev intent](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/KluNhawvzgM)); I found no sign of it in Safari. | Keep AES-128-GCM for frames on both ends. Changing it would fork the profile, lose the vectors and lose non-extractable keys. Keep XChaCha20-Poly1305 where it is, and for wrapping or distributing the room credential later. The two layers do not need the same cipher, which is the rule the house document already states for LUKS and Recrypt (`docs/encryption-and-security.md:261`). AES-GCM breaks badly on a repeated nonce where XChaCha with random nonces is forgiving, which is why the per-instance epoch and the snapshot rule matter. |
| 128-bit keys where 256 were expected | The profile derives 128-bit keys from a 256-bit secret. | A profile matter. Raise it upstream if wanted. Do not fork for it. |

Non-extractable keys do not require a different cipher in any of the three readings.

## Epoch in the path

The ASK stays open. This is a recommendation, not an approval.

What I verified:

- The default wire does lose the instance. `Versions::all()` excludes lite-07 and the ALPN is never offered by default (`rs/moq-net/src/version.rs:26-28`, `:271-281`). A session mints its own instance on older wires and `Epoch::of` filters minted ones (`rs/moq-net/src/instance.rs:57-64`; `rs/moq-e2ee/src/epoch.rs:37-45`). Both transcripts show it.
- iroh-live at `747b1bd` routes with `RouteOptions::hidden()` and no instance (`iroh-live-node/src/publish.rs:379-382`), and `publish_with` gives the caller the broadcast and the path (`iroh-live-node/src/node.rs:428-440`).
- The draft allows for this. On a wire that cannot carry the instance, "the application supplies the epoch over its own authenticated channel" (`drafts/draft-lcurley-moq-e2ee.md:145`), and the epoch and path "are not secret and are not authenticated" (`:147-149`). A wrong epoch fails to open.

My reading of the options:

- **A, lite-07 on the relay and every client.** lite-07 is a work-in-progress wire with breaking changes in the week after the pin (upstream #9467 on 2026-11-23). It would tie relay deploys to client pins and still leave iroh-live without an instance option. Costlier than B on every axis today.
- **B, last path segment.** Works on both transports now, leaves the relay untouched, and exposes nothing A does not. It is closer to the draft than the contract says: the path is the application's channel for the epoch under the draft's own fallback. The draft asks for an authenticated channel there and the path is not one, but the draft also says the epoch is unauthenticated and that a wrong one fails to open, so nothing rests on that. What is non-conformant is only that the broadcast is not at a bare `<opaque>`.
- **C, application channel.** There is none for guests.

Recommendation: B, for the `moq-e2ee-01-wip` profile only, with two edits. First, tie the layout to the profile named in the link and say it is decided again at the `-01` bump. That bump re-derives every key, name and path anyway, so revisiting the layout then costs nothing extra, and by then the instance replaces the epoch upstream. Second, replace "read the greatest epoch" with a rule that does not depend on ordering, such as "read an active instance, prefer the most recently announced, move when it ends", or scope the current rule to this profile in so many words. The greatest-epoch rule is also what let the probe's re-mount win.

## Scope against the original goal

The original question was whether Mjolnir is a good home for real-time media over QUIC, MoQ and Iroh, with peers contributing bandwidth, as against bandwidth-heavy central SFUs.

Plainly: **relay fan-out is central bandwidth.** Every subscriber's bytes leave the operator's relay. For N participants each sending bitrate b and all watching all, relay egress is N·(N−1)·b: 84 Mbit/s for eight people at 1.5 Mbit/s. A cap of K videos per client makes it N·K·b. This is the same shape as an SFU. What MoQ buys is elsewhere: no per-subscriber packet rewriting or feedback loops in the relay, drop decisions by group and priority that work on ciphertext, one UDP port with no ICE or TURN, the same model for native and browser, and relays that can be chained.

Peer contribution is not in this scope and cannot be for browser participants. A browser cannot accept an inbound session without WebRTC, which is a non-goal. Native iroh-live nodes could serve each other.

What this change does for that goal is real, though. Content encryption at the object layer is the precondition for letting anything untrusted carry media, whether a peer, a second operator or a rented CDN. After this change the relay is a commodity that needs no trust. The record should say that, and should say that the bandwidth saving itself is not delivered.

Two gaps between the accepted scope and the stated journey ("a person opens a link … and is in a multi-party call"), both outside this change and both made concrete by D3:

- The guest model: one hour, owner-minted, one label per link (B3a).
- Removal for link guests (B3d).

Service placement is confirmed by this design. With the relay blind to content, a host process beside the gateway is enough, and nothing in the controller, the gateway or a guest needs the credential.

On the exit test: the content key is made by the user and never held by Mjolnir, and nothing is stored. What a user pays for is relay bandwidth. That passes for native clients. For browsers it passes against the relay, with the page's origin as the accepted exception.

## Recommendation: the smallest coherent route

1. **Amend the contract** (B1–B6 and the evidence labels). About a page of edits plus moving the probe into the spikes. Then a fresh advise.
2. **Start the proofs that do not depend on the profile:** the capture instrument with its plaintext positive control (A1), and the Hang components through a pass-through local-origin bridge built with the upstream Vite plugins.
3. **Build the adapter on `01-wip` as a development target,** with the label in the context. Run rekey and Rust-to-browser through the relay in the D2 layout.
4. **Decide the default-on profile** when step 3 is green: ship on `01-wip` with B1's fix, or cut over to `moq-e2ee-01` first if it has landed. Cutting over is a client upgrade.
5. **Open the guest-model work** (token life, delegated invite) under `mjolnir-rurh.5` or a new change.

Alternatives considered:

- *Wait for `-01` before any code.* Safest for the format. Blocks the browser client on an upstream date nobody can see. Steps 2 and 3 are worth doing either way.
- *Ship the browser client in plaintext first.* Duke put encryption in scope and made the client depend on it. Step 2 gets the same de-risking without a plaintext product.
- *MLS now.* Real membership and removal. No implementation at the pin, and it needs a delivery service. The credential seam keeps it open.
