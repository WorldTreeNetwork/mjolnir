## Verdict in one screen

All eight required changes from the second read are in the contract. Six are resolved outright. Two (removal and group order) are resolved in the case the second read described and leave a bounded gap beside it. No architecture is reopened, and B1 to B6 were not re-read.

The diff did add new defects, as the two earlier revisions did. None of them is of the kind that sent the contract back before: none locks out an honest person in ordinary use, and none makes a requirement unbuildable. They are places where the new rules meet the pinned transport, which the contract could not have settled without reading that source:

1. At the pin, one frame that fails to authenticate ends the whole track subscription. The contract does not say what the reader does next.
2. At the pin, audio is one 20 ms frame per group. A reorder allowance of zero therefore drops every audio frame that arrives after a later one.
3. On the default wire (moq-lite-06) a track with no timescale is reported by the relay with timescale 1000. The new rule "a mismatch rejects the track" would refuse such a track in an honest call.
4. Read-only retention ends on an event the relay controls and has no time limit, and the removed-label list is scoped to one removal cycle.
5. A candidate that is only slow is barred for the rest of the minute, the same as one that failed authentication.

I accept because the remaining risk is in behaviour that has never run, and a fourth read of text cannot lower it. Running it can. Every new policy is labelled unrun where it is stated, and `tasks.md` has a box that runs each one.

## 1. The reader instance rule, walked

| Case | What the rule does | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Relay announces a forged greater UUIDv7 under the label's public opaque | Tried first. No frame opens, or 1500 ms pass. Skipped. The mark does not move. The real candidate is tried next. An accepted stream keeps playing meanwhile. | Correct. The second read's N1 is fixed. |
| Relay re-serves ciphertext of a real older instance | It authenticates. A reader whose mark is above it does not render it. A fresh reader has no mark and accepts it as live. | No rollback for a reader in the call. Replay to a fresh reader is a stated limit (`design.md:48`). See the next row for the one case where an older instance is also the greater one. |
| A guest on a second device, then back on the first | The first device sees the second authenticate and stops with "you joined somewhere else". On resume it mints a new, greater generation, which readers accept. | Correct on sane clocks. Two people sharing a forwarded link will stop each other in turn, which is visible and matches D3's limit 3. |
| A reload | Mark and credential are gone. The page asks for the invite. On re-entry it is a new process with a new, greater epoch. | Correct. |
| A publisher with a fast clock | Its epoch stays above every correct-clock epoch until real time passes it. Readers that accepted it show "newer stream expected, older one present" for the honest lower stream. | Stated at `design.md:46`. **One consequence is not stated.** A relay that recorded the fast-clock instance can replay it. It authenticates, so it marks every reader, including fresh ones, and it makes the publisher's own page stop with "you joined somewhere else" each time the user resumes, because each new mint is still lower. The hold lasts until real time passes the bad stamp. This needs a fast-clock instance to have existed, so it is a wider form of the stated limit and not a new attack on a healthy call. Condition 1d. |
| Relay withholds the newest instance | The reader cannot know. It keeps what it has. | Stated limit. Nothing in a shared-secret design fixes it. |

**The bound of 12 candidates per label per minute.** A relay can spend it: twelve forged announcements above the real epoch, each left to time out, take 18 seconds and use the minute's budget before the real candidate is reached. Repeated each minute, a fresh reader never reaches the real stream. I checked whether this is cheaper than what the relay can already do, and it is not: an on-path relay can simply not deliver the real stream. Two properties matter and both hold. No state outlasts the forgery, because the reader recovers within 60 seconds of the relay stopping. And nobody but the relay or the label's own holder can announce under a label, because publish grants are per label. The design says the right thing: "These bounds limit work, not an active relay's power to deny service" (`design.md:42`).

**The per-key failed-open budget (first review, B2).** It is not the binding limit. A forged epoch derives its own key, so probing it costs the real instance nothing. Inside an accepted instance one failed open ends the track, so each subscription can spend at most one. At any sane resubscribe bound the 2^24 budget lasts years.

What is binding is the terminal failure, and that is the first gap:

- **An authentication failure inside an accepted instance has no stated outcome.** The spec says the reader "SHALL skip authentication failures" in the sentence about candidates, and forbids "retrying a failed epoch within that window". At the pin, one bad frame ends that track's subscription. A builder can read the text three ways: the instance is still accepted and the track is resubscribed; the epoch has now "failed" and is barred for a minute; or the track stays dead. The scenario at `spec.md:121-125` passes under all three, because it only asserts that the mark did not move. Condition 1c.
- **A slow candidate is treated like a forged one.** "candidates with no authentic frame within 1500 ms" are skipped and not retried "within that window". On a poor mobile link the first frame can take longer than 1500 ms: the subscription travels reader to relay to publisher and back. One slow start then costs up to a minute of "unavailable" in an honest one-to-one call. A timeout proves nothing about the candidate. An authentication failure does. Condition 1a.
- **The rule does not name the frame.** The first track a reader can subscribe to is `bridge.tracks`. The spec should say that the first authentic manifest frame accepts the instance and that the 1500 ms run from that subscription. Condition 1b.

## 2. Removal

**Does retention now end in a way that stops rendering the removed member?** For one removal, yes. `removedLabels` arrives with the credential and the reader stops those labels at once, "even if her old broadcast never ends" (`spec.md:85`).

Two gaps remain beside that case.

- **The list is scoped to one cycle.** `design.md:93` says the list is "cumulative for the removal cycle". The spec says only "a canonical removedLabels list". Take a reader that missed the second credential and adopts the third. It still holds the first. The third list, if it covers only the third cycle, does not name the member removed in the second. That member never appears under the new kid, so the reader keeps rendering their old broadcast until their token expires. That is the second read's N2 again, in the narrower case of two removals and one missed hand-out.
- **Retention ends only on an event the relay controls, and has no time limit.** It ends when the label "has an accepted/authenticated instance under the new kid". If that publisher has left the call, it never ends. If the relay hides the new instance, it never ends. For as long as it lasts, the removed member still holds the old secret, and with the relay's help can forge that label's old-key media to the retaining reader. The contract accepts that a holder colluding with the relay can impersonate (`design.md:36`). After removal the member is meant to stop being a holder, so this limit should not continue without a bound.

**Is completion defined where it can be observed?** Yes, at the owner's client, from authenticated instances and not from announcements. The reload case is handled ("completion SHALL be unknown"). One thing to add: a recipient who is not publishing produces no instance, so they stay pending for good. That is honest, and the page should not read it as a failure.

**Is retirement asserted on the publisher?** Yes: "instrumentation at alice observes no subsequent old-generation seal calls" (`spec.md:91`). The reader's tail is correctly kept as an observation.

**One format gap.** `removedLabels` travels "alongside" the credential, but D3 pins the invite fields as exactly `v`, `p`, `kid`, `k`. The field that carries the list has no name and no size limit. I1 will have to invent it.

## 3. Strict group order against the pinned transport

The rule is compatible with how moq delivers groups at the pin. It cannot stall playback from ordering alone: a group that starts later always advances the mark, so there is always forward progress. A late joiner is served newest first, takes the newest group from its first frame, and refuses the backlog, which is what a live reader wants. A group skipped under congestion is a jump forward, which the rule allows. Video groups are 2 seconds, so the discarded tail at a group boundary is a few frames at most.

It does drop legitimate media in one common situation, and the contract does not price it.

- **Audio under loss.** Audio is one 20 ms frame per group at the pin, and each group is its own stream. When a packet is lost, that group waits for a retransmission while the next one arrives. With zero allowance the late group is refused. So every lost audio packet becomes a gap that the codec must conceal, and the player's jitter buffer, which exists to wait for exactly that group, never gets it. This is roughly what a call without audio retransmission sounds like, so it is a tolerable choice. It should be a measured one. `design.md:120` says "live calls prefer forward progress over late-group playback" without the 20 ms fact, and no task runs the rule with loss or reordering. The second read asked for "a small reorder allowance"; the author chose zero. A window that remembers which starts were opened, refuses those, and refuses anything older than a short span behind the mark would keep the replay protection and the jitter buffer. Decide from a run. Condition 3a.
- **A group offered again after a resubscription.** The rule refuses "newly offered groups at or below the mark". After the reader resubscribes a track, the relay serves the newest group again, and that group is the mark. For video the reader then waits up to 2 seconds for the next group. For a track that is one long-lived group, such as the catalog, nothing newer may ever come. If the implementation rebuilds its local mirror on resubscription, the catalog is gone and the mark refuses to read it again. That is a stall. The transport can resume a group above the frame already read, and the frame index is in the nonce, so it is authenticated. The mark should be the pair of group start and last authenticated frame, and the mirrored state should live exactly as long as the mark. Whether an honest reconnect reaches the bridge as a new offer is unverified. The resubscription in condition 1c certainly does. Condition 3b.

## 4. Manifest rules

**Do they break a legitimate call?** The bounds do not. A participant has a handful of tracks, each entry is under 100 bytes, and every name in the logs is 12 bytes or fewer. 32 entries and 16 KiB are generous.

One rule can break an honest call:

- **"A mismatch with relay-supplied TRACK_INFO SHALL reject the affected track"** (`spec.md:60`). The second read asked which value wins. The author added a rejection. On the default wire a track with no timescale is reported with timescale 1000, because lite-05/06 cannot encode "none". The manifest schema allows `timescale: null`. Such a track would be rejected in every honest call on moq-lite-06. No track in today's logs is scale-less, so nothing breaks today. The rejection also buys nothing: a relay that wants the track gone can drop it. What matters is that relay values are never substituted, and the sentence before it already says so. Nobody has compared the two values: the browser reader used the manifest, the native reader used TRACK_INFO. Condition 4a.

**Can a member or the relay mislead a reader?** A member writes only its own manifest, under its own label context, within the bounds. The relay cannot roll a reader in the call back to an older track set. It can freeze the track set by withholding newer snapshots, and it can show a fresh reader an old one. Both are stated (`design.md:84`: "It cannot establish freshness for a newly loaded page").

Two notes for the builder, neither a condition. Refusing duplicate JSON keys needs a scanning parser in both languages, because `JSON.parse` and serde both keep the last one silently. And the harness numbers manifest groups from `Time.Timestamp.now()` (`bridge.ts:76`, `:86`). The pinned producer refuses a start below the last one, so if that clock can step backwards the manifest goes silent until it catches up. I did not verify which clock it is. A per-generation counter avoids the question.

## Conditions

To be written into the change before any product code. Each names the place.

1. **Instance rule: timeouts, the frame, a failure inside an accepted instance, and the replay limit.** In the identity requirement of `spec.md`, D2 and ADR item 2:
   a. The no-retry rule applies to a candidate whose frame failed authentication. A candidate that only timed out is retried after a backoff that starts at 3 seconds and doubles, and each retry counts in the budget of 12.
   b. The first authentic `bridge.tracks` frame accepts an instance. The 1500 ms run from the subscription to that track.
   c. An authentication failure on a track of an accepted instance does not un-accept the instance and is not a candidate failure. At the pin it ends that track's subscription. The reader resubscribes the track on the same consuming generation and keeps the track's group mark, at most 6 times per track per 60 seconds, then reports the track unavailable and retryable. Add the outcome to the scenario at `spec.md:121`.
   d. In D2's clock limit and D6's table: a relay holding a recording of an instance with a greater epoch can replay it to any reader and can make the publisher's page stop again after each resume, until real time passes that epoch's stamp. Record as a candidate for the successor profile: refuse a candidate epoch whose time is more than a stated margin ahead of the reader's clock.
2. **Removal: the list's scope, a limit on retention, the unobservable recipient, the field.** In the removal requirement, D5, D3 and ADR item 5:
   a. `removedLabels` holds every label removed since CreateCredential, not only those of the current cycle.
   b. Retention for a label also ends after a stated maximum from adoption. I suggest 10 minutes, since hand delivery takes minutes. After it the label shows as waiting for the new key. State the limit beside it: old-key media read during retention can be forged by the removed member with the relay's help.
   c. A recipient who is not publishing cannot be observed and stays pending.
   d. Name the invite field that carries the list and give it a size limit (I suggest the manifest's 32 entries).
3. **Group order: the cost for audio, and the group equal to the mark.** In the group-order requirement, D6 and the ADR:
   a. Record that audio is one 20 ms frame per group at the pin, so zero allowance drops each audio frame that arrives after a later one. Keep zero for the manifest and the catalog. For media, decide between zero and a short window of remembered starts from the run in condition 6.
   b. The mark is the group start and the last authenticated frame index. A group equal to the mark that is offered again continues strictly above that index. Nothing at or below it reopens. The local mirror of a track lives as long as its mark, and a resubscription resets neither.
4. **Manifest: relay metadata is never used, and never grounds for rejection on its own.** In `spec.md:60`, the scenario at `:68`, D4 and ADR item 4: replace "A mismatch with relay-supplied TRACK_INFO SHALL reject the affected track with a visible metadata error" with "Relay-supplied TRACK_INFO SHALL NOT be substituted for manifest values; a difference SHALL be reported." Record the reason: lite-05/06 reports a scale-less track with timescale 1000.
5. **States, not sentences.** In `spec.md`, name the states the adapter exposes (superseded by another instance, older stream only, unavailable and retryable, metadata difference, invite needed, close the old tab) and say the page must surface them. Keep the quoted wording in the design as suggested copy for `mjolnir-rurh.5`.

## Decisions that are Duke's, in the order he must answer

Building cannot start until 1 and 2 are answered. 3 is needed before C3 and costs nothing to answer now. 4, 5 and 6 can wait until the browser client.

1. **Which profile to build on, and which ships on by default.** Recommended: build on `moq-e2ee-01-wip` now and decide default-on when the browser client is ready. If finished `-01` has landed and passes the vectors by then, cut over before launch. Cost: the adapter, bridges, manifest and reader rules are written once and replaced once.
2. **Epoch in the path for `01-wip`.** Recommended: approve for this profile only. Cost: the layout is decided again at the profile bump.
3. **One secret per call, or a standing room link.** Recommended: one per call. Cost: no bookmarkable room link. Know also that the owner's copy lives only in the open page, so an owner who reloads without a saved invite must start a new credential and send new links.
4. **How the invite secret is delivered.** Can wait. Recommended: a navigated link, with the history limit disclosed in the page, and paste into an open page built as a second entry. Cost: the key and token stay in each guest's browser history and Chromium session files. The key is useful only to someone who also holds that call's ciphertext. The author recommends paste as the only entry. Its cost is that joining is no longer one click and the flow is unrun (I1).
5. **How remaining link guests get a replacement credential.** Can wait. Recommended: paste a replacement invite into the open page, with rejoin as the fallback. Cost: I1 must pass first. Until it does, every remaining guest rejoins and must close the old tab.
6. **Guest lifetime beyond one token.** Can wait. Recommended: calls end at token expiry with a visible countdown. Cost: one hour per call.

Already answered: the origin that serves the page's code is trusted for this iteration (Duke, 2026-11-23).
