## Independent take — written before opening design, tasks, or delta

These ten lines were persisted in this file before opening those documents. The supplied packet disclosed the intended revisions, so this is a source-order blind pass, not ignorance of the packet summary. Sources were proposal Why, the living web-pty-edge spec, cited code, and the cited learning.

1. Pin the stable cryptographic edge identity; address discovery must never replace that trust anchor.
2. Require an explicit, verifiable delegation from the stable identity to each operational signing key.
3. Ordinary sessions and restarts must work without loading the stable private key or contacting an identity service.
4. Refuse silent identity replacement when persisted keys are missing, malformed, or only partially present.
5. Keep every private key and temporary copy outside the resolved BTRFS data tree, with restrictive permissions from creation.
6. Define rotation as a durable transition with bounded overlap, separate issuance and verification rules, and unambiguous key identifiers.
7. Refuse rollback that resurrects retired keys; backup restoration must preserve revocation knowledge or require explicit recovery authority.
8. Bind signatures to purpose, edge identity, key, and validity interval so proof and capability authority cannot be substituted.
9. Preserve hosted /term authentication and existing credential precedence; this key-storage node must not silently widen API access.
10. Accept the availability cost of operator-assisted renewal or recovery in exchange for keeping the stable trust anchor offline.

## Comparison and steelman

Decisions 1, 3, 5, and 9 answer the identity/delegation concerns: the pin is checked against the inception public key, discovery supplies only an address, and a signed operational grant binds key, purpose, edge, and lifetime. Decision 6 separates initial generation from activation and supports restart without the stable private. Decisions 8–9 cover private-file permissions at creation and open, path aliases, atomic activation, and preservation of durable revocations. This uses the host-secret separation in `lib/mjolnir/secret_escrow.ex:16–30` as precedent; that module's write-then-chmod implementation is not sufficient implementation evidence for the new permission-at-creation contract.

The existing API still has sites-token, loopback, and JWT paths (`lib/mjolnir/api/auth.ex:5–24`), and application startup still has the conditional synchronous JWKS child (`lib/mjolnir/application.ex:130–137`). This review does not infer that offline JWT availability has already been implemented. Hosted `/term` remains governed by `openspec/specs/web-pty-edge/spec.md:113–124`; its public XID subject and lack of a device-code replacement remain separate from edge identity.

The strongest objection from my take is that a fresh-looking signed restore record cannot itself prove that an operator possesses the latest revocation history. Decision 9 now explicitly chooses a trusted operator ceremony with independently retained evidence, rather than claiming that a signature or unique ID proves completeness. That is sufficient for the declared-restore model requested in advise-4. The operator must establish current revocations, merge them into the output, and freshly authorize that output; inability to do so fails closed. A replayed A1+B1 is expressly excluded. This acceptance does not claim automatic detection of an undeclared rollback of all host state, or protection against a dishonest recovery authority.

Similarly, immediate revocation at a disconnected verifier is not promised. A stale verifier can accept a stolen operational key until its delegation expires. This is an explicit bounded exposure, with numeric lifetimes deferred. Requiring an online revocation oracle, hardware rollback counter, or independent root-recovery committee would add scope beyond the stated architecture. Operator involvement in renewal/recovery and a new pin after stable-private compromise are coherent tradeoffs.

## Residuals from advise-4

**R4e — resolved.** `design.md:128–147` identifies the fresh ceremony, unique restore instance, signed output coverage, independent current revocation evidence, mandatory merge, and fail-closed outcome. The delta tests old-attestation replay, missing current evidence, and interrupted activation after durable revocation (`specs/edge-auth/spec.md:114–145`). These are the missing cases from advise-4. The attestation authenticates reconciled public authority state; matching private material remains required by the activation-bundle contract at delta lines 51–52.

**R3d — resolved.** `design.md:110–126` makes `op-supersede` the sole encoding, defines `exp_now` as effective time rather than record expiry, requires late receipt to take effect, retains negative evidence through the original grant's remaining lifetime, and requires a separate delegation for `next_kid`. It also retains every unexpired, unrevoked public grant across repeated rotations. The delta now tests target tampering, late receipt, restart/replay, K1→K2→K3 overlap, and permission-at-open failure (lines 147–179). Decision 8 and delta lines 52–55 supply the permission rule itself.

Decision 7 still uses the older shorthand “new delegation that supersedes” and “exp=now,” and its backup sentence omits the ceremony. Read together, Decision 9's explicit **only** encoding and mandatory fresh-restore procedure settle these ambiguities: expiry is not successor authorization, a backup is not live authority, and supersession is not a positive delegation. This is editorial residue, not a surviving alternative authorization path. The already-planned ADR should express this single reconciled contract; the signed tuples are specified in Decision 9 even though the delta mainly captures their observable effects.
