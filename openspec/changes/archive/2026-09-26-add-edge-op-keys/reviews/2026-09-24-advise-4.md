3. Require operational-key authority to trace to that pinned identity.
4. Keep ordinary session signing independent of the stable private key.

## Comparison and steelman

The design answers most of the take. Decisions 1–3 pin identity independently of discovery. Decisions 5–6 distinguish delegation from generation and support root-offline restart. Decision 8 places private and temporary files outside BTRFS, specifies creation permissions, and makes activation atomic. These fit the existing host-secret separation in `lib/mjolnir/secret_escrow.ex:16–30`; the implementation there is precedent, not evidence that the new key store exists. `lib/mjolnir/application.ex:130–137` and `lib/mjolnir/api/auth.ex:5–24` still describe the existing JWT/credential path. Hosted `/term` remains governed by `openspec/specs/web-pty-edge/spec.md:113–124` and its public-XID requirement.

Decision 9 now makes three meaningful improvements: a restored prefix is explicitly reconciliation input, the supersession tuple authenticates its target under a distinct operation name, and stable-private compromise requires a new identity and pin. The latter is also captured in the delta's stolen-root scenario; R4d is resolved. The text correctly discloses that clients retaining the stolen pin remain compromised.

The strongest reading for acceptance is that an operator-held attestation implies a fresh recovery ceremony, and `op-supersede` implies a permanent revocation event rather than an expiring positive grant. Those are plausible intended meanings. The packet also explicitly says observed revocations survive. I accept operator-mediated recovery, bounded stale-verifier exposure, deferred numeric lifetimes, and deferred hardware custody. No online revocation service, committee, or transparent whole-disk rollback detector is required.

However, neither implied meaning is actually specified sufficiently to decide the cases below. These are the same recovery and supersession boundaries requested in advise-3, not additional product scope. Marking R3c/R4c checked also overstates the delta: it adds only the stale-backup-without-attestation and stolen-root cases, not the requested late-receipt, replay, repeated-rotation, interrupted-activation, or permission-at-open cases.

## Required amendments

### R4e — Authenticate current recovery authorization, not merely an old bundle

Evidence: `design.md:113–119`, `:127–128`; delta `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:112–117`.

The tuple `["identikey-mjolnir/v1", "auth-restore", bundle_hash]` authenticates the chosen bundle. It contains no recovery-instance or ordering information, and the text does not require a newly authorized ceremony or an independently retained current record. "Operator-held" describes custody, not freshness. The alternative "newer-than-observed supersession log" likewise has no defined authenticated ordering or required merge rule. The current supersession tuple has a target, time, and successor, but does not itself define a complete ordered log.

Concrete case: B1 and a valid attestation A1 were retained while K1 was current. K1 was subsequently superseded by K2. Following loss of the active auth directory, B1+A1 still pass the stated signature check. The new prohibition on stale-prefix activation says this must fail, but the recovery procedure supplies no input or operator step that distinguishes this pair from an authorized current restore. Even with independently retained K1 revocation evidence, the delta only tests absence of an attestation; it does not say that a valid old attestation cannot override that evidence.

Define the supported declared-restore procedure and its source of current authority. A concrete operator reconciliation ceremony is sufficient: specify what the operator must establish before authorizing the reconciled output, what state/evidence must remain outside the restored bundle, and what happens when it is unavailable. Do not treat a previously signed backup hash as a reusable override of learned revocations. If keeping the log alternative, define what authenticates its ordering/completeness and how it incorporates every retained revocation. Define exactly what `bundle_hash` covers, including the revocation/current-key metadata being authorized. No particular hash algorithm or storage layout is demanded by this review.

Carry this into the delta with B1+A1 replay after K1 retirement, missing current recovery evidence, and interrupted activation after a revocation was durably learned. Reconcile Decision 7's backup guidance with this procedure: waiting for expiry does not authorize a successor, and a usable backup must contain the required independently authorized successor/recovery material. A stale prefix can remain reconciliation input, never the activated result that revives K1.

### R3d — Finish supersession time/effect rules and synchronize the normative delta

Evidence: `design.md:71–83`, `:103–111`, `:130–134`; delta `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:84–91`.

The explicit dCBOR `op-supersede` tuple resolves the unsigned-target ambiguity. The final paragraph also improves repeated-rotation retention to cover any unexpired kid. But the earlier paragraph still says "same domain" with `supersedes=kid`, and retains material for "superseded kids only." More materially, the new tuple calls its time `exp_now` without defining whether it is an effective revocation time or record expiry, whether a record first received after that time must still be honored, or the retention rule for the revocation record itself. The earlier positive-delegation validity rule cannot supply those semantics.

Concrete case: K1's grant expires at T. Its valid supersession names time R < T, and a verifier first receives it at R+1. The required result is rejection of K1 through the remaining grant lifetime, including after restart and replay of the old grant. Explicitly say that the revocation record is not discarded as expired at R+1; define effect, time units/boundaries, and how long the negative evidence remains authoritative. Say whether `next_kid` only names a separately delegated successor rather than authorizing it. Reconcile the two descriptions into one contract and define or precisely reference the canonical field representations needed by independent signers/verifiers.

Put the signed representation and validation/effect rules in the delta or an exact normative reference. Add the already-requested modified-target, late-receipt, restart/old-grant replay, and K1→K2→K3 graceful-overlap scenarios. The latter must retain unexpired, unrevoked K1 verification material even after two rotations. Also restore the outstanding permission-at-open failure contract/scenario requested by R4c: the current text specifies creation permissions only. These cases make the amendment testable without implementing product code in this review.
