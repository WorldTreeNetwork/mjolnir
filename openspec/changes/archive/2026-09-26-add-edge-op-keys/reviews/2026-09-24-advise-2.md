## Blind take

Written before opening design.md, tasks.md, or the edge-auth delta; inputs were proposal Why, the living web-pty-edge spec, cited code, and the cited learning.

1. Pin the stable edge XID independently of DNS and operational key fingerprints.
2. Require a verifiable delegation from that identity to each operational key.
3. Bind delegation to the key, permitted uses, edge identity, and validity interval.
4. Keep ordinary proof and capability signing independent of the offline private anchor.
5. Distinguish generating a replacement key from authorizing it to serve.
6. Refuse silent identity replacement after missing, corrupt, or restored state.
7. Keep secrets, temporary files, and backups outside the snapshotted data volume.
8. Make rotation overlap finite and separate routine retirement from compromise revocation.
9. Preserve hosted `/term`, existing credential scopes, and the separate endpoint work.
10. Accept bounded offline availability as a tradeoff, provided expiry and recovery fail closed.

## Comparison and steelman

Decisions 1–3 and the no-pin scenario preserve the selected trust model: a stable XID, explicit provisioning, and discovery supplying addresses only. Decision 5 now binds the operational public key, kid, purposes, validity, and prohibition on onward delegation. Decision 6 distinguishes generation from activation and allows root-offline restart with a valid bundle. The corresponding delta scenarios cover restart and failure with missing state and no root. Those changes answer the central R2 concern without requiring unattended root signing.

Decision 7 correctly states that issuing K2 does not invalidate K1, and that a stale verifier can accept freshly forged K1 capabilities until K1's delegation expires. This is the right exposure bound; capability lifetime alone cannot provide it. Decision 8 adds private permissions at creation, path-alias rejection, and atomic activation of matching material. These are substantial answers to the blind concerns, not reasons to replace the design.

The strongest case for acceptance is that encoding, revocation storage, and recovery procedure could be filled in during act. I agree that implementation mechanics, numeric lifetimes, hardware custody, and HTTP endpoints can stay outside this review. Sharing an operational key across two explicitly separated signing domains is also a reasonable v1 choice. However, authenticating the root public key, defining the effect of a supersession, and deciding which restored authority is trustworthy are observable security contracts owned by this node. They cannot safely be inferred independently by sibling consumers. The amendments below complete that design rather than introduce another one.

## Required amendments

### R1b — Authenticate the identity public key against the pin

Evidence: `design.md:46–56` names a 64-hex XID, Ed25519, a supplied identity public key, and delegation bytes. It checks an asserted edge XID but never specifies how the supplied identity public key is proven to belong to that XID. The delta at `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:43–46` similarly relies on “the pinned XID’s identity key” without defining that binding. The parenthetical `identikey-auth v1` is not a precise reference to an XID derivation or identity-history verification contract.

**Failure to exclude:** a verifier compares a delegation's edge-XID string to the pin, then validates its signature with the identity public key supplied alongside it. An attacker can create a different identity key and sign a delegation carrying that same XID string. Signature validity alone does not establish the claimed relationship. This is an underspecified verifier contract, not a claim that such code has already been implemented.

Specify the authenticated XID-to-identity-public-key check, including what public inception/history material is required if applicable. Pin an exact existing contract or define the signed delegation representation and its domain/version so implementations agree on the bytes and authority being verified. Add a negative scenario using an attacker-owned identity public key with the correct victim XID string, plus wrong-edge and delegation validity-boundary scenarios. Checking hexadecimal shape or repeating the XID inside signed data is insufficient.

### R3b — Define supersession as verifiable, durable revocation state

Evidence: `design.md:74–83` calls retirement a new stable-key-signed delegation that supersedes K1, then proposes a supersession with `exp=now` for K1. Decision 5's signed field set has no superseded-key reference or record type. It also rejects delegations outside their validity interval (`:50–54`). The delta at `:84–91` promises immediate rejection by a verifier holding supersession without defining what it verifies or retains.

**Failure to exclude:** a verifier processes the proposed `exp=now` object as an ordinary delegation and discards it as expired. The original still-valid K1 delegation remains acceptable. Alternatively, a verifier temporarily applies it but forgets it after restart or when older delegation material is replayed. Reissuing K1 with a shorter expiry does not cryptographically invalidate its earlier signed delegation.

Define the authenticated supersession/revocation object's target and authority, its effective time, how its validity differs from a positive delegation, and how learned revocation persists and takes precedence over replayed older material. Explicitly state what an informed verifier rejects and what a never-informed offline verifier may continue accepting until expiry. No requirement for instant global revocation or online checks is implied.

Also complete the overlap contract for repeated rotations: retain K1 public verification evidence as required even after K2→K3, separately from retiring private signing material. Reconcile Decision 4 and the unconditional unexpired-capability scenario (`delta:21–26`) with Decision 7's delegation-expiry bound: either constrain capability expiry to the issuing delegation or explicitly promise acceptance only while both remain valid and unrevoked. Add cases for repeated rotations, expiry, and replay after learned supersession. Numeric defaults may remain deferred.

### R4b — Make stale restore refusal achievable under an explicit recovery model

Evidence: `design.md:91–94` defines backup as a copy of the auth directory and promises restore will not revive a superseded kid. It identifies no authoritative revocation state outside an old copy and no required operator reconciliation before activation. Decision 7 (`:80–83`) suggests restoring a backup that already contains supersession when the stable private is unavailable; a backup taken before an unanticipated compromise cannot contain its future revocation. The delta has no stale-restore or interrupted-rotation scenario.

**Failure to exclude:** backup B contains valid K1, K1 is later superseded by K2, and the host loses its newer auth directory. Restoring B restores the old current-kid and loses the supersession. Checking only B cannot distinguish this from a legitimate pre-rotation restart. “Must not revive” states the desired result without a recovery input that can enforce it.

Define the supported restore procedure and the authority used to reconcile a backup with later revocations, or explicitly require an operator recovery step and keep direct auth disabled until that evidence is available. The recovery model may distinguish ordinary crash recovery from declared backup restoration; it need not promise hardware rollback resistance. State what happens when no current revocation evidence or stable signer is available. Waiting for K1 expiry can stop its authority, but cannot authorize a replacement key or manufacture a missing supersession.

Cover root-private compromise separately from root loss: a stolen anchor can authorize successors, so preserving the old pin needs an authenticated recovery mechanism, or the procedure must require a new identity/pin. Host compromise exposing online op keys is already acknowledged; that is not the same case. Add stale-backup, interrupted-activation, and failed private-permission scenarios to the delta so the stated guarantees are testable.
