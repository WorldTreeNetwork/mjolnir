## Blind pass, recorded before design/tasks/delta

The following ten lines were persisted first in `notes/2026-09-24-blind-take-astra.md`. Inputs were the packet's Why/What, the living `web-pty-edge` spec, cited escrow/application/auth code, and the cited September 20 learning. They are concerns for the design to answer, not a competing design.

1. Pin the selected edge's stable public XID through explicit trusted provisioning; DNS and discovery may supply addresses only.
2. Require a verifiable authorization chain from that stable identity to each operational public key, with purpose, validity, and edge binding.
3. Keep stable private material out of ordinary session handling; explain how fresh operational keys gain authorization while the anchor is offline.
4. Persist operational keys across restarts; distinguish first provisioning from missing, corrupt, or rolled-back established state and refuse silent identity replacement.
5. Enforce private files at 0600 and their directory at 0700 outside the actual BTRFS data tree, including temporary files and configured path aliases.
6. Make key publication and activation crash-consistent so a restart cannot advertise one key while signing with another.
7. Define rotation overlap against capability lifetime, proof validity, clock skew, and verifier cache lifetime, including behavior after retirement.
8. Bound compromise recovery: retiring a signing key must eventually stop its authority, and a stolen operational key must not authorize arbitrary successors.
9. Preserve sites-token scope, localhost handling, JWT verification, and hosted /term authentication; the edge key owner must not silently introduce a new auth lane.
10. Accept the tradeoff of host-file operational keys for autonomous service, while requiring explicit root custody/recovery and acknowledging that host compromise exposes online authority.

## Comparison and steelman

Design decisions 1–3 and the delta's no-pin scenario answer the trust-selection concern well. Refusing TOFU is explicit. Separating identity from operational authority is the right basis for preserving the pin through rotation. The storage location honors the user decision and escrow precedent. The proposal keeps endpoints, Biscuit runtime, and login flags outside this node; those need not be implemented or redesigned here. Hosted `/term` remains governed by `openspec/specs/web-pty-edge/spec.md:113`, including public XID subjects; a pairwise OIDC subject is not a substitute edge identity.

The strongest case for accepting is that this is deliberately a small foundation: the ADR and implementation tasks could fill in encoding and filesystem mechanics later, and the steer explicitly skipped a numeric overlap lifetime. I agree that the review must not invent a duration, require an HSM, or force the root to be physically absent from the host. “Not required online for ordinary sessions” permits explicit root use during provisioning or rotation. Keeping private root material in the chosen directory also does not promise survival of host compromise.

However, the unchecked tasks currently delegate the central architectural decisions to implementation. The delta demonstrates an already-delegated key and a successful overlap but never defines the authority being verified or the failure cases. Bead `mjolnir-22ff.2` explicitly owns scope, validity, key IDs, supersession/revocation, persistence, backup, and recovery. These are prerequisites for the sibling consumers, not endpoint details. The following four findings must be answered in the design and delta before fresh advise.

## Required amendments

### R1 — Define what an offline verifier actually authenticates

Evidence: `design.md:17` says the identity key delegates; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:14` assumes a delegated key and then accepts it. Neither specifies the verifiable binding from XID to identity public key or from identity key to operational authority.

Pin a delegation contract or an exact existing contract reference: XID/public-key validation, supported algorithms and signed representation, operational public key and key ID, edge binding, permitted purposes, validity bounds, and whether onward delegation is forbidden or constrained. Specify the verifier inputs and rejection rules. Edge-proof signing and capability issuance may share a key under the chosen design, but their authorization and signed-message domains must be unambiguous. Include negative scenarios for wrong edge, tampered delegation, unauthorized purpose, expired/not-yet-valid delegation, and a substituted public key with a reused key ID. A key ID is a selector, not evidence of authority.

### R2 — Reconcile offline root custody with startup generation and rotation

Evidence: `proposal.md` promises generation on first start and one-time stable provisioning; `steer.md` says operational keys are minted on start if missing; `design.md:23` says ordinary sessions need no stable key. There is no transition describing how a generated key becomes authorized.

Specify first provisioning, restart with valid delegated material and no accessible root, rotation, and missing/corrupt operational state. State when the stable signer is needed and what happens if it is unavailable. Generation alone must not activate an undelegated key; loss of established state must not silently replace the stable identity. Define which direct-auth functions fail closed and whether existing hosted authentication remains available. Add scenarios for root-offline restart and missing operational material with root unavailable. An explicit maintenance signing step is compatible with the steer; unattended generation is not by itself a delegation mechanism.

### R3 — Bound overlap and make offline revocation claims honest

Evidence: `design.md:38` promises validity until expiry or revocation and describes recovery as revoke/supersede; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:21` covers only successful rotation. No authenticated revocation state, freshness rule, or retirement boundary is specified.

Define the relationship among maximum capability lifetime, delegation validity, proof validity, allowed skew, retained public verification material, and any cached key/revocation state. A numeric default may remain deferred as the steer allows; the enforceable bounds may not. Separate stopping old-key issuance and removing private material from retaining public verification evidence. Include repeated rotations, expiry/retirement, and the explicit compromise exception to graceful overlap.

Explain how an offline verifier learns supersession/revocation, what happens with stale state, and the maximum accepted exposure or fail-closed condition. An offline verifier with unchanged inputs cannot learn that a key has just been stolen. Merely issuing K2 does not invalidate a still-valid K1 delegation, and a holder of stolen K1 may mint fresh capabilities while that authority remains accepted. Do not let capability expiry alone stand in for bounded delegation authority. Define the recovery guarantee and test a stale verifier presented with a capability newly forged under retired K1.

### R4 — Specify safe persistence and restore behavior

Evidence: `design.md:28` gives only a path and mode; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:28` assumes the keys are already safely placed; `tasks.md:8` defers recovery to future writing. Escrow documents 0700 directories and temporary-file rename at `lib/mjolnir/secret_escrow.ex:28`, but its writer sets file mode after writing and ignores chmod errors (`:59` onward). It is a location precedent, not a complete key-store security contract.

Require private permissions at creation, protected directory ownership/mode, and rejection of unsafe paths or aliases into the data volume, including temporary files. Define atomic activation of matching private key, delegation, and current-key metadata, with a recoverable or fail-closed outcome after interruption. Define an explicit backup/restore procedure outside VM snapshots: how the same stable XID is restored, how stale operational/revocation state is handled, and what happens when the root is lost or compromised. Restoring the auth directory must not be presented as erasing compromise or silently reviving retired authority. Add negative permission/path, interrupted-rotation, corrupt-state, and stale-restore scenarios. The particular filesystem implementation can remain an act decision.
