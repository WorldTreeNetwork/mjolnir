## Blind pass and evidence

Before opening design.md, tasks.md, steer.md, or the delta, I read the proposal's Why, confirmed there is no living `openspec/specs/edge-auth/spec.md`, read the reachable upstream challenge protocol, checked the cited current auth code, and read the two relevant LEARNINGS entries. I then persisted [the ten-line independent take](../notes/2026-09-24-blind-take.md). Its concerns, unchanged, are:

1. Pin the unchanged v1 Challenge and Response bytes, signing domain, verification policy, and algorithm-committing holder fingerprint; refuse a Mjolnir identity-protocol fork.
2. Pin Challenge.aud to the expected edge stable XID, with a trustworthy client-side pin; refuse discovery data as its own trust anchor.
3. Require exact issued challenge matching, bounded lifetime, and atomic single-use consumption; define concurrent submission and restart behavior.
4. Require a separately domain-separated authorization signature from the authenticated holder that binds this specific identity exchange to the session public key, requested operation, and channel context.
5. Require a concrete channel-context derivation and verifier comparison for every supported transport; a transport name alone cannot prevent replay between connections.
6. Require proof of possession of the bound session key before granting its use, with explicit separation between identity success and authorization issuance.
7. Require canonical operation/resource encoding and equality with the executed request, plus local authorization policy; a valid holder signature alone must not confer arbitrary scope.
8. Preserve the v1 fingerprint namespace and distinguish holder keys from stable user XIDs and rotating edge keys; refuse raw-key fingerprint substitution or implicit account linking.
9. Treat C1/C2 and C3/C4 responses identically and keep identikey-core on the claimant side; refuse custody inference and protocol Schnorr additions.
10. Accept the extra signing and replay-state cost of two objects for identity-protocol reuse, provided vectors exercise success, replay, expiry, wrong edge/audience, altered request, key substitution, and cross-connection replay.

Source baseline: `../identikey-protocol/docs/standards/identikey-auth-challenge-v1.md` §§4–7, 8.1, 10 (sibling repository); `native/mjolnir_api/src/auth.rs:229–236`; `lib/mjolnir/api/auth.ex:5–24`; `docs/LEARNINGS.md:35–36`. Current login uses device-code/loopback OIDC; the API's existing credential lanes are sites tokens, localhost bypass, and JWT. None supplies the new direct exchange by implication.

## What the design answers

`design.md:17–18` and the delta's first requirement retain v1 and pin the audience to the stable edge XID. `design.md:20–23` places session, operation, channel, and challenge association in a second object. The delta explicitly rejects a bind signed by another holder (`specs/edge-auth/spec.md:34–39`). `design.md:25–35` preserves custody-neutral verification and refuses Schnorr. Referencing v1 also preserves its fingerprint namespace and PQ verification rules; those need not be rewritten here. Pin provisioning, capability profiles, endpoint implementation, and account migration appropriately remain outside this node.

## Required amendments

### R1 — Define the authorization verification contract and its signer handoff

Evidence: `design.md:20–23,27–29`; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:8–10,34–39`; `tasks.md:4`.

The field list and different-holder rejection are useful, but the contract never fixes the authorization signing domain, unambiguous signed representation, exact challenge association, operation/resource meaning, or expiry relationship. Two implementations can satisfy the field list while accepting different bytes or interpreting an operation differently. An unchanged signed bind attached to a different valid exchange also has no explicit rejection scenario.

Pin those semantic rules and make the same authenticated holder algorithm/key the verifier's reference. State that the requested operation must be checked against local policy and that the accepted authorization is limited to the signed session key and operation/resource. Reference the owner of later session-key proof of possession and its enforcement point; it need not become a third object or be implemented here.

For C1/C2, the only described managed output is a Response over the Challenge. That signature cannot authorize the second object, and a client holding only the ephemeral session key cannot satisfy the delta's same-holder rule. Specify the interface obligation to `add-managed-challenge-responder`: who obtains the holder's second signature, and how consent covers the requested bind. Alternatively, explicitly defer managed completion of this flow. Do not infer consent from the operator's Challenge signature; upstream §8.1 expressly separates the access credential from that signature.

Exact byte fixtures may remain the existing implementation task, as steer allows. The blocker is the missing verification and signer contract, not a preference for a particular serialization.

### R2 — Define what channel binding proves and how it is verified

Evidence: `design.md:20–22`; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:8–10,21–26`; `proposal.md:11,25–26`.

“Channel context” has no derivation, trust source, or verifier comparison rule. There is no transport-replay scenario in the delta. An implementation that signs only `https` or a client-chosen opaque string would fit the present wording while providing no connection binding. Likewise, a peer can copy the expected XID into an unsigned Challenge; an audience equality test alone does not authenticate the peer as that edge.

Define the transport adapter's required authenticated inputs, the connection/exchange scope of the binding, and rejection when those inputs are unavailable or differ. Reference the edge-authentication contract owned by `add-edge-op-keys` rather than redesigning pinning here. Specify how it establishes that the current peer represents the pinned edge, and how the authorization verifier checks the signed context against that peer/session. Explicitly delimit supported transports if concrete mappings are deferred.

Add a case where a valid exchange/bind is moved to another connection of the same transport, and a case where the wrong peer advertises the correct `aud`. Explain the expected result and what authenticated input distinguishes each case. These cover gaps that the current wrong-audience scenario cannot detect.

### R3 — Carry the atomic consumption/issuance contract into the design and delta

Evidence: `steer.md:18`; `design.md:22–23`; `specs/edge-auth/spec.md:12–19,28–32`. Additional post-blind code check: sibling `identikey-protocol/crates/identikey-auth/src/verify.rs:27–39,76–83` and `src/nonce.rs:12–19,40–51`.

Steer requires atomic nonce consumption and capability issuance, but the reviewed design only says verify identity, verify bind, then mint. The existing `verify_response` consumes the nonce before returning `Verified`. A straightforward composition therefore burns the proof before bind validation or issuance succeeds. This does not demonstrate duplicate issuance in the current code; it demonstrates that calling the existing verifier sequentially is insufficient to establish the promised transaction and failure behavior.

Define the observable acceptance boundary for the combined exchange and reference an integration mechanism compatible with the unchanged v1 verifier (for example, a transaction-scoped NonceStore). Keep implementation in `add-direct-auth-endpoints`. Pin outcomes for invalid bind after valid identity, concurrent duplicate submissions, issuance failure/crash, restart, and response loss. No capability may be usable before the required checks and commit, and no replay may create a second grant. A fresh challenge after failure is a valid recovery choice if stated consistently with the atomicity requirement.

The success scenario should distinguish authenticated identity from successful authorization and describe the downstream issuance precondition. Add independent expiry coverage for a previously unused proof; the current “previously accepted Response ... or after exp” case can pass solely because replay was rejected, without testing expiry. Ensure all eight promised vector classes have separate, testable expectations, including bind/challenge substitution and transport replay.

## Steelman against the independent take

The strongest defense is that this is deliberately a small protocol-consumer change. Upstream v1 already specifies identity crypto and replay checks; `add-edge-op-keys` owns trust bootstrap, `add-direct-auth-endpoints` owns minting and storage, and the existing task explicitly promises to specify the second object. Steer also intentionally skips exact encoding. Requiring all adapter implementations, a new durability service, session-key proof during login specifically, or managed signing code here would overreach.

I accept that defense for implementation placement, exact vector bytes, and the timing of session-key possession checks. I do not require a competing design or duplicate upstream requirements. It does not answer the remaining interface questions: whose authorization signature clients can obtain, what a channel value proves, and how the consuming verifier participates in the atomic result. Those choices determine the safety and compatibility of downstream work. Small normative contracts and explicit handoffs are sufficient to resolve this send-back.
