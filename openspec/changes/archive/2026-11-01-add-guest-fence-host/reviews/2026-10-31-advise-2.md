## Blind take

Written before opening design.md, tasks.md, the proposed spec, or the previous review; inputs are proposal Why and api-proxy/src/fence.rs only. No living spec was supplied.

1. Pin journal identity and per-boot instance to authenticated host evidence; copied guest disk must confer neither identity nor ownership.
2. Pin one host ordering boundary for admission, transfer, and revocation; refuse a detached lease check followed by an unprotected mutation.
3. Retain accepted admissions durably across local-write failure, response loss, and host restart, without allowing a retry to create another acceptance.
4. Classification must cover all retained host history, including absent guest keys; unknown, mismatched, or stale coverage must fail closed.
5. Ownership replacement must be host-authorized, with monotonic generations and stable pending/confirmed grants on adoption retry.
6. Make the remote meaning of commit's local closure explicit, including interrupted delivery, transfer during local work, and bounded recovery.
7. Bind every mutation to its authenticated instance, journal, grant, and exact payload; treat generation zero as legacy rather than host authority.
8. Reconciliation requires independent instance authorization and an existing admission; terminal uncertainty must not become fresh admission or egress permission.
9. Refuse effect-contract names as guest-provided proof: policy must recognize the idempotency mapping or reconciliation procedure before admission.
10. Tradeoff: conservative retained uncertainty and unavailable service can block progress, but recovery must preserve safety without silently promoting a clone.

## Comparison and steelman

The amendment substantially improves the contract: identity now matches the trait; pending grants cannot Lease or Admit; confirmation is instance-bound and idempotent; classifications bind journal, instance, and history; reconciliation has separate authorization; effect identifiers are policy-owned; and restart must retain admissions and generations. Refusing adoption on uncertainty remains a defensible availability tradeoff. Neither a particular storage engine nor a wire is required for acceptance.

The strongest accepting interpretation is that “same grant” preserves confirmed state, “record acceptance” already means durable acceptance, and “exact mutation” supplies the missing case distinctions. But the explicit pending-only and before-return wording permits incompatible implementations, and the owner's coverage rule requested in R2 is still absent. These are unresolved contract obligations, not a request to copy the synthetic implementation.

## R1–R4 disposition and required amendments

**R1 — Partially addressed; adoption state and replacement authority remain ambiguous (high).** `specs/guest-fence/spec.md:30` says begin SHALL return a pending grant even when recovering a lost confirm reply; line 115 also says Adopt SHALL yield only a pending grant. The new lost-confirm scenario tests another `confirm`, whereas the actual recovery path calls `classify → begin_adoption → commit(Adopt) → confirm` again (`api-proxy/src/fence.rs:135–164`). Specify that begin returns the existing pending **or confirmed** grant, that repeated Adopt does not demote a confirmed grant, and that commit(Adopt) validates/persists adoption of the supplied grant rather than issuing another one. Add the full lost-confirm recovery sequence and failure-before-confirm scenario. Host-controlled designation is also still implicit: an authenticated instance with complete receipts must not be able to elect itself merely by calling begin. Require host authorization of replacement and a negative scenario for an authenticated but undesignated instance. Identity/generation shape and pending Lease/Admit rejection are resolved.

**R2 — Partially addressed; active-owner coverage advancement is missing (high).** The blanket stale-history rule at spec lines 98–105 invalidates a classification after any later admission. `FencedJournal` retains the classification obtained during adopt and reuses it for successive new claims (`src/fence.rs:163,205–215`); it does not reclassify after each success. Consequently, a literal implementation rejects the second distinct claim after the first advances history. State how the accepting owner's coverage advances atomically with its accepted mutation while other old classifications remain stale. Add paired scenarios: two successive claims by the active owner succeed, but a separately captured older classification fails at begin/commit. The fixture demonstrates this distinction at `tests/fence.rs:245–249`; its revision-counter mechanism is not mandatory. Journal/instance binding and unknown/generation-mismatched receipt rejection are resolved in prose; add the previously requested foreign-classification and wrong-generation scenarios to make those obligations testable.

**R3 — Core authority split addressed; scope the unknown-key refusal (medium).** The authorized revoked reconciler, unauthorized reconciler, and policy-owned effect identifier cases now answer the principal concern. However, spec line 115 says without qualification that an unknown key SHALL fail closed and SHALL NOT admit. Reconcile must refuse a key absent from host admissions, while a valid fresh Admit necessarily introduces such a key. Scope that refusal to Reconcile (and unknown receipts during classification), and add a positive fresh-Admit scenario alongside an unknown-key Reconcile rejection. Keep exact journal/instance/generation/grant binding and same-key/different-digest conflict checks explicit when making this distinction; do not authorize a reconciler merely because it possesses a revoked grant.

**R4 — Partially addressed; durability timing and the ordering oracle remain incomplete (high).** Spec line 66 records acceptance before the guest write, but line 140 and design line 17 require durability only before commit returns. That wording permits a volatile acceptance, invocation of the guest write, then a host crash before the durable flush or return. The restart scenario expresses the desired outcome but does not reconcile this timing ambiguity. Require admission and its ownership/generation binding to be durable **before invoking or releasing the guest write**, including when commit never returns. Also specify durable begin/confirm/transfer transitions before exposing their results, so a crash after begin but before commit cannot reuse a generation or forget the pending grant. Unresolved-admission retention and fail-closed recovery are now stated; retain those protections.

R4 also requested explicit failure tests and a channel-spike oracle. The checked amendment task claims these are specified, but the test task (`tasks.md:17`) omits commit-versus-transfer interleavings and the design's hypothesis (`design.md:29–33`) still says only that the host can order commit with transfer. Require the spike to exercise acceptance-before-write crash, lost acceptance response, and transfer/revocation while a guest write stalls or the channel disconnects. Its finding must explain how the callback-bearing trait preserves the shared ordering boundary and safely resolves interrupted work without turning an acceptance response into reusable authority. This defines what a go finding must establish; it does not select a transport or demand that the spike run during this review.
