## Blind take

Written before opening design.md, tasks.md, or the proposed guest-fence spec; inputs were proposal.md's Why and api-proxy/src/fence.rs. No living guest-fence spec was supplied.

1. Pin journal and per-boot instance identity to authenticated host context; copied guest disk and caller-supplied identifiers cannot establish authority.
2. Pin a durable, monotonic generation and one host-controlled owner transition; adoption retries must return the same pending or confirmed grant.
3. Pin classifications to the full retained host admission history, including keys missing from guest receipts, and reject stale coverage.
4. Refuse complete classification for unknown or mismatching receipts and refuse to turn uncertain history into permission to retry an effect.
5. Pin commit's linearization boundary across exact-mutation validation, retained acceptance, and ownership transfer or revocation.
6. Retain accepted admissions through local-write failure, lost replies, and host restart; ambiguous recovery must fail closed.
7. Pin policy-owned effect-contract identifiers and journal/instance/generation/grant bindings; caller assertions are not reconciliation evidence.
8. Keep reconciliation separately authorized and limited to existing admitted keys; a revoked grant may identify work but cannot authorize fresh admission.
9. Refuse reusable send authority from grants, lease observations, adoption, or admission; the callback-bearing guest trait needs an explicit host-side semantic boundary.
10. Tradeoff: conservative retained history and serialized ownership cost availability and storage, but weakening either needs an explicit safety argument and failure tests.

## Comparison and steelman

The draft answers the central concerns: host-sourced identity, coverage of missing admissions, copied-grant rejection, acceptance before the guest write, transfer ordering, and no send authority. Keeping the wire undecided is appropriate. The blanket refusal of uncertain rows is stricter than the synthetic guest fixture, which can retain uncertainty while allowing adoption; that is a defensible availability tradeoff, not a reason to demand the fixture's design. No living guest-fence spec exists.

The strongest case for acceptance is that design.md:11 imports exact-mutation validation and the trait supplies the remaining detail. That is insufficient for this host implementation contract: the normative scenarios and task list omit distinctions the actual guest relies on. The following are semantic obligations, independent of transport choice.

## Required amendments

**R1 — Complete the adoption/confirmation state machine (high).** The spec's adoption requirement (lines 25–39) guarantees retry only for the *same classification*, and neither design nor tasks specifies `confirm`. But `FencedJournal::adopt` reclassifies on every attempt, writes adoption, then confirms (`api-proxy/src/fence.rs:135–164`). A lost begin or confirm reply followed by a fresh classification must recover the same pending or confirmed grant for that instance, without advancing the generation again. Specify host-controlled designation, pending versus confirmed permissions, instance/grant-bound idempotent confirmation, and retries across fresh valid classifications. Add scenarios for lost begin reply, failure before confirmation, lost confirm reply, and another instance attempting confirmation; pending grants must not permit Lease or Admit. Existing evidence: `api-proxy/tests/fence.rs:398–441`. Also correct the identity scenario's “current triple”: the trait's `Identity` returns journal/instance, while generation is in `Grant` (`src/fence.rs:12–24`); the host can own all three without changing that API.

**R2 — Bind classification to current history and reject foreign receipts (high).** The classification requirement (spec lines 14–23) covers missing keys and digest mismatch but omits unknown guest rows, generation mismatch, and stale or cross-instance classification identifiers. An empty classification can become stale after a later admission yet still satisfy the written adoption scenario. Require coverage to be checked at use under the commit/transfer boundary, with journal/instance/history binding and a stated rule for advancing the accepting owner's coverage. Add rejection scenarios for unknown rows, wrong generations, foreign classifications, and classify → later admission → begin/commit. This follows the trait's explicit stale/unknown prohibition (`src/fence.rs:87–100`) and the existing stale-classification test (`tests/fence.rs:586–608`); it does not require adopting the fixture's revision-counter implementation.

**R3 — Specify mutation-specific authority (high).** “Checks ... the exact mutation” (design.md:11) does not define the four cases in `Mutation`. In particular, `Reconcile` needs separate instance authorization and an existing admitted key; it may use a revoked owner's recorded grant to identify work but must never create an admission (`src/fence.rs:95–100, 234–248`). A blanket current-grant check breaks this path; merely trusting an old grant opens it too widely. Specify Adopt/Lease/Admit/Reconcile permissions, exact grant binding, same-key/different-digest conflict handling, and policy validation of `EffectContract` identifiers (`src/fence.rs:42–66`). Add scenarios for an authorized revoked reconciler, an unauthorized one, an unknown key, and an unrecognized effect contract, asserting no fresh admission or send authority results.

**R4 — Make retention and recovery testable (high).** The commit requirement (spec lines 41–50) preserves history after a failed guest write but leaves host restart, lost acceptance replies, retention lifetime, and generation recovery unspecified. A volatile host map could pass every listed scenario and forget an accepted key after restart. Define which admission/ownership facts must survive, when acceptance is durable, and how uncertain or unavailable recovery fails closed without resetting generations or forgetting unresolved admissions. Require failure tests at acceptance-before-write, lost reply, restart, and commit-versus-transfer interleavings. The channel spike should demonstrate how the callback-bearing trait's ordering survives the future host/guest boundary, including a stalled or disconnected guest and transfer; it must not reduce commit to a reusable authorization response. This requests a safety contract and an oracle, not a storage engine or wire choice.
