## Blind take

Written before opening design.md, tasks.md, the proposed guest-fence delta, or the second review; inputs are proposal Why and api-proxy/src/fence.rs. There is no living guest-fence spec.

1. Pin journal and per-boot instance identity to authenticated host provenance, never restored guest metadata.
2. Pin one durable ownership order across adoption, revocation, and accepted mutations, including crash recovery.
3. Adoption retry must recover the same pending or confirmed grant without allowing a displaced instance to elect itself again.
4. Classification must account for every retained admission, including keys absent from the guest and stale coverage.
5. Explain how classification stays usable across sequential admissions and a fresh classification obtained during adoption retry.
6. Pin exact mutation and grant bindings before the local callback; acceptance survives callback failure or a lost response.
7. Refuse any interpretation of a lease or accepted mutation as reusable upstream-send authority.
8. Reconciliation needs independent instance authorization for an existing admission and cannot mint ownership or new work.
9. Refuse a host claim about completed versus uncertain local outcomes that the existing Reconcile payload cannot convey.
10. Accept reduced availability during ambiguous recovery in exchange for preserving host admission history, without choosing transport or storage.

## Comparison and steelman

The amendment answers the major second-review concerns: full adoption retry preserves confirmed state, replacement requires host designation, successive owner admissions advance coverage, fresh Admit can introduce a key, and durability precedes release of the guest write. The channel hypothesis now has the requested crash, response-loss, and stalled/disconnected-write oracle. Conservative refusal on uncertainty is an acceptable availability tradeoff.

The strongest accepting reading treats “older copy” as a separately issued classification, “host grant” as the historical grant associated with each admission, and the digest-conflict scenario as applying only to Admit. Those readings fit the guest interface, but the new normative wording and scenarios do not consistently say that. The remaining amendments concern observable inputs and history preservation, not a competing implementation.

## R1–R4 disposition

**R1 — Resolved.** The adoption requirement and full lost-confirm retry preserve pending or confirmed state through begin, commit(Adopt), and confirm. Failure-before-confirm and undesignated-instance scenarios are present. Grant issuance and adoption persistence are distinguished; identity and generation match the trait.

**R2 — Substantially addressed; two corrections remain.** Atomic owner coverage advancement and successive-claim, foreign-classification, and wrong-generation scenarios are present.

- **Classification identity (medium):** `specs/guest-fence/spec.md:123` includes “an older copy of that owner's classification” in the stale-classification rule. The caller retains the same `Classification.id` and passes only that string at begin/Admit (`api-proxy/src/fence.rs:35–38,92,205–215`). Copying the string adds no history version. For the same authenticated instance, the host cannot distinguish the owner's retained ID from a copy of it; a different instance already fails the foreign-classification check. Define stale coverage using a separately issued classification whose host coverage was not advanced. Make the paired scenario concrete: one instance obtains C0 and C1 over the same history, adopts with C1, accepts a new key using C1, then C0 fails while C1 supports the next claim. Remove any obligation to distinguish byte-identical copies under identical authority. The synthetic fixture advances the entry keyed by the accepting classification ID (`tests/fence.rs:245–249`); its data structure is not required.
- **Receipt generation (high):** The new wrong-generation scenario (`spec.md:161–166`) compares a receipt to “the host grant.” A retained receipt records the generation of its admission, which may predate the current ownership grant. After completed work at generation 1 and adoption at generation 2, those receipts must remain valid historical evidence; comparing them to the current grant would block subsequent classification/recovery. Specify comparison with the retained admission's generation, separately from current mutation/grant checks. Add a positive mixed-generation history case and reject a receipt that disagrees with its own retained admission. This distinction is explicit in `api-proxy/tests/fence.rs:121–125`; `src/journal.rs:267–309` changes ownership without rewriting historical receipt generations. Generation zero remains legacy, not host admission authority (`src/fence.rs:31–32`).

**R3 — Unknown-key and authorization follow-up resolved; new conflict scenario needs correction (medium).** Fresh Admit and unknown-key Reconcile now have separate positive/negative cases, with independent reconciler authorization. However, `spec.md:186–191` requires “Admit or Reconcile” to submit a different digest. `Mutation::Reconcile` contains only scope and key (`api-proxy/src/fence.rs:67–70`); the host cannot inspect a digest or terminal outcome hidden inside the callback. Scope submitted-digest conflict rejection to Admit and mismatching receipts at classify. For Reconcile, assert authorization, existing-key binding, and preservation of the retained digest without inventing a digest-bearing payload. No trait expansion is needed for this correction.

**R4 — Resolved as a contract obligation.** Admission plus ownership/generation binding is durable before invocation or release of the guest write even when commit never returns. Begin/confirm/transfer persist before exposing results. Restart, lost-response, and transfer-race scenarios and the test task are present. The fence-channel finding must now explain interrupted callback work and exercise the three requested ordering cases. This is adequate specification of the spike's oracle; it is not empirical evidence that the channel works.
