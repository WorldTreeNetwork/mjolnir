## Independent take — written before opening the proposed contract

1. Pin host-owned NPU execution over the guest overlay; the spike proves this path without a guest accel device, but not occupancy or authorization.
2. Pin one explicit capacity-offer identity and an authenticated VM principal; a locator, guest IP, or VM identifier alone cannot confer execution rights.
3. Pin the existing IdentiKey capability holder fingerprint namespace, keeping it distinct from the raw-key SecretStore keyspace fingerprint.
4. Pin request proof outside the Biscuit evaluator, binding the operation, target, payload, audience, token, and freshness before injecting trusted holder facts.
5. Pin nonce ownership, consume-once behavior, and restart failure semantics across the BEAM/sidecar boundary; the existing nonce store is process memory.
6. Pin authority custody and scoped grant minting; refuse copying the host production mint root into a guest or treating the current unbounded mint helper as an expiring grant.
7. Pin offer authenticity, updates, revocation, and resource limits before dispatch; existing SecretStore canonicalization does not yet recognize an offer record.
8. Refuse a universal guild prerequisite for ordinary public-key holders; guild membership must be separately authenticated and injected with explicit freshness semantics.
9. Pin optional locator injection on boot and resume, the host-only operator socket boundary, and occupancy coordination with IRON before declaring the path shareable.
10. Tradeoff: synchronous bounded jobs and fail-closed control-plane dependence simplify v1, at the cost of availability and throughput; require explicit contention and crash behavior.

## Comparison and steelman

The ten lines above were persisted in this file before opening `design.md`, `tasks.md`, the AIE spec delta, or ADR 0015. Initial evidence was the packet's Why, biscuit-runtime and blob-store living specs, the archived IdentiKey guild-holder requirement, and the cited code and spike. The operator pipeline and authentication code below were inspected subsequently to test the revised boundary. Prior review bodies were not used to reach this verdict.

The revision answers most of the take. Decisions 1–3 keep the proven overlay path, identify the VM being, distinguish the host audience from a reused overlay origin, and move locator arguments to a map. Decisions 4–5 correctly retain the production root and proof verification in the BEAM, distinguish fingerprint namespaces, require the offering-VM allowlist, and bind grants to a signed leaf incarnation. Monotonic sequence and a retained tombstone floor answer resurrection; whole-offer revocation explicitly accepts the cost of re-minting personal grants instead of pretending the current mint API has expiry.

Decision 6's consumer-VM holder plus member-signed delegation is stronger than requiring a human member key in the guest. The personal holder path remains independent of guild lookup. Admission-time lookup, incarnation comparison, and failure-closed behavior address stale queued policy. Decisions 7–8 bound jobs to a host catalog and a wall time, retain the lock through worker quiescence, and correctly leave the occupancy spike unproved. These are architecture-level answers; I do not require the child implementations or envelope encoding to exist for this node to pass.

The strongest case for acceptance is that the BEAM is already the trusted policy owner and a dedicated sidecar can enforce its decisions. Reusing the operator socket avoids moving nonce state, mint keys, or SecretStore verification into another process; splitting enqueue from admission avoids consuming a nonce twice after waiting for occupancy. Both intentions are sound. The remaining problems are the authority actually granted by that socket and the missing identity/lifecycle of the authenticated request between those two calls. These are shared security contracts, not choices of HTTP path or serialization that a child can settle independently.

## R5 — Reconcile the sidecar identity with the operator socket authority

**Blocking.** `design.md:63–67` and the delta at `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:85–88` require the sidecar to call `/run/mjolnir/operator.sock`, while `design.md:195–196` and the locator requirement require a dedicated non-root user. The existing listener is chmod `0600` (`lib/mjolnir/application.ex:141–165`). `lib/mjolnir/api/operator_pipeline.ex:4–7` explicitly defines `0600`, owner root, as the credential; its `call/2` marks every connection as operator and forwards to the general router. `lib/mjolnir/api/auth.ex:69–73` then grants all operator scopes and the `localhost` identity.

As written, the dedicated user cannot connect to the production socket. Merely granting it an ACL or group access repairs connectivity by giving a guest-facing NPU process the general operator credential, including VM execution and lifecycle authority. Running it as root contradicts the new requirement. A route called “admit” on that listener does not constrain which other routes the process may call.

**Owed contract amendment:** pin an accessible, authenticated, admission-scoped BEAM channel for the sidecar and its relationship to the existing operator listener. The author may choose the mechanism; a distinct restricted listener or an explicitly separated principal/routing policy could satisfy it. Preserve the operator boundary, specify credential/permission ownership across listener restart, and assign the work to the responsible child. Reconcile design, delta, and ADR. Required scenarios: the configured non-root sidecar can perform enqueue/admission; an unrelated local user cannot; that sidecar identity cannot use general operator VM/snapshot routes; BEAM/listener restart does not widen access.

## R6 — Pin the authenticated enqueue-to-dispatch lifecycle

**Blocking.** `design.md:63–77` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:85–97` introduce two admit phases: enqueue consumes the client nonce and stamps time; later admission revalidates an “already-authenticated request” without consuming another nonce. The contract does not identify who retains that request, how admission refers to it, how the reference is bound to the verified frame, or when it becomes unusable. Signing an idempotency key does not itself specify duplicate handling. `Mjolnir.Secrets.Nonces` is an in-memory consume-once GenServer; its map alone cannot distinguish an authenticated queued job from a previously dispatched job after the nonce is consumed.

For example, enqueue succeeds, admission succeeds, and the admission reply is lost. The sidecar retries. Or the BEAM restarts while a request remains queued in the sidecar. The current text supplies neither the authoritative state transition nor the fail-closed recovery rule. Rechecking a valid signature and current policy is insufficient to establish that a nonce-free admission belongs to a still-pending, never-dispatched request. The generic replay scenario states the desired result but does not resolve this new two-call protocol.

**Owed contract amendment:** name the owner of authenticated pending-request state (or an equivalent authenticated mechanism), bind its reference immutably to the complete verified frame and original BEAM enqueue time, and pin the atomic transition that permits at most one dispatch. Define repeated/concurrent admission, lost replies, cancellation/expiry, and BEAM/sidecar restart behavior. Invalidating pending work on restart and requiring a fresh client challenge is sufficient; this does not demand a durable queue or exactly-once completion. Explicitly refuse admission without a successful enqueue and prevent retries from resetting queue age. Assign the shared contract to the sidecar and guest-offer children. Required scenarios: admission without enqueue; changed job/reference; concurrent duplicate admission; lost-admit-reply retry; restart with pending work; expired/cancelled reference reuse. Keep worker fencing/quiescence as the separate occupancy invariant.
