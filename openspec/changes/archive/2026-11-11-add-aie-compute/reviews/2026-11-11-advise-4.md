1. Pin host RPC as the compute boundary; the spike supports reachability and execution.
2. Refuse guest device passthrough or guest ownership of the host driver.
3. Treat the injected locator as discovery; prove execution authority separately.
4. Keep the network-facing sidecar outside the general operator socket's authority.

## Comparison and steelman

The contract answers these concerns without requiring a competing transport or a durable scheduler. Host KMD ownership and overlay HTTP fit both the Cloud Hypervisor payload and the limited result of `aie-npu-path`. The locator map preserves existing discovery fields; possession of a locator confers no execute authority. Catalog-only designs, bounded input, signed wall-time limits, and exclusion through worker reaping and device quiescence bound the execution surface. The advisory-lock limitation against non-cooperating host clients is explicit.

The offering VM's identity, operator-held capacity allowlist, host production mint root, and consumer holder have separate roles. Design Decision 5 distinguishes SecretStore's raw-key fingerprint from the algorithm-committing holder fingerprint required by biscuit-runtime. It assigns the signed offer codec to `add-aie-keyspace-claims`, rather than assuming the existing record-type sniffing in `secret_store.ex:711` accepts arbitrary offers. The resource includes the verified leaf incarnation; increasing sequences and retained tombstone floors prevent retired bytes from reviving old grants. Whole-offer revocation is an explicit v1 compromise consistent with the existing resource/operation-only mint helper, not an implied per-holder expiry feature.

The proof binds the token, nonce, host audience, offer incarnation, catalog design, input hash, and idempotency key. Holder facts come from verified key possession. Guild offers additionally require current trusted membership and a member-signed delegation to the consumer VM; personal offers remain independent of guild lookup. Request proof and policy stay outside the HTTP-free Biscuit crate, matching the existing split between `Secrets.Redeem` and `Biscuit`.

The strongest objection from the blind take is the extra online BEAM dependency and loss of queued work on restart. The author's choice is sound for this scope: nonce and pending state are deliberately transient, unavailable authorization fails closed, and fresh enqueue revalidates policy. It does not promise exactly-once completion or recovery of every accepted job. Requiring durable recovery here would add a different product contract without improving the specified fail-closed boundary.

## R5 and R6: restricted admission and authenticated pending state

R5 is resolved by a separate `/run/mjolnir/aie-admit.sock`, mode `0660`, root:`mjolnir-aie`, with an enqueue/admission-only pipeline that never grants operator identity. This directly answers the existing operator socket's `0600` credential and unconditional operator marking in `application.ex`, `operator_pipeline.ex`, and `auth.ex`. Non-root success, unrelated-user refusal, denial of general VM/snapshot routes, and restart permissions have named scenarios. The sidecar does not receive the production mint root or operator socket.

R6 is resolved by the BEAM-owned pending table, immutable verified-frame binding, server-stamped enqueue time, nonce consumption only at enqueue, and atomic pending-to-dispatched transition. Unknown, altered, expired, cancelled, or restarted references cannot obtain a new dispatch. Retry does not refresh queue age. These are new obligations on `add-aie-host-sidecar`, not capabilities incorrectly attributed to the existing nonce GenServer. The shared client obligations remain with `add-aie-guest-offer`.

## R6a: replayed outcomes have a bounded load lifetime

Design Decision 4 (`design.md:101` onward), the Execute requirement, and ADR item 4 now distinguish a recorded dispatch from executable authority. The first load requires all four conditions together: the same occupancy attempt still holds the flock, its admission deadline remains live, a success names that exact `(pending_id, attempt_id)`, and no load has already started for that pending request. The sidecar owns duplicate-load suppression.

The revised scenarios at `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:250` onward cover the previously ambiguous cases:

| Case | Contract outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Admit reply lost while the same attempt still holds the lock within its deadline | Retry of the same pair returns the original success; the first load may start once. |
| Deadline expires or the lock is released, then policy is revoked | The old pending reference cannot load on a later attempt; fresh enqueue must pass current admission policy. |
| Success arrives after cancellation, restart, deadline expiry, or lock release | The abandoned attempt cannot load. |
| Duplicate successful replies arrive during a live attempt | Sidecar suppression permits at most one load. |
| BEAM or sidecar restarts | Pending references are invalidated; a fresh nonce and enqueue are required. |

The general already-dispatched refusal is read together with the explicit lost-reply scenario: it forbids another admission transition, while replaying the original outcome for the same live pair is permitted. That replay cannot create a new attempt or extend its deadline. The original R6a counterexample therefore no longer reaches an authorized load after abandonment. Decision 6 also explicitly bars cached dispatched status from skipping current-policy revalidation on a later try-lock. This closes the protocol gap rather than merely adding a deduplication label.
