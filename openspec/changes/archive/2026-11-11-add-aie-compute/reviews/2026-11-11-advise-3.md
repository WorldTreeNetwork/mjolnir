## Independent take (written before opening the proposed contracts)

1. Pin host-owned NPU execution over overlay RPC; refuse guest device passthrough or any assumption that the spike proved occupancy isolation.
2. Pin a least-privilege sidecar/control-plane boundary; refuse access to the existing root-credential operator socket merely to obtain compute authorization.
3. Pin VM key possession, offer ownership, and execution authority as distinct checks; a reachable locator or claimed VM ID is not a credential.
4. Pin signed request bytes to token, nonce, audience, operation, resource, and actual payload; refuse caller-injected holder or membership facts.
5. Pin nonce ownership, consume-once concurrency, expiry, and restart behavior outside the pure Biscuit evaluator, including cross-process failure semantics.
6. Pin expiry, offer revision/revocation, and authority key handling explicitly; the current resource/operation mint helper does not supply a complete compute grant.
7. Pin a real signed offer record and canonical codec before treating SecretStore as an arbitrary signed-record service; preserve fingerprint namespace distinctions.
8. Pin additive locator injection and boot/resume reconciliation so old guests, disabled hosts, and existing blob locators remain coherent.
9. Pin finite admission, kernel allowlisting, cleanup, and result ownership so sharing capacity cannot silently become arbitrary host execution or cross-tenant disclosure.
10. Accept the tradeoff of online authorization and fail-closed outages for simple host-local sharing, provided guild injection remains a later verified policy input and single-key grants work first.

## Comparison and steelman

The ten lines above were persisted before opening design, tasks, delta, or ADR. Inputs were the packet's Why, the cited living specifications and archived guild-holder requirement, and the cited repository code and spike. The preceding pass's review body was read only after independently comparing the current contract, to preserve finding continuity.

The design answers the central concerns. It keeps device ownership on the host, separates offer ownership from the mint authority, distinguishes the two fingerprint namespaces, and binds grants to verified leaf incarnations. The allowlist supplies host capacity authority; sequence and tombstone floors prevent revival of retired grants. Whole-offer revocation is an explicit v1 tradeoff, not an invented expiry feature in the existing mint helper. Consumer-VM proofs plus member-signed delegation avoid putting a human private key in the guest, while personal grants remain guild-independent. Catalog-only jobs, bounded input and wall time, and quiescence before lock release provide the intended execution boundary. The locator map and independent child ownership are appropriate at this rigor.

**R5 is satisfied.** Design Decision 4, the scoped-channel requirement, and ADR item 4 agree on a separate `aie-admit.sock`, root:`mjolnir-aie` ownership, mode `0660`, and a pipeline limited to enqueue/admission that never marks callers operator. Restart and negative-route scenarios are present. This answers the actual `0600` operator credential and general-router behavior in `application.ex:141–165`, `operator_pipeline.ex:16–19`, and `auth.ex:69–73`. It does not require broadening that credential.

**Most of R6 is satisfied.** The BEAM owns the pending table; immutable references bind the verified request and original enqueue stamp; enqueue consumes the nonce; admission has an atomic transition; cancellation, expiry, and restarts invalidate references. Those are meaningful answers beyond the existing in-memory nonce map. This review does not demand a durable queue, exactly-once completion, HTTP route choices, or implemented child code.

The strongest case for acceptance is that the sidecar can serialize each pending job locally, treat the BEAM transition as one admission decision, and return cached status to duplicate callers without loading twice. That is a plausible implementation. However, the current text does not distinguish cached status from permission to perform the first load, or bind that permission to the occupancy attempt and deadline. The shared contract must choose those semantics because returning a previous success and enforcing fresh admission policy can conflict after a timeout.

## R6a — Separate an admission outcome from reusable load authority

**Blocking; continuation of R6.** `design.md:90–102` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:144–156` require refusal of an already-dispatched reference but also require returning the original outcome when that reference is retried after a lost reply. The concurrent-duplicate scenario permits failure or the same outcome; the lost-reply scenario (`spec.md:240–246`) requires the original outcome. Neither defines whether a repeated successful outcome authorizes a load that has not yet occurred.

This matters at the policy/occupancy boundary, not merely in response wording:

1. The sidecar acquires the occupancy lock and calls admission for P. The BEAM validates current policy and transitions P to dispatched, but the reply is lost; no NPU load has occurred.
2. The admission deadline expires. The sidecar releases the lock, as Decisions 6–7 require on failure to proceed.
3. The offer is withdrawn or membership is removed. The sidecar later reacquires occupancy and retries P.
4. The retry clause returns the original successful outcome. If that means permission to load, the first load can use policy from the previous occupancy attempt. If it means status only, P must not start from that response, but that distinction and terminal behavior are not stated.

Decision 6 requires membership/delegation revalidation at each successful try-lock; the delta at lines 271–287 makes lookup evidence specific to one admission attempt and permits completion after withdrawal only for already-loaded work. The cached-outcome clause must be reconciled with those rules. The atomic BEAM transition alone does not define the sidecar's load decision after the reply is lost.

**Owed amendment:** distinguish historical admission status from executable authorization; define which response, under which still-valid occupancy attempt/deadline, permits the first load, and which component suppresses duplicate loads. Specify what happens after deadline expiry, lock release, cancellation, or restart, including late replies. A simple acceptable v1 choice is terminal fail-closed handling of an uncertain admission: a retry can report historical status but cannot authorize a load, and a fresh enqueue must undergo current-policy validation. A bounded attempt-bound authorization is also possible. The author should choose; this review does not require recovery of every accepted job.

Reconcile design, delta, and ADR and retain ownership with `add-aie-host-sidecar` and `add-aie-guest-offer`. Add scenarios for lost reply followed by deadline/lock release and offer or membership revocation; delayed success arriving after its attempt was abandoned; and duplicate successful replies reaching the sidecar with at most one load. State whether an ordinary lost-reply retry is status-only or may still start the first load. Keep queue age anchored to the original enqueue.
