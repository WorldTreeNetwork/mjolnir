## Verdict

Direction is right and unchanged from 08-26: three layers, tokenator as
a verifier application of agency, secret bytes out of the token,
nextKey hops as monotonicity-only, guilds deferred. Two defects are in
the SHALL text that `act` would copy verbatim into the protocol
standard (`tasks.md:21-24`), and both let an implementer who follows
the words build a bearer token while believing it is holder-bound.
They are each a one- or two-sentence amend. Send-back, not because the
architecture is wrong, but because the folded text would not produce
it.

## Blind take (written before design.md / tasks.md)

1. Pin check-vs-fact. `spec.md:7-8` says "The Datalog fact SHALL be
   `holder(<fingerprint>)`" and `spec.md:12-13` says the verifier
   injects `holder(<fingerprint>)`. If the token carries the *fact*,
   every check passes for every presenter. The token must carry only a
   *check*; the fact must come only from the verifier.
2. Pin the signed payload. `spec.md:9-11` signs "nonce (and the token
   identity)"; the scenario at `spec.md:30` signs "the nonce and
   audience". Members must agree, and must include audience, or a proof
   made for one verifier replays at another. Reuse auth-challenge v1
   §4.3's domain-separated payload rather than a second format.
3. Pin the release channel. `spec.md:113` "the tokenator may return the
   PAT to Z" and `spec.md:114-115` asserts intermediaries do not receive
   it, but no requirement produces that outcome. A holder proof proves Z
   consented, not that only Z receives. D-5 §2 binds the recipient "in
   the math"; this profile needs the analogue stated.
4. Steelman the author must answer: why not Recrypt-encrypt the PAT to
   Z and skip the tokenator? `spec.md:104-106` forbids it without a
   reason. The reason I would accept is tier, not impossibility.
5. Accept nextKey hops (`spec.md:69-74`). Monotonicity-only is the
   honest v1. Do not lean the third-party-block deferral on "P-256
   cannot sign blocks"; newer Biscuit spec revisions add P-256 block
   keys (verify at `add-capability-hop`; not blocking here).
6. Refuse "copy-out" as an undefined noun at protocol tier
   (`spec.md:43`). Define it or point at the profile that does.
7. Pin online-ness. `identikey-capability-v1.md:52-54` sells offline
   verify; redeem is inherently online. Say the redemption profile is an
   online-verifier application and does not weaken §2 for others.
8. Pin nonce freshness: single-use until expiry, or the proof replays
   within the window.
9. Tradeoff: release vs proxy. Returning the raw PAT puts it in Z's
   memory; the real tokenator pattern performs the call. v1 release is
   the right cut; name proxy as the later profile.
10. Accept secret-bytes-out-of-token and guild-not-v1 as written. The
    salt clause should cite D-5 "Salting is mandatory" for low-entropy
    secrets; PATs are fine, short API keys are not.

## Findings (send-back)

### 1. The delta says "fact" where it must say "check"

`spec.md:7-8`: "The Datalog fact SHALL be `holder(<fingerprint>)`."
`spec.md:12-13`: the verifier "inject[s] `holder(<fingerprint>)`, then
evaluate[s] the Biscuit."

A minter reading the first sentence writes `holder("Z")` into the
authority block. Authority-block facts are visible to every check, so
`check if holder($fp), $fp == "Z"` passes with no verifier input and
the "Stolen token without the named key" scenario (`spec.md:20-25`)
fails in practice while the token looks holder-bound on inspection.
`design.md:60-63` already has the correct shape; the delta does not.

Amend (two sentences in the requirement): the token SHALL carry a
holder **check** (`check if holder($fp), $fp == "<fingerprint>"`) and
SHALL NOT assert a `holder` fact in any block; the `holder` fact SHALL
be supplied only by the verifier from the presented `{alg, key}`. Add
a rejection scenario: a minted token whose blocks contain a `holder`
fact is rejected by the verifier.

### 2. No requirement produces "intermediaries do not receive it"

`spec.md:114-115` asserts, as a scenario outcome, that A, B, C "do not
receive" the PAT. The requirement above it (`spec.md:99-106`) says
only that redemption is an agency operation verified by a tokenator.
The holder proof proves Z signed a nonce; it says nothing about where
the released bytes go. If Z's request is relayed (an agent whose only
route to the verifier is through its parent, a proxy, an MCP relay),
the relay holds the PAT and the Why (`proposal.md:14-15`) is not met,
while every SHALL in the delta is satisfied.

Design 3 (`design.md:73-75`) claims this is "bearer-plus-holder, the
same idea as Recrypt D-5 §2". D-5 §2 gets custody from the math: a
stolen recryption key "yields nothing". A signature over a nonce gives
consent, not custody. The claim is only true once the release is bound
to the holder.

Amend (one requirement, protocol tier, transport-agnostic): the
verifier SHALL release the secret only to the party that completed the
holder proof, over a channel that delegation-path intermediaries
cannot read. Application profiles choose the binding: a host-local
overlay where the holder is the socket peer (Mjolnir v1), a transport
authenticated to the holder key (TLS client auth, Iroh to the holder's
node key), or sealing the response to a response key carried inside
the signed holder proof. Mjolnir v1 satisfies the first; the sibling's
"TLS client cert as the only holder proof, fine later"
(`add-secret-tokenator/design.md:147`) is then a profile choice, not a
gap.

### 3. One signed tuple, not two

`spec.md:9-11` (nonce, token identity) and `spec.md:30` (nonce,
audience) disagree inside the same requirement, and `design.md:68-69`
has a third list. Pin the members at protocol tier: token identity,
verifier-chosen nonce, audience; encoding and the definition of "token
identity" stay with `add-biscuit-runtime` and `ikp-6yz.2` as 08-26
already allowed. If finding 2 takes the sealing route in any profile,
the response key is a fourth member. Say audience in the requirement,
not only in the scenario; without it a proof made for one tokenator
replays at another that serves the same secret id.

## Steelman against my own send-back

**08-26 Fable already accepted these SHALLs; the human amend only
touched salting.** Strongest version: re-reading the same text and
finding new defects is churn, and both 08-26 readers checked the
holder requirement line by line. Why it fails: 08-26 verified that
Grok's three send-back findings were closed (`2026-08-26-fable-advise.md:31-61`)
and checked fingerprint encoding; neither read asked what a minter
does with the word "fact" or where the released bytes go. Both
findings are in sentences that `act` copies into a standard other
implementers read without this repo. A one-sentence fix now is
cheaper than a second protocol revision after `add-tokenator-redeem`
exists.

**Why not PRE the PAT** (take 4, what the author must have answered):
`design.md:41-42` says the PAT "cannot be recrypted into a form only
Z can read without GitHub participating". It can. The owner encrypts
the plaintext under a Recrypt key and gears a recryption key to Z; no
GitHub participation is needed. The rejection is still right, for a
different reason: PRE is the data-access tier and hands Z a durable,
unattenuable plaintext with no per-use policy, no TTL enforced at use,
no redemption record, and rotation of the PAT re-keys every holder.
Agency plus an online verifier gives per-redeem scope and TTL, a
metadata log the sibling already commits to
(`add-secret-tokenator/design.md:152-156`), and PAT rotation without
re-issuing tokens. That is why a tokenator is allowed for foreign
secrets without undoing D-5: the secret is foreign, so no ciphertext
of ours goes behind an online service, and the thing being granted is
a *use*, not a *read*. Say that; the folded standard should not carry
a false impossibility claim that the first Recrypt reader will refute.

## One real tradeoff

**Transport-agnostic release binding costs a sentence per profile.**
Finding 2 can be satisfied in Mjolnir v1 by the overlay topology
alone (each guest's TAP terminates at the host; there is no
intermediary on the wire). Stating the requirement at protocol tier
means every future profile (a public tokenator, a relayed agent tree
outside Mjolnir) must say how it binds release to the holder, and
the cheapest general answer, sealing to a response key inside the
holder proof, adds a key to the signed tuple and a decrypt step at Z.
The alternative, leaving release binding to each product, keeps the
standard shorter and lets the sibling ship `{value, salt}` unchanged.
I take the requirement: the Why is custody, and a standard whose
holder proof does not deliver custody is the "Biscuit as hot as the
PAT" outcome Grok's 08-26 tradeoff (`reviews/2026-08-26-advise.md:35-42`)
rejected.

## What is solid

- Profile-scoped holder SHALL with the explicit VM-exec carve-out
  (`spec.md:16-18`). Do not widen it.
- Zero-hop redeem, nextKey-only v1 hops, third-party blocks deferred
  to `add-capability-hop` (`spec.md:63-97`). The two rejection
  scenarios are good review hooks.
- Salted Blake3 commitment, unsalted hash forbidden, fingerprint
  namespace pinned to auth-challenge §5 (`spec.md:35-46`). Matches
  D-5 "Salting is mandatory" and the sibling's domain string.
- "Recrypt ciphertext proposed as tokenator payload" rejection
  (`spec.md:117-122`) is the D-5 boundary stated as a rule.
- Guild holder class as a future verifier-injected fact
  (`spec.md:124-135`, `design.md:98-103`): token shape does not change.
