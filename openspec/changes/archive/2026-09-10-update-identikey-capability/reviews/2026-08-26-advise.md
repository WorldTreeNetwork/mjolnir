## Verdict

Direction stands. Three layers, tokenator-as-verifier, PAT-not-in-
the-token, and “PRE is for our ciphertext” are the right split.
Do not `act` this into the protocol spec until the three spec
bugs below are amended. They are in the SHALLs, not in the prose.

## Steelman against

**Use PRE / recrypt the PAT.** Strongest version: D-5 already
rejected a registry that sees every redemption; a tokenator that
returns GitHub bytes *is* that registry; gear the PAT to Z’s key
and stay offline. Why it fails: GitHub issued plaintext. There is
no ciphertext-for-owner to transform. Forcing PRE here invents a
second encryption of someone else’s secret and still needs a
place that saw the PAT in the clear (the owner’s deposit). The
design names that place and lives with it. Hold that.

## One real tradeoff

Holder-plus-bearer (signature at use) vs bearer-only Biscuit.
Bearer-only is smaller and matches how `biscuit-auth` demos work.
It also means anyone who sees the mailbox payload can redeem.
The intend was “don’t send the PAT down the tree,” not “the
Biscuit is as hot as the PAT.” Holder proof is the right extra
round trip. Keep it.

## Findings (send-back)

### 1. Holder SHALL is global. It must be a profile.

`specs/identikey-capability/spec.md` lines 3–10: “A Biscuit that
authorizes **an operation** SHALL name the holder class.” That
covers future VM-exec tokens (`docs/plans/rbac-design.md` root
caps have `right(vm, exec)` and no holder). Either every agency
token in the stack becomes holder-bound (say so, and rewrite
rbac-design) or this SHALL is scoped to the secret-redemption
profile. Today it silently forbids Phase 1 VM biscuits.

### 2. Hop SHALL contradicts Decision 4.

Spec lines 42–46: forwarding **SHALL** be Biscuit blocks signed
by the forwarding key. Design Decision 4: v1 redeem **does not
require** intermediate hops to append a block. One of those is
false. Amend the spec: hops, when they exist, are blocks; v1
redeem of an issuer-bound token MAY have zero hop blocks.

### 3. “Forwarding agent’s key” is not biscuit-auth attenuation.

Attenuation blocks are signed by the token’s **nextKey**, which
the current holder of the bytes has. That is not the forwarder’s
Identikey (Ed25519-or-P-256, `identikey-capability-v1.md` §3.1–
§3.2: P-256 does not sign Biscuit blocks). Identikey attribution
per hop is a **third-party block**, or a separate signature
outside the token. The design fuses monotonic attenuation with
identity provenance. Name which biscuit mechanism is v1, or drop
“signed by the forwarding agent’s key” and say “the holder of
the token appends a nextKey block; Identikey attribution is
third-party / later (`add-capability-hop`).”

## What is solid

- Decision 1 vs Recrypt D-5: foreign secret ≠ our ciphertext.
- Decision 2: secret bytes out of the token.
- Decision 3’s *HTTP* holder proof (nonce + audience + inject
  `holder(pk)`) is the right verifier-side move and does not
  need the hop mechanism.
- Decision 5: guilds as a later injected fact, not a token
  reshape.
- Rejected list (unsigned mode, UCAN, resources-on-challenge,
  identikey-log) matches the protocol file as it stands.

## Implementer gaps (after amend)

- Canonical encoding of `holder($pk)` vs HTTP `public_key` +
  `alg` (self-describing key bytes, same as auth-challenge).
- Test vectors remain `ikp-6yz.2`.
