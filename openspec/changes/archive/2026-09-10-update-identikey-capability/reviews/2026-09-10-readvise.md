## Verdict

Accept. The Why (`proposal.md:14-18`) asks three questions and the
delta now answers each in SHALL text that would fold verbatim and
produce the architecture:

- **Bound to the using key.** Token carries a holder *check*, never a
  `holder` fact; the fact is verifier-injected from the presented
  `{alg, key}` after a signature over one tuple of (token identity,
  nonce, audience, optional response key) (`spec.md:6-17`). Possession
  of bytes is insufficient by construction, and the rejection scenario
  at `spec.md:40-46` makes the mistake the 09-10 finding 1 described a
  verifier error, not a silent bearer token.
- **Hops proven.** Block chain only; zero hops valid when bound at
  mint; nextKey attenuation proves monotonicity and the spec says so
  in those words (`spec.md:81-91`). Identikey attribution is
  third-party blocks, deferred with a named change.
- **Tokenator without undoing D-5.** Redeem is agency on a Biscuit,
  verified by a party that already holds the secret; PRE stays the
  layer for our ciphertext; release is bound to the party that
  completed the proof over a channel intermediaries cannot read
  (`spec.md:116-131`). `design.md:39-47` now argues tier (durable
  read vs redeemable use with TTL, scope, log, rotation), not the
  false "GitHub must participate" claim. That is the argument I would
  have accepted.

The remaining items below are fold-time clauses, one phrase each,
that tighten defense-in-depth. None changes a decision, and none
lets an implementer who follows the words build the wrong thing. They
go to `act`, not back to the author.

## Blind take (written before design.md / tasks.md / prior review)

1. Pin: the holder check must live in the authority block at mint.
   `spec.md:81-82` implies it; `spec.md:6-8` does not say it. A check
   only in an attenuation block leaves the pre-attenuation bytes
   holder-free.
2. Pin: "SHALL NOT assert a `holder` fact" (`spec.md:8-9`) is not
   enough. A Datalog **rule** with head `holder` in any block derives
   the same fact at evaluation. Ban rules too; enforcement is
   structural block inspection, since a Datalog check cannot forbid a
   predicate's presence.
3. Pin: the fingerprint literal in the check needs a fixed string form
   (base58 display per auth-challenge §5 line 143, or bytes). Leaving
   it to `ikp-6yz.2` is fine only if named.
4. Pin: token identity must cover the full block chain, not the
   authority block alone, or a proof made for an attenuated token
   replays against its wider ancestor.
5. Pin: Mjolnir v1 "holder is the socket peer" (`spec.md:128-129`)
   must mean the connection that carried the proof. An overlay IP
   names a VM, not a key.
6. Refuse: nothing. Redeem-as-agency with a tokenator that already
   holds the secret is the correct layer; PRE of a PAT would turn the
   token into a data object.
7. Refuse: any relaxation of "nor a ciphertext the presenter can
   decrypt without the verifier" (`spec.md:51-52`). That clause is
   what keeps the token agency, not data.
8. Tradeoff: salt travelling with the token (`spec.md:55`) hides the
   secret from wire observers only; D-5 line 213 says exactly that.
   Fine for a PAT. For a low-entropy secret it hands A, B, C a
   brute-forceable commitment, which is the case the cited D-5
   sentence exists to prevent.
9. Tradeoff: nextKey hops prove monotonicity only (`spec.md:87-90`).
   Honest and the right v1 cut; "hop provenance" then means what was
   narrowed, not who forwarded.
10. Tradeoff: an online verifier drops §2 offline verify for this
    profile. Acceptable because the secret store is online by nature,
    and the spec says so (`spec.md:61-63`).

## Steelman against my own accept

**The verifier algorithm at `spec.md:11-17` has no step that fails
when the token carries no holder check.** Strongest version: a minter
that omits the check (a VM-exec minter reused for `redeem`) yields a
token that passes the injected-fact evaluation for any signer, so the
spec rejects the less dangerous mistake (`holder` fact present,
`spec.md:40-46`) and accepts the more dangerous one (check absent).
That asymmetry is real and is the best reason to send back. Why it
does not carry: the minter is the Biscuit trust root; a minter that
violates "the token SHALL carry a holder check" is in the same class
as one that mints `right` for the wrong secret, and no verifier
policy catches that class. The fact-rejection scenario is
defense-in-depth against a minter that misread the 08-26 text, which
is a mistake the current text no longer invites. Closing the
asymmetry is one clause in the verifier steps (note A), cheaper at
fold than as a third advise round, and it does not change any
decision in `design.md`.

**Churn.** Two send-backs already; a third on one-phrase items would
be the pattern the 09-10 steelman warned against. The 09-10 findings
were sentences that produced a bearer token when followed. Nothing
below does.

## One real tradeoff

**Defense-in-depth inspection vs. Biscuit-native evaluation.** Notes
A and B ask the verifier to walk block contents (is there a `holder`
check; is there a `holder` fact or rule) before running the Datalog
authorizer. That is a second evaluation path outside `biscuit-auth`'s
authorizer, a few lines per verifier, and it must track the block
API across `biscuit-auth` versions. The alternative is to trust the
minter and keep the verifier purely "signature chain plus
authorizer", which is what `identikey-capability-v1.md:108-112`
describes. I take the inspection, because the delta already commits
to it for the fact case (`spec.md:40-46`) and doing it for one
predicate but not its rule and absence forms is the asymmetry above.
Either way the architecture stands.

## Carry to `act` (fold-time clauses, not send-back boxes)

Each is one phrase in the ADDED text as it is copied into
`identikey-capability-v1.md`. None requires a re-advise.

- **A.** `spec.md:6-8`: "carry a holder check **in the authority
  block**"; and in the verifier steps, "a redeem token that carries no
  holder check SHALL be rejected".
- **B.** `spec.md:8-9` and scenario `spec.md:40-46`: "SHALL NOT
  contain a `holder` **fact or a rule whose head is `holder`** in any
  block".
- **C.** Where `spec.md:17-19` defers token identity to
  `add-biscuit-runtime`: add the constraint "token identity SHALL
  commit to every block, not the authority block alone" so a proof
  cannot be replayed against an ancestor with fewer checks.
- **D.** `spec.md:128-129`: "host-local overlay where the release is
  returned **on the connection that carried the holder proof**
  (Mjolnir v1)". Socket peer by IP names a VM, not a key.
- **E.** `spec.md:55`: replace "Salt MAY travel with the token" with
  "Salt SHALL be returned with copy-out and SHALL NOT travel with the
  token". Copy-out already returns `{value, salt}` (`spec.md:57`,
  sibling `design.md:159`), so the MAY has no consumer, and for a
  low-entropy secret it hands intermediaries a brute-forceable
  commitment, the case D-5 line 213 names.

## What is solid

- Holder is a check; fact is verifier-injected only; failed proof
  fails closed; profile-scoped with the VM-exec carve-out
  (`spec.md:3-23`).
- One signed tuple with audience in the requirement and the
  scenario; response key as the optional fourth member
  (`spec.md:12-14`, `spec.md:35`).
- Secret bytes and presenter-decryptable ciphertext out of the token;
  salted Blake3 commitment; unsalted hash rejected at review
  (`spec.md:48-78`).
- Zero-hop redeem; nextKey attenuation named as monotonicity, not
  attribution; two rejection scenarios that will catch the two
  tempting wrong designs (`spec.md:80-114`).
- Release bound to the proving party with three named profile
  bindings (`spec.md:125-131`); the Mjolnir v1 binding is real today
  because each guest's TAP terminates at the host.
- Tier rationale for tokenator-over-PRE (`design.md:39-47`): use vs
  read, TTL at use, redemption log, rotation without re-keying.
- Guild holder class as a future verifier-injected fact with no token
  shape change (`spec.md:150-161`, `design.md:103-108`).
