## Independent take (written blind)

1. Pin the watcher record: where it lives, who writes it, how durable it is. It also has to tell a missed transition apart from no transition.
2. Pin the live death channel. "Watcher event replaces `:exit_status`" needs a path into a running BEAM, not only a read at startup.
3. Pin identity. Nonce and starttime are gone, so identity is the scope unit name plus systemd's state. A scenario that says "identity matches" is undefined otherwise.
4. Pin the fail-safe. If StateStore or the watcher record can't be read, nothing gets touched. The old classifier had this row and the spec dropped it.
5. Give `:dormant` and `:stopped` their own scenarios, not just a clause.
6. Refuse the ADR as written. The Status paragraph says the steer replaced the classifier, yet the Decision body still states that classifier ("alive and `:stopped`/`:failed`: do not kill", "leave it unresolved"). The spec says to stop the scope and back off, so the ADR holds two decisions.
7. Refuse a watcher that infers anything. It records and the BEAM decides.
8. Pin the Off switch and the cutover cost. Guests still in the service cgroup die once, on the cutover restart.
9. Tradeoff: an out-of-BEAM watcher is one more unit to deploy and keep alive. In return it sees deaths during outages, which systemd forgets for dead transient scopes. That is worth it if the record is append-only and dumb.
10. A watcher outage is its own blind window. Say what the BEAM does across a gap in the record.

## Steelman

The steer answers the shape. Take items 2, 7 and 9 are covered. It has one restarter. The watcher only witnesses, with no `Restart=on-failure`. Its stream replaces `:exit_status`. `:dormant` is a real row. Unresolved means the existing `resume_failures` backoff, which `Reconcile` already keeps in `runtime` and retires to `:failed`. Under the spec's `:failed` rule that ends with the scope stopped, so a live guest that can't be attached gets a terminal path at last. The previous Opus caution named three problems: the starttime row overlapping cold-resume, the missing `:dormant` case, and no death signal. All three are closed. Take item 6 is owned by the task "Amend ADR 0014 to the watcher steer once advise accepts", so the stale body is known and gated. Mechanism (record path, D-Bus vs `journalctl`, the delivery channel) can fairly be left to `mjolnir-uepi.3`, which gets its own advise.

The direction is right. I'm sending it back for missing **rows**, not a different design. Those rows are contract, not mechanism. The ADR amendment is meant to transcribe this spec, and uepi.3 and uepi.4 build from it. A row left out here gets left out in all three places.

## R1 — The startup process kill is not ordered or fenced (blocking)

`Cleanup.sweep/0` (`lib/mjolnir/cleanup.ex:18-27`) starts by running `kill` on every `cloud-hypervisor` and `virtiofsd` whose args contain `socket_dir`. It doesn't check the store. After this change, every detached guest matches that rule. The spec orders the watcher read "before deleting sockets or TAPs and before Reconcile", but it never mentions the process kill. You could implement the spec to the letter, keep that line, and still kill every surviving guest at BEAM startup, which is the one thing the change exists to prevent. The spec also dropped the true-orphan row: a scope in `mjolnir-vms.slice` with no StateStore record while the store is readable. The old ADR said kill that one. The spec says nothing.

**Owed:** a requirement that startup kills a slice member only through the classification rules, never by matching ps args. A true-orphan scenario: no record and a readable store means the scope is stopped.

## R2 — Fail-safe on an unreadable store or watcher was dropped (blocking)

The old ADR's first row was "the store cannot be read: touch nothing", and the rootfs sweep already does this (`safe_state_lookup/1` → `:unknown`). The spec carries no equivalent for StateStore, and none at all for the watcher record. Picture a watcher record that's missing, corrupt, or behind. If the BEAM treats that as "no deaths seen", it attaches to scopes that are gone. If it treats it as "all dead", it cold-resumes beside a live VMM.

**Owed:** a requirement and scenario. If StateStore or the watcher record can't be read, startup does not kill, cold-resume, or delete a socket or TAP for any slice member. That guest goes into the resume-failure backoff.

## R3 — Who is authoritative for "alive" now (blocking)

The spec decides attach vs cold-resume from what "the watcher reports". The steer says the same ("scopes the watcher still reports alive"). But the watcher is history. systemd's current unit state is the present. Two cases break if the watcher is authoritative. Host power loss: the last record says active, but the unit isn't loaded. A watcher gap (take item 10): the last record says active, but the scope died while the watcher was down. "Cold-resume only after that scope is inactive" can only be checked against systemd.

**Owed:** pin it. Liveness is the current state of `mjolnir-<id>.scope` in systemd. The watcher record supplies exit status and the fact of a death that happened while the BEAM was away. If the record says active but systemd says inactive or not loaded, that is a death, and the guest is cold-resumed with exit status unknown. Add that scenario, which covers host reboot.

## R4 — Smaller rows to carry into the same edit (non-blocking)

- Identity: put `mjolnir-<id>.scope` in the spec. The steer pins the name, but the spec says only "single child id". Define "identity matches" in the reattach scenario as that name plus the record.
- Add scenarios for an interrupted `:stopped` and an interrupted `:dormant`, each with the scope alive: the scope is stopped and nothing boots.
- Add a live-death scenario with the BEAM up: the watcher event, not a port `:exit_status`, drives the stop or resume path.
- Off: the proposal lists an Off surface ("`detach` is not in use") but no requirement or config names it. Name the switch or drop the surface. Write down the cutover: guests that start under the old code die once, on the first restart after deploy.
- When the ADR amendment lands, it replaces the Decision body's classifier rows. Leaving them below a new Status paragraph doesn't count.

## Not asked for

I'm not asking for the record format, the D-Bus vs journal choice, or how the stream gets delivered. Those belong to uepi.3 and its advise.
