## Independent take (written blind)

1. Pin: a `restart_policy: :never` guest whose scope died, in an outage or a host reboot, is finalized per Reconcile/I5 and not cold-resumed. The Liveness requirement and the "host rebooted" scenario say "SHALL be cold-resumed" and carve out no policy.
2. Pin the difference between "the watcher record cannot be read" (absent or corrupt) and "the record is readable but has no entry for this id". At cutover and on a fresh install there are no entries. A host reboot loses the record if it lives in `/run`.
3. Refuse this chain: unreadable record → resume-failure backoff → `:failed` → "`:failed` with the scope alive: stop". Together these rules kill a healthy, living guest after a watcher outage.
4. Pin the stop order. Intent `:stopped` or `:dormant` is persisted before the scope is signaled. Otherwise the "interrupted stop" scenario can't be reached.
5. Pin that there is no gap between the startup read of the record and the live subscription.
6. Pin the TAP. It is a netdev, not a scope member, so say who deletes or recreates it when a guest died during the outage.
7. Pin that every virtiofsd, including the `extra_mounts` daemons, is in the same scope.
8. Tradeoff: the out-of-BEAM watcher buys outage history, but liveness is now systemd's own state. The record only earns its keep for exit status and death-while-away, so gating attach of a living scope on the record is backwards.

Take items 1–3 survive the steelman, though. Each is a place where the spec, read literally, either breaks an existing invariant or kills a guest the change exists to keep. uepi.3 and uepi.4 build from this spec, not from the ADR, so a missing row here is missing everywhere downstream.

## S1 — No row for "readable record, no entry, unit gone" (blocking)

The spec cold-resumes in two cases: "a scope the watcher saw die" (the restarter requirement) and "the record says active and systemd says inactive or not loaded" (the Liveness requirement). It has no row for a `:running` StateStore record whose id has **no watcher entry** and **no loaded unit**. Every guest the cutover reaps is exactly that case. The proposal's Cutover surface says the first restart "still reaps guests that were started inside `mjolnir.service`", and those guests were never in the slice, so the watcher never saw them. Under the spec as written they fall to "cannot be classified" → resume-failure backoff → `:failed` after `:reconcile_max_failures` (10) or 24h (`reconcile.ex` `note_failure/2`). Today they cold-resume within seconds. ADR row 4 does cover it ("or systemd says the unit is gone"), so the ADR and the spec disagree, and the spec is what gets built.

The same scenario has a durability hole. "The host rebooted" assumes the record still says active after the reboot. If the record is not on persistent storage, a reboot turns every guest into "the watcher record cannot be read" → touch nothing → backoff → `:failed`.

**Owed:** a requirement and scenario. When StateStore intent is `:running`, the record is readable with no entry for that id, and systemd has no such unit or it is inactive, that is a death with unknown exit status, and the guest is cold-resumed. Add a cutover scenario that says guests reaped at the cutover come back by cold resume. Add one line that says the watcher record survives a host reboot. The path stays mechanism. Define "cannot be read" as absent or unparseable *file*, distinct from an absent *entry*.

## S2 — Cold resume overrides `restart_policy: :never` (blocking)

`Reconcile.build_plan/1` finalizes a stranded `:never` record (intent → `:stopped`, rootfs preserved) and does not resume it, unless `revive_authorized?/1`. The moduledoc ties that to Buzz invariant I5: intentional termination is terminal. The spec says "the guest SHALL be cold-resumed" in the Liveness requirement, the restarter requirement, and the "host rebooted" and "cold resume only after the scope is gone" scenarios, with no policy qualifier. Read literally, a `:never` agent that shut itself down while the BEAM was away gets revived. That is the regression `:never` was added to prevent. The watcher now even carries the exit status that makes "it ended itself" visible. Policy, not inference, still decides, and the spec doesn't route through that policy. The ADR's attach row mentions "a live `:never` guest", which shows the author knows the case exists, but `:never` is a `restart_policy`, not an intent, and no death row honors it.

**Owed:** every "cold-resumed" in the spec reads "handed to Reconcile's existing plan (`:never` finalizes unless revive is authorized)". Add a scenario: a `:never` guest whose scope died during the outage is finalized and not booted. Fix the ADR attach row's wording so `:never` reads as a restart policy.

## S3 — Unreadable record → retirement → stop kills a living guest (blocking)

The spec puts an unreadable store or watcher record into "the existing resume-failure backoff". That backoff increments `resume_failures` on every pass and retires the record to `:failed` at 10 failures or a 24h streak. The restarter requirement then says a `:failed` guest with its scope alive has that scope stopped. So a watcher record that stays corrupt or missing for a day (or for 10 Health.Monitor passes) ends with healthy, living guests killed on the next readable start. The guests were never dead, and systemd said so the whole time. That defeats both "touch nothing" and the change's purpose. It comes from treating the record as a gate after R3 already made systemd authoritative for liveness.

**Owed:** pin one of these. Recommended: (a) with the watcher record unreadable, a scope systemd reports active whose StateStore intent is `:running` is still attached, because the record is history and liveness is systemd's state. Only guests whose unit is gone wait in backoff, since their exit status is unknown. Or (b) a backoff caused by an unreadable record does not count toward retirement, and the `:failed`-with-scope-alive stop row applies only to `:failed` set by an operator or by a real resume failure. Add a scenario for whichever is chosen: the watcher record is unreadable past the retirement limit and the living scope is not stopped.

## S4 — Smaller rows for the same edit (non-blocking)

- **Stop order.** Today `VM.stop/1` → `terminate/2` trashes the rootfs and then deletes the record. It never writes `:stopped`. The "interrupted stop finishes" scenario therefore can't happen: a BEAM that dies mid-stop leaves `:running` plus a trashed rootfs plus maybe a living scope, and that classifies as *attach*. Add a row: the operator stop and the dormancy path persist intent `:stopped` / `:dormant` before signaling the scope.
- **TAP.** Requirement 1 says the TAP "SHALL run in" the scope. A TAP is a netdev, not a process, and does not die with a cgroup. Say the TAP is keyed by the scope id and that the BEAM deletes or recreates it on stop and on cold resume.
- **All virtiofsd.** Say every virtiofsd for that VM, including the `extra_mounts` daemons, is in the scope and dies with it.
- **No read/subscribe gap.** Say the BEAM subscribes to the live stream before the startup read, or otherwise loses no transition between the two. The mechanism belongs to uepi.3.
- **Bookkeeping.** `tasks.md` has "Amend ADR 0014 … once advise accepts" checked before an accept. Either uncheck it or reword it to "amended; ratified on accept". ADR Status already says "not a license to ship until that advise accepts", which is right.

## Not asked for

I'm not asking for a record format, a D-Bus vs journal choice, a delivery channel, or a record path. Those belong to uepi.3. S1 asks only that the record survives a reboot.
