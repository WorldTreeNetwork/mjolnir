## Comparison and steelman

The design answers the authority-boundary concerns. The consumer VM proves possession of the sole Biscuit holder key. `Guild.admit/1` is an additional BEAM application gate that requires both current membership and member consent. It cannot replace execute proof, Biscuit rights, the current offer incarnation, or holder verification. This fits `lib/mjolnir/biscuit.ex:141–209` and the proof-before-authorize boundary in `lib/mjolnir/secrets/redeem.ex:53–73`; it introduces neither a guild holder class nor membership crypto into the authority block. Personal offers bypass the guild portion, including its configuration and availability dependency.

My blind preference for offer- and audience-bound delegation is answered by an explicit different scope: consent is guild-wide for AIE execute. Requiring an offer incarnation or host audience in the delegation would contradict that chosen scope. The request proof and Biscuit still bind the individual execution to its offer incarnation and verifier, while a G1 delegation cannot satisfy a G2 offer. I accept that division of responsibility. The single current record is per member/holder, so a newer grant replaces that pair's previous guild consent rather than accumulating multiple guild grants; the sequence floor has the same scope.

The selector is untrusted input captured in the pending execute frame. Deriving its storage namespace from the raw member key, then comparing that key with the signed leaf and roster subject, provides deterministic discovery without treating the selector as authority. The consumer fingerprint remains the algorithm-committing §5 identity; the base58 raw-key hash serves only the existing SecretStore namespace. The proposed codec owns the integration seam at `SecretStore.canonical_bytes_without_sig/2`, which currently recognizes other record types (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:711–735`).

The configured, authenticated, subject-bound roster callback is an adequate architecture boundary even though production Recrypt wiring is deferred. An unconfigured guild refuses, an always-member stub does not satisfy the contract, and malformed, negative, unavailable, stale, or future evidence cannot fall back to earlier success. The 60-second default age and five-second future skew describe bounded freshness rather than instantaneous global revocation. Lookup and leaf read occur only after successful try-lock under that attempt's deadline; enqueue evidence and abandoned-attempt results cannot authorize a later load.

## R7 and R8 are resolved

**R7:** the withdrawal now has a separate signed domain and purpose, a bound member/holder target, and a sequence. A grant signature cannot be reinterpreted as withdrawal, and a tombstone cannot authorize execute. A stale withdrawal cannot remove a higher-sequence regrant. The delta includes authorized withdrawal, substitution in both directions, wrong-target refusal, and stale tombstone replay scenarios. I independently reconstructed both unsigned vectors from the documented length-prefix grammar: the grant is 160 bytes and withdrawal is 170 bytes, and both match exactly. These are encoding fixtures, not signature-verification tests.

**R8:** the floor and current authorization state now occupy the same authoritative record. One atomic rename commits either a grant or a tombstone; admission reads one complete snapshot. This removes the former interval in which an advanced floor coexisted with a stale authorizing leaf. Missing, unreadable, malformed, or wrong-codec state refuses admission, and generic delete or unrelated put cannot reset the floor. The existing store serializes put/delete through its GenServer (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:255–283`) and reads directly from files (`:60–69`); a single-record rename fits those boundaries. The new sequence validation and reserved-key protection are implementation obligations, not claims about what today's store already enforces.

The concurrency policy is explicit: a grant snapshot can authorize an overlapping admission before withdrawal commits; an admission observing the tombstone cannot. That is a bounded authorization point, not a promise to cancel work already validly admitted. The revision covers interruption, concurrent admission versus mutation, malformed state, restart retention of the sequence floor, and floor-reset refusal. Eliminating the second file also eliminates the old failed-leaf-delete recovery case. No additional architecture blocker remains.
