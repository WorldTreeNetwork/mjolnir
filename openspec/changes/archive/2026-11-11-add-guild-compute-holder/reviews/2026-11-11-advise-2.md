## Independent take (written before opening design.md or tasks.md)

1. Pin the consumer VM's single IdentiKey as the token holder; guild membership must remain an admission policy outside the holder class.
2. Preserve resource-plus-operation minting, verifier-only holder injection, and the HTTP-free Biscuit runtime.
3. Derive holder identity with identikey-auth v1 §5 algorithm-committing fingerprints, never a raw-public-key hash or caller-supplied fingerprint.
4. Require the member's signed delegation to bind the guild, consumer holder, intended offer scope, operation, and validity bounds without copying a human private key into the guest.
5. Pin canonical delegation bytes, signature verification, and trusted membership-provider identity so independent implementations cannot disagree about who delegated what.
6. Resolve current membership and delegation after occupancy try-lock under a hard deadline; refuse unavailable, stale, negative, or malformed evidence without an older-positive fallback.
7. Bind each lookup result and policy success to one admission attempt; enqueue evidence, late replies, and a released lock must not authorize a later load.
8. Refuse token-authored membership facts and mismatched delegation signer/member/holder identities, and revalidate against the current offer incarnation.
9. Preserve personal-fp mint and execute without guild lookup, and keep sidecar occupancy, offer codec, and guest verbs with their named owners.
10. Accept the availability and latency cost of fresh fail-closed membership lookup; require explicit freshness and revocation semantics instead of implying instantaneous global revocation.

The consumer VM proves possession, while the member signs a separate delegation. ## R4 — Give admission a defined way to select the member and leaf

**Blocking.** `design.md:35–36,52–55,72–76` and the delta at `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:57–58,157–160` specify a lookup that needs `member_pk` and a store read that needs `member_fp`. But `Guild.admit/1` receives only the current offer, consumer holder, pending/attempt IDs, and deadline. None identifies the delegating member. The offer supplies the guild, not a unique member; the roster callback takes a member and returns a boolean, so it cannot discover that member. SecretStore's `list/2` also requires an already-known owner fingerprint (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:79`).

A valid M-to-H leaf can therefore exist while the specified admission inputs provide no defined route to it. An implementation must invent a guest-supplied selector, an index, or another lookup. Multiple members delegating to H make that choice observable, including which failure ends the search.

**Owed:** pin the source and representation of the member/delegation selector, how it reaches admission (or the bounded internal discovery contract), and the relationship to the stored pending request. A caller-supplied locator may be an untrusted hint; it must never establish membership or signer identity by itself. Specify derivation of the store owner from the verified member key and the exact signer/roster/holder/guild comparisons. Add an end-to-end scenario that starts from the actual admit inputs and finds a valid leaf, plus missing/forged selector and wrong-member cases. No particular selector mechanism is mandated.

## R5 — Finish the signed-byte and SecretStore integration contract

**Blocking.** `design.md:19–24` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:48–58` give an ordered field list but do not define an interoperable canonical encoding: length-prefix width/endianness, version and timestamp representation, fingerprint representation, and enclosing leaf/signature encoding remain unspecified. “Length-prefixed” alone admits incompatible byte streams. The existing Redeem frame (`lib/mjolnir/secrets/redeem.ex:114–130`) is explicit about unsigned big-endian 32-bit field lengths and leaves its domain unprefixed; the new profile neither references that grammar nor defines its own exact one.

The proposed persistence path also needs an explicit integration decision. Actual SecretStore code, despite its stale module comment about stubbed verification, parses JSON, loads a registered owner key, and accepts only recognized record codecs through `canonical_bytes_without_sig/2` (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:617–735`). An unrecognized delegation fails; a signature over the proposed frame is not automatically a signature over a recognized store envelope. Moreover, store identity bootstrap uses `Mjolnir.Sites.IdentiKey.fingerprint/1`, the base58 raw-public-key Blake3 namespace (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:640–647`; `lib/mjolnir/sites/identikey.ex:69–78`). The consumer holder uses identikey-auth §5 instead. `member_fp` has not been assigned a namespace or mapping.

**Owed:** define or cite an exact byte grammar and leaf envelope, with at least one deterministic signing vector; pin validity boundaries including expiry equality and maximum lifetime. State how the new record is accepted and verified by SecretStore, who owns that integration and owner-key registration, and how the storage fingerprint and protocol holder fingerprint remain distinct while binding to the intended keys. Add a contract scenario for publishing and rereading a valid leaf through the real store interface, plus malformed encoding, wrong-purpose, and wrong-namespace rejection. This asks for a contract and implementation ownership, not a product-code patch in this review.

## R6 — Make withdrawal survive replay through the write path

**Blocking.** `design.md:35–43` and `specs/aie-compute/spec.md:57–62,94–99` require a current record and refuse a withdrawn leaf presented at admission. They do not specify who may publish/replace/delete that record or how replayed writes are rejected. The proposed signed fields have no revision counter. Existing generic SecretStore `put` verifies a recognized signature and overwrites the record; `delete` verifies a signature and removes the file, retaining no tombstone/floor (`lib/mjolnir/secret_store.ex:255–284`). Merely reading the current file after restart does not supply durable revocation history.

Once the codec is integrated, the trace to rule out is: publish signed L; withdraw or replace L; replay L through a write path that treats its still-valid signature as sufficient publication authority; admission reads L as current. The existing withdrawn-leaf scenario tests only presenting L while its key is absent/tombstoned, not restoring L to that key. This is an underspecified threat boundary, not a claim that an implemented delegation endpoint is currently exploitable.

**Owed:** pin authenticated publication/update/deletion authority and anti-replay behavior, including after restart. A durable monotonic revision/tombstone floor is one option; a fresh member-authorized mutation protocol with explicit replay protection is another. Define how an intentional later regrant differs from replay of a withdrawn grant. Add scenarios for replayed put after withdrawal, rollback after replacement, and restart preservation. Do not inherit an offer-specific floor from the sibling offer codec without explicitly owning the delegation integration.
