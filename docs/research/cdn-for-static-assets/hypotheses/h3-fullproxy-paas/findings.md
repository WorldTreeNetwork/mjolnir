# Hypothesis H3: Full-proxy PaaS/edge (Cloudflare, Netlify, Vercel) deliver the best turnkey edge but are the deepest intermediary (DNS delegation + vendor TLS)

## Summary

Confirmed, with an important split inside the hypothesis. Cloudflare is the only one of the three that is architecturally a **reverse proxy in front of an existing, independently-hosted origin** — which fits Mjolnir's single-origin model — but its free/Pro tiers require **full authoritative DNS delegation** (nameserver change) and Cloudflare's edge **terminates TLS with its own certificate**, putting Cloudflare in-path on every plaintext request. CNAME-only ("partial/SaaS") setup that avoids nameserver delegation requires **Business ($200/mo) or Enterprise**. Netlify and Vercel are fundamentally **build-and-host platforms, not generic reverse proxies**: their rewrite/proxy features exist for narrow path-based API proxying (with hard limits like Netlify's 26-second timeout) and are explicitly discouraged or unsupported as a way to front an entire externally-hosted app — Vercel's own docs say a *third-party* reverse proxy in front of Vercel "is not recommended" and breaks its firewall, and nothing in either platform's docs describes fronting an arbitrary external origin as a first-class, supported pattern for whole-site serving. For Mjolnir's single self-hosted origin, Cloudflare is the only one of the three that matches the requirement at all; Netlify/Vercel would require migrating the deployment target itself, not just edge/CDN, making them a much deeper and more total form of lock-in.

## Evidence

### Cloudflare

**Origin integration (pull-proxy in front of existing origin — "orange-cloud"):**
Cloudflare's proxy mode inserts itself between visitors and an existing origin server. When a DNS record is "Proxied," Cloudflare returns one of its own anycast edge IPs instead of the origin's real IP; all traffic flows through Cloudflare's network before reaching the origin, which can remain anywhere (e.g., Mjolnir's single host). "DNS only" (grey-cloud) mode is also available per-record for traffic that should bypass the proxy entirely. [1]

**DNS requirement — precise, by plan:**
- Free and Pro plans: only "primary setup" (full) is available, which requires changing the domain's authoritative nameservers to Cloudflare's assigned ones — i.e., full DNS delegation. [2]
- "CNAME setup" (partial/SaaS-style, keeping your existing DNS provider and only pointing specific subdomains at Cloudflare via CNAME) is gated to **Business or Enterprise plans only**. Domains on CNAME setup are explicitly *not* using Cloudflare's authoritative nameservers — resolution depends on a CNAME at the original DNS provider instead. [2][3]
- Cloudflare for SaaS (a related but distinct product for multi-tenant custom domains) likewise builds on this CNAME pattern but is also not a Free/Pro capability path. [3]

**TLS / key custody:**
- Universal SSL issues a free, Cloudflare-managed certificate automatically on full (proxied) setups for the apex + first-level subdomains — Cloudflare's cert, Cloudflare's keys, terminated at Cloudflare's edge. [4]
- "Advanced Certificate Manager" (paid add-on) gives more control (choice of CA, deeper subdomain coverage, custom validity) but Cloudflare still issues/manages it. [4]
- "Custom certificates" let you upload your own cert/key to Cloudflare's edge, but you (not Cloudflare) are responsible for renewal/monitoring — and the cert/key still lives on Cloudflare's edge servers, terminating client TLS there. [4]
- Net effect for the "middleman" concern: in every configuration, when proxied, Cloudflare's edge terminates the client-facing TLS connection and can see plaintext HTTP — origin-to-edge can be re-encrypted separately (Full/Full-Strict SSL mode), but the edge is unavoidably in the cleartext path for request inspection, caching, WAF, etc. This is true even with a custom uploaded certificate, since the private key must be installed on Cloudflare's edge to terminate there.

**Features:**
- Network: ~330 cities across 125+ countries (Cloudflare's current self-reported figure, multiple 2026 secondary sources converge on this number; treat as approximate/marketing figure rather than an audited count). [5]
- HTTP/3 + QUIC: enabled by default at no extra cost on all plans. [6]
- Brotli: enabled by default on Pro and Business plans (compression behavior on Free is more limited per Cloudflare's compression docs). [7]
- Cache Rules + Purge API: granular Cache Rules plus instant purge via dashboard or API, with audit logging of purge events. [8]
- Edge compute: Cloudflare Workers (full V8 isolate compute, KV/R2/D1 bindings), available across plans with usage-based pricing beyond free quotas.
- WAF/DDoS: unmetered, "unlimited" DDoS mitigation is a headline Free-tier feature; WAF and more advanced security controls scale with paid plans.
- Signed-URL/token auth: native "Token Authentication" feature (HMAC-based, via WAF custom rules / `is_timed_hmac_valid_v0()`), and Workers-based signed-URL patterns (e.g., signing requests, validating prefixes before serving from R2). This directly supports Mjolnir's signed content-addressed asset model if assets are fronted by Cloudflare. [9]

**Pricing model + ToS limit on large/non-HTML files (the §2.8 question):**
- The literal "primarily HTML" / non-HTML restriction (old §2.8) was removed in a May 16, 2023 ToS rewrite. Cloudflare's own blog described the old rule as an "antiquated HTML vs. non-HTML construct" that was "far too broad." [10]
- It was replaced by a CDN-specific clause in the current Service-Specific Terms (page states "Last Updated: June 02, 2026" at time of this research): "Unless you are an Enterprise customer, Cloudflare offers specific Paid Services (e.g., the Developer Platform, Images, and Stream) that you must use in order to serve video and other large files via the CDN." Cloudflare may "disable or limit" CDN access if you use it "without such Paid Services to serve video or a disproportionate percentage of pictures, audio files, or other large files," with "reasonable efforts" to notify first. [11]
- **Important nuance for Mjolnir**: the post-2023 rule is arguably *more* restrictive for the "front an external origin" use case than the old "must be mostly HTML" rule suggests at first glance. Cloudflare's blog explicitly states "Video and large files hosted outside of Cloudflare will still be restricted on our CDN" — i.e., the new bar isn't "is it HTML," it's "is the large-file storage itself a Cloudflare-owned service (Stream/Images/R2)." Mjolnir's content-addressed static assets and SvelteKit build output are mostly small, fingerprinted text/JS/CSS/image files (not bulk video), so this is very likely fine in practice, but if any workload trends toward large media served from the self-hosted BTRFS origin at volume, that is the literal trigger condition for Cloudflare to throttle/restrict — not an edge case, a named one. [10][11]
- Bandwidth itself is **not metered or capped on any self-serve plan** — Cloudflare states bandwidth and DDoS mitigation are unmetered across Free/Pro/Business. The constraint that matters is the ToS large-file clause above, not a GB cap. [12]
- Plan pricing: Free = $0; Pro = $20/mo (annual billing) / ~$25/mo monthly; Business = $200/mo (annual) / ~$250/mo monthly; Enterprise = custom. Plans are priced **per domain/zone** — e.g. 10 separate Business zones would be ~$2,000/mo, not $200/mo flat. This matters for Mjolnir since `identikey.io` and `worldtree.network` (and per-customer custom domains, if any) would each be a separate zone. [12]

**Realistic cost at 1 TB/mo and 5 TB/mo:**
- If usage stays compliant with the large-file ToS clause (i.e., assets aren't bulk video/media served directly off-Cloudflare-storage at "disproportionate" scale), Cloudflare Free is **$0/mo at both 1 TB and 5 TB** — this is the standout structural advantage of this vendor: bandwidth is free regardless of scale, full stop, as long as the traffic profile resembles a normal proxied website rather than a video/file CDN. [11][12]
- If the workload trips the large-file clause, the prescribed remedy is to move large-file storage into Cloudflare-owned services (R2/Images/Stream) — which are separately metered (R2 storage + Class A/B operations; R2 itself has **no egress fee**, which is also notable) — or to go Enterprise (exempt from the clause, custom pricing, unverifiable here).

**Lock-in verdict:**
Deep but the shallowest of the three. The lock-in is **DNS custody**: Free/Pro requires moving the zone's authoritative nameservers to Cloudflare, meaning Cloudflare controls DNS resolution for the whole domain, not just specific records, and sees/terminates all proxied traffic. This is swappable in principle — repoint nameservers back to original registrar/DNS host and the origin keeps working unmodified, since Cloudflare never owned the origin/app, only the edge — but doing so loses every Cloudflare-side feature instantly (WAF, caching, signed URLs, Workers) and requires a DNS cutover with TTL-bound propagation risk. Avoiding nameserver delegation entirely (CNAME/partial setup) is possible but gated behind the $200/mo Business plan.

**Trade-off paragraph:** Cloudflare is the only one of the three vendors that actually fits Mjolnir's architecture — a single self-hosted origin behind a pull-proxy CDN — and it does so with the strongest free-tier economics in the entire CDN market (unmetered bandwidth, global anycast, HTTP/3, WAF, native signed-URL/token auth). The cost is that Free/Pro requires surrendering authoritative DNS for the zone (not just a CNAME record) to Cloudflare's nameservers, and Cloudflare's edge terminates client TLS — it is unavoidably positioned to see plaintext traffic and is, functionally, the literal "middleman" the team is wary of. The CNAME-only path that would avoid nameserver delegation exists but costs $200/mo per zone (Business), pricing it well above the free/Pro economics that make Cloudflare attractive in the first place. The large-file ToS clause is a real, named constraint (not a single complaint), but is very likely compatible with Mjolnir's fingerprinted-static + SSR asset profile as long as it doesn't grow into bulk media serving from the BTRFS origin.

### Netlify

**Origin integration — build-and-host, not a general reverse proxy:**
Netlify is fundamentally a deploy target: you push a build (static site or functions) to Netlify, and Netlify serves it from its own CDN. It does have a "rewrites and proxies" feature (`/path/* https://target 200`) that can proxy specific request paths to an external origin, including the wildcard root path `/*`, which technically *could* be configured to proxy 100% of traffic to an external self-hosted origin. [13] However: this mechanism has a **26-second timeout** per proxied request (explicitly documented, with guidance to use async patterns for anything longer), is described in Netlify's own docs primarily as a same-origin/API-proxying tool (the canonical example is "communicate from a single-page app with an API"), is limited to one proxy "hop" by default, and has documented edge cases (relative asset paths breaking, HTTP→HTTPS redirect issues) that make full-site proxying fragile in practice rather than a supported architecture. [13] There is no Netlify documentation framing "front your existing self-hosted SSR origin entirely via Netlify" as a first-class deployment model — the product is oriented around Netlify owning the build and the deploy artifact.

**DNS requirement:** CNAME (subdomain) or full nameserver delegation (Netlify DNS) are both offered, similar in shape to Cloudflare's options, but without Cloudflare's plan-gating — i.e., Netlify doesn't restrict CNAME-only setup to a higher paid tier the way Cloudflare does. (Verified only via general product knowledge of Netlify's domain docs during this pass; not independently re-confirmed with a fresh fetch in this research round — flagged as lower-confidence below.)

**TLS:** Netlify auto-provisions Let's Encrypt certificates for custom domains, terminated at Netlify's edge — same "vendor holds the client-facing key" dynamic as Cloudflare, just without the DNS-delegation requirement being as strictly plan-gated.

**Edge compute:** Netlify Edge Functions (Deno-based) run at the edge per matching URL pattern, chainable.

**Pricing / realistic cost at 1 TB and 5 TB/mo:**
Netlify's 2026 pricing is **credit-based**, a structural pivot from old flat bandwidth tiers. Pro plan: $20/mo (now flat-fee with unlimited team members per an April 14, 2026 pricing change), including 3,000 credits/mo; bandwidth consumes credits at a rate that works out to roughly **$0.13/GB** (20 credits/GB at the ~$0.00667/credit pack rate) once included credits are exhausted, or a simpler stated overage figure of **$0.55/GB** depending on which billing mechanism is cited in secondary sources — these two figures disagree by ~4x and could not be reconciled to a single authoritative number in this pass; treat both as **unverified, conflicting** until checked against Netlify's live billing dashboard. [14]
- Using the more conservative ~$0.13–0.55/GB range: 1 TB/mo overage ≈ **$130–$550/mo**; 5 TB/mo overage ≈ **$650–$2,750/mo**, on top of the $20/mo base (minus whatever the included credit allotment covers, which is small relative to TB-scale — 3,000 Pro credits ≈ only ~150 GB of bandwidth before overage kicks in). [14]
- Business tier bandwidth/pricing is custom/negotiated, not published — unverifiable without sales contact. [14]

**Lock-in verdict — deepest of the three (tied with Vercel):** Unlike Cloudflare, Netlify is not optional infrastructure layered in front of an origin you still own and control — it *is* the deploy target. Migrating off Netlify means re-platforming the deployment pipeline (build, functions, edge config, redirects/rewrites config), not just changing DNS. For a team that already runs its own self-hosted SvelteKit SSR + signed content-addressed static origin (i.e., already owns the "build-and-host" layer Netlify wants to replace), adopting Netlify as the public-facing layer requires either (a) actually moving the deployment onto Netlify's platform (defeating the point of self-hosting), or (b) abusing the 26s-timeout proxy/rewrite feature to forward 100% of traffic back to the self-hosted origin, which is an unsupported, fragile shape for a primary CDN.

**Trade-off paragraph:** Netlify delivers a polished all-in-one build+CDN+functions experience, but it assumes Netlify *is* your hosting platform, not a CDN layer in front of someone else's. Forcing Mjolnir's single self-hosted origin behind Netlify would mean either abandoning self-hosting (defeats Mjolnir's purpose) or contorting Netlify's path-rewrite proxy — built for API calls, not full-site fronting, and capped at 26 seconds per request — into a role it isn't designed for. Bandwidth economics are also worse than Cloudflare at any meaningful scale: TB-scale traffic triggers real metered overage (low hundreds to low thousands of dollars per month depending on which Netlify rate applies), versus Cloudflare's $0 as long as ToS-compliant.

### Vercel

**Origin integration — build-and-host, explicitly discourages being proxied, and does not offer a "front an external origin" pattern:**
Vercel's own docs are explicit on the adjacent question (a reverse proxy *in front of* Vercel): "We do not recommend placing a reverse proxy server in front of your Vercel project," because it breaks Vercel's firewall visibility (can't see real client IPs, can't detect/purge against attacks routed through the proxy). Self-hosted proxies (Nginx, HAProxy, etc.) require manual Enterprise-only onboarding ("Verified Proxy Advanced") with requirements like static egress IPs and custom client-IP headers. [15] This documents Vercel's posture toward *third-party* proxies sitting in front of it — but more importantly for Mjolnir's actual question (using Vercel itself as the pull-proxy in front of an external, self-hosted origin), there is no equivalent first-class feature: Vercel does have a "proxy requests to external origins" template/pattern (via rewrites/middleware calling out to an external URL per-route), but this is positioned as a route-level integration pattern (e.g., proxy `/api/*` to a backend), not a way to make Vercel the front door for an entire externally-built-and-hosted application. Vercel's core model is: you deploy your build (static + serverless/edge functions) to Vercel's infrastructure; Vercel is the origin, not a CDN layer in front of one. [16]

**DNS requirement:** Subdomains use a CNAME record pointed at a project-specific Vercel hostname (e.g., `cname.vercel-dns-0.com`); apex domains use an A record (`76.76.21.21`); a full nameserver-delegation option also exists and is required if you want wildcard subdomain support with auto-provisioned wildcard certificates. CNAME/A record setup (i.e., not delegating DNS) is available on every paid tier with no Cloudflare-style plan gating. [17]

**TLS:** Automatic certificate provisioning and edge termination, including wildcard certs when using the nameserver method; same "vendor terminates client TLS" dynamic as the other two.

**Edge compute:** Edge Middleware (interception layer, runs before the app on every matching request, V8-isolate-based) and Edge/Serverless Functions — generally considered best-in-class for Next.js-style SSR (note: Mjolnir's stack is SvelteKit, not Next.js — Vercel's edge primitives are framework-agnostic but most polished for Vercel's own framework, Next.js).

**Pricing / realistic cost at 1 TB and 5 TB/mo:**
- Hobby (free): 100 GB/mo "Fast Data Transfer" included, hard-capped — no paid overage available on Hobby at all (must upgrade to Pro to exceed it). [18]
- Pro: $20/mo per seat; **1 TB/mo Fast Data Transfer included, then $0.15/GB** overage. A separate "Fast Origin Transfer" meter (function→edge data) bills at $0.06/GB from the first byte, partially absorbed by the monthly usage credit bundled with the Pro seat. [18][19]
- At **1 TB/mo**: stays within the included Pro allotment → **~$20/mo base** (plus whatever Fast Origin Transfer is incurred from SSR function responses, likely small for a mostly-static, ~90%-cacheable workload, but non-zero given Mjolnir's SvelteKit SSR component).
- At **5 TB/mo**: 4 TB over the included 1 TB → 4,000 GB × $0.15/GB = **$600/mo** in bandwidth overage alone, on top of the $20/mo seat — **≈$620+/mo**, before Fast Origin Transfer and function compute costs are added. [18][19]

**Lock-in verdict — deepest of the three (tied with Netlify):** Same structural issue as Netlify: Vercel is the deploy target, not a CDN layer. Its own docs actively discourage third-party reverse proxies in front of it (firewall/visibility concerns), and it has no documented, supported pattern for being the pull-proxy in front of an external, independently-hosted origin at the whole-site level. Adopting Vercel for Mjolnir would mean moving the SvelteKit SSR deployment itself onto Vercel's infrastructure (their build pipeline, their function runtime, their edge network) rather than keeping Mjolnir's own host as the origin — the deepest form of lock-in among the three, on par with Netlify.

**Trade-off paragraph:** Vercel offers the most refined edge-compute/SSR developer experience of the three, but architecturally it is the least compatible with Mjolnir's "single self-hosted origin behind a CDN" model — it wants to *be* the origin. Bandwidth economics are metered past 1 TB ($0.15/GB), which is cheaper per-GB than Netlify's published rates but still real money at multi-TB scale (~$600+/mo at 5 TB) versus Cloudflare's $0. Its explicit discouragement of reverse proxies in front of itself, combined with the absence of any supported "front my external origin" capability, makes it the weakest fit for Mjolnir's constraints even though it's the strongest pure hosting platform of the three.
