# Hypothesis: H6 (CONTRARIAN) — A managed CDN is unnecessary or net-negative

## Summary

**Partially supported, with a hard ceiling.** The free/cheap wins — HTTP/2 (and HTTP/3) at the gateway plus a host-side immutable-asset cache — eliminate the *connection-multiplexing* tax currently inflating Mjolnir's `_app/immutable/*` waterfall, and should be done regardless of any CDN decision. But they do nothing for the actual problem stated in the gateway plan's own framing ("Real CDN last — origin is fast by then, so it's pure geo/latency win"): a single origin in one datacenter is still ~150–300ms+ RTT from the far side of the world, full stop, no protocol change fixes that. A self-hosted multi-PoP/anycast layer *can* close that gap, but the moment you add real BGP anycast it stops being "free" — own-ASN/IP costs, multi-node TLS/cert ops, and cross-node cache-invalidation become a standing ops burden that a $5–$10/mo Bunny pull zone replaces outright, cheaper than the engineer-hours alone. The honest verdict: do the free wins now, and treat a managed CDN as a thin, swappable, DNS/cert-sovereign edge layer added later for geo + DDoS — not as a thing to avoid on principle.

## Evidence

### 1. HTTP/2 (+HTTP/3) + tuned caching at the existing single origin — what it buys, what it doesn't

**What it buys:** HTTP/1.1 browsers cap parallelism at roughly 6 TCP connections per origin; HTTP/2 replaces that with a single multiplexed connection and removes application-layer head-of-line blocking, letting "browsers request and download hundreds of files simultaneously without the overhead of repeated TCP handshakes" [4]. This maps directly onto the gateway plan's own observation that zine's cold load fires "~10 parallel `_app/immutable/*` asset requests" that currently bottleneck on HTTP/1.1 (`docs/plans/gateway-local-routing.md:112`). Benchmarks generally agree that pages with "ten to twenty small files tend to benefit more" from HTTP/2 multiplexing than single-large-asset pages [4] — which is exactly the SvelteKit `adapter-node`/`sirv` immutable-chunk pattern the gateway plan documents (`x-sveltekit-page`, `cache-control: public,max-age=31536000,immutable`, `docs/plans/gateway-local-routing.md:22-23`). A host-side cache of those immutable, content-hashed assets (so requests never reach the VM at all) removes the remaining per-request VM round-trip entirely — this is explicitly listed as a "secondary win" in the plan, independent of the CDN question (`docs/plans/gateway-local-routing.md:113`).

**What it does NOT buy:** none of this touches geographic distance. The speed-of-light floor through fiber is ~200,000 km/s [6], and empirical inter-region pings bear that out: intra-city ~10–40ms, intra-continent ~40–160ms, but Frankfurt-to-Asia jumps to ~394–788ms depending on destination and path [6][7]. Mjolnir's single origin sits in one datacenter (`45.76.77.97`); a user on the opposite side of the planet pays that RTT on every connection regardless of how efficiently the origin multiplexes requests within that one round trip. HTTP/2's own caveat reinforces this: on lossy/high-latency links (the email-client image-loading use case this research targets), "the loss of a single packet would halt reception on all [multiplexed] streams," and "measurements in cellular networks show HTTP/2 does not have a significant performance advantage over HTTP/1.1" [4] — so HTTP/2 is not a strict universal win even on the protocol axis, let alone the distance axis. HTTP/3 (QUIC, no head-of-line blocking across streams) mitigates the packet-loss caveat but still doesn't shorten the wire.

**Verdict on this piece:** HTTP/2/3 + host-side cache fixes connection/multiplexing/origin-compute latency — likely the dominant cost of the *current* 7s cold-start bug per the gateway plan's own root-cause analysis (`docs/plans/gateway-local-routing.md:5-30`, which attributes most of the pain to Iroh connection setup, not geography). It does not fix geographic RTT for genuinely distant readers (e.g. email-client image pixels loaded from Asia/Oceania against a US/EU-region origin).

### 2. Self-hosted multi-PoP cache (Varnish/nginx + GeoDNS or anycast)

**GeoDNS path (DNS-based, no BGP/ASN needed):**
- PowerDNS supports GeoIP-based and Lua-scripted latency/weighted routing natively, self-hostable [2].
- Cheap at small scale: AWS Route53 geo/latency-based routing runs roughly "$0.70 per million queries (GeoDNS) / $0.60 per million queries (latency-based)," reported real-world cost "around $1/month" for modest traffic [2]. ClouDNS and easyDNS offer similar managed GeoDNS products [2].
- Caveat: GeoDNS routes the *DNS answer*, not the packet — it has the well-known resolver-locality problem (public resolvers don't always reflect true client location) and TTL-bound failover (a dead PoP keeps getting handed out until TTL expires) [2]. Materially weaker failover than anycast.

**BGP anycast path (real anycast, no DNS games):** this is the part that breaks "free."
- DIY-cheapest documented route: Vultr offers free BGP sessions and lets you announce your own prefix (or use theirs) at ~$5/month per PoP across 15+ locations on 4 continents — "BGP session fees included" [1]. A sponsored-ASN path (vs. becoming a full RIPE/ARIN LIR) is the commonly recommended cheap on-ramp — providers like iFog, Route64, Openfactory bundle a sponsored ASN with a delegated IPv6 block, "much cheaper and way less hassle" than full LIR membership [1].
- Fully turnkey: BuyVM sells an IPv4-anycast VPS package (Luxembourg + Las Vegas + New Jersey) starting at **$60/year**, anycast free for all BuyVM customers across their locations [1]. Closest thing to "anycast without doing BGP yourself" at consumer pricing — but only 3 PoPs, US/EU-weighted, nothing in Asia-Pacific.
- For a realistic 3–5 node spread: figure $5–$20/mo per VPS (Hetzner CPX22-class ≈ $9.49/mo with 20TB EU egress included, but US/Asia Hetzner regions capped at 0.5–1TB with steep overage — Singapore €7.40/TB [9]; Vultr ≈ $20/mo for 3TB at comparable tier, 32+ global locations [9]) plus BGP session fees (~$5/mo/PoP on Vultr [1]) plus a sponsored ASN (cost not consistently published, but a recurring line item and an extra entity to manage) [1]. **Rough all-in estimate for a real 5-PoP BGP-anycast layer: $75–$150/month in raw infra/session fees alone**, before engineering time.

**Varnish/nginx caching capability:** Varnish has **no native TLS support** — requires a separate terminator (Hitch, HAProxy, or nginx) at every node [5]. Hitch is documented to scale to "15,000 listening sockets and 500,000 certificates" [5], so software is not the constraint — the constraint is *operational*: per-node Let's Encrypt issuance/renewal (or syncing a wildcard cert+key securely to every node), per-node config drift, and cross-node cache-invalidation consistency when content changes. No off-the-shelf multi-region cert-sync or cross-node purge tool for bare Varnish/Hitch surfaced in research — this would be custom tooling Mjolnir would have to write and maintain, overlapping with (but not provided by) the existing Forge host-reconciler work.

### 3. Honest break points vs. a managed CDN

- **DDoS absorption is the sharpest break point.** Cloudflare's claimed network capacity is in the hundreds-of-Tbps range ("over 310 Tbps... across 300+ cities" per one source [3]), with L3/L4 DDoS mitigation free and unmetered at every plan tier [3]. A self-hosted single origin — or even a 5-node self-hosted anycast layer — "cannot realistically absorb large-scale volumetric attacks"; per-server tools like HAProxy can "absorb and rate-limit a surprising amount of abuse" but are fundamentally bounded by that server's own NIC/bandwidth capacity [3]. 5 small VPS nodes don't change the order of magnitude — now there are 5 origins to harden instead of 1.
- **PoP breadth.** BuyVM-class managed anycast gets 3 nodes; a determined DIY build on Vultr-class pricing might stretch to 10-15 nodes for real money. Cloudflare/Bunny operate across "300+ cities" [3]. 5 self-hosted nodes covers obvious big regions (NA-east, NA-west, EU, maybe APAC) but leaves large population centers (South America, Africa, South/SE Asia outside one node) on long RTT to the *nearest self-hosted PoP*, not zero.
- **Cost-per-GB crossover.** Bunny's pull-zone CDN runs as low as **$0.01/GB (Standard Network, EU/NA)** down to **$0.005/GB (Volume Network, global flat rate)**, $1/month minimum, no contract [8]. Hetzner EU egress is bundled free up to 20TB/mo, but that's EU-only — adding US or APAC nodes for genuine multi-region coverage hits capped 0.5–1TB egress with expensive overage [9]. Once cert sync, cross-node purge, BGP/anycast monitoring, and health-check failover (engineering time) are counted against a managed CDN's checkbox equivalent, the self-hosted multi-PoP layer is plausibly **more expensive in total cost** than a Bunny-class pull zone for a small/medium static-asset workload, even though the raw per-GB number looks competitive on paper.
- **Where self-host still wins outright:** sovereignty over DNS/registrar/origin certs (a stated team value), no vendor lock-in to a middleman that can rate-limit/deplatform/repriced, full control of cache-purge semantics tied directly into Mjolnir's own deploy/registry system. For genuinely low-traffic, not-under-attack workloads, a single tuned origin plus HTTP/2/3 may simply be good enough.

### 4. Hybrid recommendation

The evidence supports exactly the layering the gateway plan already proposes (`docs/plans/gateway-local-routing.md:109-114`): **"Enable HTTP/2 at the gateway," "host-side cache of immutable assets," then "real CDN last — origin is fast by then, so it's pure geo/latency win."**

- **Do now, unconditionally, regardless of CDN decision:** HTTP/2 (ideally HTTP/3/QUIC) at `mjolnir-gateway`, plus a host-side cache for `_app/immutable/*` and other content-hashed, long-`max-age` assets so they never reach the VM. Free, removes the measured multiplexing bottleneck, de-risks the cold-start bug the plan was written to fix.
- **Skip building real BGP anycast / a DIY multi-PoP fleet** unless and until (a) DDoS becomes an active, demonstrated threat, or (b) traffic volume and global user-distribution data justify the standing ops cost of cert sync + cross-node purge + BGP session management across 3-5+ nodes.
- **Add a pull-zone CDN (Bunny-class) for geo-latency + DDoS once the origin is otherwise fast,** specifically in front of static/immutable assets and email-client image loads. Keep DNS, registrar, and origin Let's Encrypt certs under Mjolnir's own control (pull-zone CDNs don't require surrendering DNS ownership the way Cloudflare's proxy mode does) — preserves the self-hosting/sovereignty value while buying PoP breadth and DDoS absorption no realistic 5-node self-host can match.
- **Pure self-host wins when:** traffic is small, audience is regionally concentrated near the origin, DDoS risk is low, and the team treats multi-PoP caching as a genuine ongoing ops project, not a one-time setup.
- **Managed CDN earns its keep when:** the audience is genuinely global (the "email-client image loading" framing strongly suggests this — recipients open emails everywhere), DDoS is a live concern, or engineer-hours to build/maintain DIY anycast exceed a few dollars/month of Bunny-class billing — which, per the pricing above, it almost certainly does.
